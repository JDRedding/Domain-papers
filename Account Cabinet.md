## Claims

1. Box with downward-forward inclined back; flexible hangers removably suspended in the box; trays hung from the hangers by flexible supports; each tray horizontal with its forward end ahead of the one above; rear ends resting on the inclined back.

2. Same, without requiring the trays to be strictly horizontal.

3. Box, removable flexible hangers, and trays hung by flexible supports.

4–5. Inclined-back box, removable flexible hangers, trays attached to the hangers (with or without separate flexible supports), each tray advanced relative to the one above, rear ends on the inclined back.

6. Casing, superposed trays, each with individual collapsible supports, and means in the casing to which those supports attach.

7. Trays on flexible hangers/supports with inverted-V forward extensions, apex of one in the opening of the extension above.

8. Same, with forward and rear extensions inclined so as to overlap the tray above.

9. Sides and ends of each tray inclined outward so as to overlap the tray above.

10–11. Upward-outward / inverted-V forward extensions as above.

## Field and Object

The invention is a cabinet containing a series of trays for filing bills, such as the current or running accounts of a retail grocer’s customers.

General objects:

- Shape the trays so loose bills cannot slide or be blown out.  
- Support the trays so any tray can be lifted for free insertion and removal of bills from the tray below.  
- Arrange, connect, and sustain the series so the trays can be taken out of the cabinet together and collapsed/nested for overnight storage in a vault or fireproof safe.

## Construction

### Cabinet box

- Box is deeper at bottom **2** than at top , so sides incline downward and forward.  
- False back, preferably sheet metal, has the same inclination as the sides.  
- A removable **bridge-board** sits in the upper part of the box, held by ends entered from the front into horizontal grooves.  
- With two or more tray series side by side, a removable bar marks the division in the face of the frame.

### Hangers and supports

- Tray-hangers are canvas, chain, or other flexible, contractible, or compressible material, attached above to the bridge-board.  
- Shorter supports of similar material attach above to the hangers and below to the sides of trays.  
- Trays hang approximately horizontal, each slightly forward of the one above.  
- Rear ends of the trays normally rest against the inclined false back.

### Tray shape

Trays are preferably foil or sheet metal.

- Rear end is bent to incline upward and forward.  
- Forward end is bent upward and then downward in an **inverted-V** form.  
- Side extensions are bent upward and outward, limited to the mid-portion of the sides, and serve as a rest for index-card.  
- Lip is further curved and extended forward near the median line of the tray.  
- Turned flanges on the face of the inverted-V extension, with tongue, hold the index-card.  
- The apex of one tray’s forward extension sits approximately in or near the opening of the inverted-V of the tray above. Raising a tray nests its V into the tray above.  
- Forward ends of bills extend beyond the short part of the tray end so they can be grasped; that exposed strip is visible because each tray sits ahead of the one above.

### Collapsing for the safe

Remove the bridge-board and its hanging trays from the box. Flexible hangers/supports plus the flared tray form let the series nest one in another for compact vault storage.

A series of trays on individual collapsible supports can be used without the full hanger system when the series need not be collapsed as a whole. Inclination of the box and freedom of each tray to oscillate on its supports are conveniences for reading bills and cards, not essential to the nesting idea.
