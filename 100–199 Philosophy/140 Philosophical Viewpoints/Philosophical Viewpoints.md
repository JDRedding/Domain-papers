A structured overview of major schools and related systems, with historical context, branches of philosophy, and notable figures.

## Idealism and Related Systems

**Core claim.** Reality is fundamentally mental, spiritual, or dependent on perception.

**Key subtypes**

- **Berkeleyan idealism.** “To be is to be perceived” (George Berkeley).
- **Hegelian absolute idealism.** Reality unfolds as the dialectical progress of Spirit (Hegel).

**Branch focus.** Metaphysics; epistemology (knowledge as mind-dependent).

**Influence.** Critical philosophy, German idealism, and later phenomenology.

## Critical Philosophy

**Founder.** Immanuel Kant.

**Core claim.** Human knowledge arises from the interaction of sensory experience and the mind’s structuring faculties (space, time, causation).

**Branch focus.** Epistemology, metaphysics, ethics.

**Impact.** A foundation of modern German philosophy; a middle path between rationalism and empiricism.

## Bergsonism and Intuitionism

**Founder.** Henri Bergson.

**Core claim.** True knowledge comes from duration and intuition rather than analytic reasoning. Emphasis on creative evolution.

**Branch focus.** Metaphysics; philosophy of mind.

**Related viewpoint.** Moral intuitionism (G. E. Moore, W. D. Ross) holds that moral truths are grasped directly.

## Humanism and Related Systems

**Core claim.** Emphasis on human dignity, rationality, ethical self-determination, and cultural achievement.

**Influence.** Renaissance humanism (Erasmus); modern secular ethics.

**Branch focus.** Ethics, political philosophy, aesthetics.

## Sensationalism

**Core claim.** Knowledge arises from sensory experience; closely tied to empiricism.

**Key figures.** John Locke, David Hume, Étienne Bonnot de Condillac.

**Branch focus.** Epistemology; a basis for empiricist science.

## Naturalism and Related Systems

**Core claim.** Philosophical inquiry should align with natural law and empirical science. Reality is continuous with nature.

**Key figures.** Aristotle (in a teleological sense), W. V. O. Quine, Wilfrid Sellars.

**Branch focus.** Metaphysics, ethics, philosophy of science.

**Variants.** Ethical naturalism; scientific naturalism.

## Pantheism and Related Systems

**Core claim.** God and the cosmos are identical; divinity is immanent in nature.

**Key figures.** Baruch Spinoza; some forms of Stoicism.

**Branch focus.** Metaphysics; philosophy of religion.

**Related idea.** Panentheism, in which God interpenetrates the universe but also transcends it.

## Eclecticism, Liberalism, and Traditionalism

- **Eclecticism.** Combines insights from several schools in a pragmatic way.
- **Philosophical liberalism.** Defends individual freedom, political pluralism, and tolerance (John Locke; Kant in political thought).
- **Traditionalism.** Stresses continuity with historical or cultural authority (Scholasticism; Confucian orthodox lines).

**Branch focus.** Ethics, political philosophy, education.

## Other Philosophical Systems

- **Existentialism.** Freedom, choice, and human meaning (Kierkegaard, Sartre).
- **Phenomenology.** Consciousness and experience as they appear (Husserl, Merleau-Ponty).
- **Analytic philosophy.** Logic, language, and clarity rather than speculative metaphysics (Frege, Russell, Wittgenstein).
- **Postmodernism and critical theory.** Question universal truths and examine power in systems of knowledge (Foucault, Derrida).
- **Ethical systems.** Utilitarianism (Bentham, Mill); deontology (Kant); virtue ethics (Aristotle); care ethics (Gilligan, Noddings).

## Cross-Cutting Observations

Schools often cut across branches: metaphysics, epistemology, ethics, logic, aesthetics, and politics.

Many later debates grow out of earlier tensions:

- Rationalism versus empiricism, leading toward critical philosophy and pragmatism
- Idealism versus materialism, shaping political thought and phenomenology
- Experience versus reason, still central to epistemology and ethics
