# Sigma 2‑swap Configuration Graph $G(P)$
## Configuration Graph $G(P)$, 2‑swap

### Alphabet, words, and Parikh vector

Let $\Sigma=[\sigma]=\{1,2,\dots,\sigma\}$ be a finite ordered alphabet, and let $w\in\Sigma^n$ be a word of length $n$. The **Parikh vector** of $w$ is  

$$
P(w)=(P(w)[1],\dots,P(w)[\sigma]),\qquad
P(w)[i]=|\{j\in[n]:w[j]=i\}|.
$$

Fix a vector $P\in\mathbb{N}_0^\sigma$ with 

$$
n=\sum_{i=1}^\sigma P[i].
$$

The set of all words realizing $P$ is  

$$
\Sigma^*|_P=\{w\in\Sigma^n:P(w)=P\}.
$$

Its cardinality is the multinomial coefficient  

$$
|\Sigma^*|_P|=\frac{n!}{\prod_{i=1}^\sigma P[i]!}.
$$

### 2‑swap operation

A **2‑swap** (transposition of positions) is an ordered pair $s=(i,j)$ with $1\le i<j\le n$ and $w[i]\ne w[j]$. Its action on a word is  

$$
w\circ(i,j)
=w[1..i-1]\;w[j]\;w[i+1..j-1]\;w[i]\;w[j+1..n].
$$

All other symbols remain unchanged, so the Parikh vector is preserved.

### Graph definition

The **configuration graph** is the undirected simple graph 

$$
G(P)=(V(P),E(P)),
$$

where  

$$
V(P)=\{v_w:w\in\Sigma^*|_P\}
$$

and  

$$
E(P) =\{\{v_w,v_u\}:\exists\,i,j\in[n]\text{ with }w\circ(i,j)=u\}.
$$

Two vertices are adjacent exactly when the corresponding words differ by a single 2‑swap.

### Structural properties

**Local isomorphism.**  
For every radius $r\ge0$ and every pair of vertices $u,w\in V(P)$, the induced subgraphs on the closed balls of radius $r$ are isomorphic.

**Diameter.**  

$$
{diam}(G(P))=n-\max_{i\in[\sigma]}P[i].
$$

This bound is tight: it is achieved by moving each occurrence of a minority letter past a contiguous block of the most frequent letter.

**Clique number.**  
$\omega(G(P))$ is determined by $P$. A maximum clique corresponds to a largest set of pairwise compatible 2‑swaps that can be performed independently on the same word. Its exact value depends on how many distinct symbols occupy mutually swappable position pairs.

**Induced subgraphs.**  
If a set of positions is frozen, the induced subgraph on the words agreeing on those positions is itself a configuration graph $G(P')$ for a strictly smaller Parikh vector $P'$. This yields a recursive decomposition.

### Hamiltonicity

For every vertex $v\in V(P)$ there exists a Hamiltonian path in $G(P)$ beginning at $v$:  

$$
\forall v\in V(P)\;
\exists\text{ a path }v=v_0-v_1-\dots-v_{|V|-1}
$$

that visits every vertex exactly once.

When all symbols are distinct, so $P=(1,1,\dots,1)$, this recovers the classical fact that the Cayley graph of $S_n$ generated by all transpositions is Hamiltonian.

### Enumeration algorithm

**Input.**  
A word $v\in\Sigma^*|_P$ of length $n$.

**Output.**  
The successive 2‑swaps of a Hamiltonian path beginning at $v$.

**Complexity.** 

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{preprocessing} &= O(n\log n),\\
\text{delay between consecutive edges} &= O(\sigma\log n)
\end{align*}
$$

($O(\log n)$ when $\sigma=2$). The algorithm never materializes the exponential vertex set; it maintains a constant number of position lists per symbol and walks through a Gray‑code‑like order of suffixes.

**High‑level procedure**  

$$
\begin{align*}
&\textbf{Preprocess}(v) && O(n\log n)\\
&\textbf{Initialize path construction}\\
&\textbf{while not all vertices visited}\\
&\qquad\text{emit next 2‑swap }(i,j)\\
&\qquad\text{advance in }O(\sigma\log n)\text{ time}\\
&\textbf{end}
\end{align*}
$$
