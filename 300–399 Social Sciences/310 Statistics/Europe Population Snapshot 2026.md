# Europe Population Snapshot 2026

As of 2026, Europe’s population is about **743.5 million**, slightly lower than in recent years. The continental median age is **43.1 years**, and the population continues to age.

In 2026 Europe is marked by **slow population decline**, **ageing**, **low fertility**, **high urbanization**, and—especially in the EU—**greater diversity through migration**. Eastern Europe is the largest subregion. Fertility, density, and age structure vary widely by country. These patterns matter for social policy, labour markets, and health systems.

## Trends and Historical Context

*Source: [Worldometer](https://www.worldometers.info/world-population/europe-population/)*

Europe grew slowly through the late 20th century and peaked near **749.5 million around 2020**. Since then the total has edged down because of negative natural change and ageing.

- Yearly change in 2026: **−0.12%**
- Net change: about **−916,471** people

## Age Structure and Median Age

**Continent (Worldometer, 2026):** median age **43.1 years**, above the global average.

**European Union (Eurostat):** figures refer to the EU-27, not all of Europe.

- Median age: **44.9 years** on 1 January 2025 ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained?title=Population_structure_and_ageing); [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/))
- Under 15: about **14%** of the EU population
- Age 65+: **22%** of the EU population
- Age 80+: about **6%**

Oldest EU medians include **Italy (49.1)**, Bulgaria, Portugal, and Greece. Youngest include **Ireland**, Luxembourg, and Malta.

## Gender Distribution (EU)

*Source: European Commission / Eurostat*

In the EU, women outnumber men by about **4.2%** (roughly 230 million women and 221 million men). The gap is largest in **Latvia** (about 15.6% more women than men).

## Fertility and Births

- Continental TFR (Worldometer, 2026): **1.41** children per woman, well below the replacement level of **2.1**.
- EU TFR was **1.34** in 2024 ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/interactive-publications/demography-2026)).

Country examples cited in the source draft (treat as EU/national figures, not a single year for all of Europe):

- Higher fertility: Bulgaria, France, Hungary  
- Lower fertility: Malta, Spain, Lithuania  

EU crude birth rates are about **7.9–8.2** live births per 1,000 people. In many countries deaths exceed births, which drives decline where net migration does not offset the gap.

Life expectancy has risen over decades, with a temporary setback during COVID-19.

## Migration and Diversity (EU)

*Source: European Commission / Eurostat (2024 flows)*

- Immigration to the EU (2024): about **6 million**, of whom **74%** came from outside the EU  
- Emigration from the EU (2024): about **3 million**  
- In 2024, **24%** of children were born to foreign-born mothers  
- About **1.2 million** people acquired EU citizenship in 2024; large groups included Syrians, Moroccans, and Albanians
