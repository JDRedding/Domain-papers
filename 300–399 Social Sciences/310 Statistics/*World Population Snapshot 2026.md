# World Population Snapshot (2026)

As of September 2026, the world population is about **8.3 billion**. Growth rates and age structures differ sharply by region ([worldpopulationclock.net](https://worldpopulationclock.net/)).

| Item | 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| World population | **~8.3 billion** |
| Annual growth | **~0.825%** |
| Largest countries | India, China, United States, Indonesia, Pakistan, Nigeria |
| Main trends | Slower global growth; ageing; Africa as the main engine of future increase; Asia’s growth easing |

The picture is uneven: some regions still add people quickly, others are already shrinking. That split will drive planning for labor, pensions, cities, and resources.

## Total Population and Growth

Over the past century the total rose from about **1.6 billion in 1900** to **8.3 billion in 2026**.

| Milestone | Year |

Current annual growth is about **0.825%**—slower than in earlier decades as fertility falls and populations age.

## Demographic Indicators

- **Fertility** is falling worldwide and is near or below replacement in much of Asia and Europe.
- **Median age** is rising; the global figure was near **31 years in 2025**.
- **Density** ranges from more than **1,200 people/km²** in Bangladesh to under **10/km²** in Russia.
- **Births:** about **470,000** live births per day in 2026 in the source draft—still a large natural increase, but slower than at mid-century.

## Historical Context

The fastest growth was **1950–1980**, when the world rate stayed above **2%**, helped by better health care, sanitation, and food. After 1980, fertility decline stretched the time between each new billion. Going from **8 billion to 9 billion** is expected to take about **15 years**. Ageing, lower fertility, and regional divergence now shape the outlook more than raw increase.
