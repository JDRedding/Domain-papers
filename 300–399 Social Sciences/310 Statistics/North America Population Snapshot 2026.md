# North America Population Snapshot (2026)

North America’s people are concentrated in the **United States, Mexico, and Canada**. Growth is moderate. Northern America is highly urban and somewhat older (median age 38.9). Density and age structure vary sharply from the Caribbean to Canada and Greenland. Smaller countries and territories add little to the headcount but a lot to regional diversity. Urban concentration in major hubs is likely to continue.

As of 2026, **North America** has about **621 million** people. The United States, Mexico, and Canada hold most of the total. Continental growth is moderate, around **0.63–0.64%** a year.

> **Note on geography.** *North America* (the continent) includes Northern America, Central America, and the Caribbean. *Northern America* is the UN subregion: mainly the United States, Canada, Bermuda, Greenland, and Saint Pierre and Miquelon. Figures below mix both; labels say which is which.

## Historical and Projected Trends

- Growth has slowed from above **2%** in the 1950s to about **0.54–0.64%** recently.
- Northern America is already highly urban; the urban share is projected at **90.6% by 2050**.
- Northern America projections ([Worldometer](https://www.worldometers.info/world-population/northern-america-population/)):
  - **2030:** 397,429,677  
  - **2050:** 426,579,885  
- Net migration is a large part of Northern America’s growth (~1.47 million in 2026).
