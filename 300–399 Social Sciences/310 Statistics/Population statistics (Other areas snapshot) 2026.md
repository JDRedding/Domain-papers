# Population statistics (Other areas) 

Asia, Europe, North America, and South America are excluded. What remains is **Africa**, **Oceania**, and **Antarctica**.

## Regional estimates

### 1. Africa
- **Population:** ~**1.59 billion**
- **Growth rate:** ~**2.5%** per year
- **Largest countries:** Nigeria (~270 million), Ethiopia (~135 million), Egypt (~120 million)

As of September 2026, Africa’s population is about **1.585 billion**, or **19.1%** of the world total. The median age is **19.5 years**. Strong growth is still projected through 2050 and later. Africa’s growth is both an opening and a strain. A young workforce can support development if education and jobs keep up. If they do not, infrastructure, health systems, and schools will be under pressure.

Growth has eased from peaks above **3%** in the 1980s. **Nigeria** is the most populous country (~**233 million** in 2024 in the source draft) and a large share of continental increase.

By **2050**, Africa is expected to hold about **one-quarter** of world population, with urbanization around **60.4%**.

Longer term, Eastern, Middle, and Western Africa are projected to grow more than **fivefold by 2100**. Middle Africa could rise from under **100 million in 2000** to nearly **800 million in 2100**, led in large part by the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

#### Demographic Characteristics

- **Youth bulge:** a large share under age 15 and high dependency ratios.
- **Life expectancy:** from under **40 years** in the 1950s to over **60** by 2010–2015.
- **Health:** still-high infant and maternal mortality; malaria and HIV/AIDS remain major burdens; non-communicable diseases are rising.

#### Socioeconomic Context

- **HDI** is highest in much of North Africa and parts of Southern Africa, and lowest in parts of Central Africa and the Sahel.
- **Urbanization** is rising as people move from rural areas to cities.
- **Languages and peoples:** Bantu languages dominate much of southern, central, and southeastern Africa; Niger-Congo, Nilotic, and Afroasiatic families are also widespread. Indigenous groups include the San and various Central African forest peoples often labeled “Pygmy.”

### 2. Oceania
- **Population:** ~**46 million**
- **Largest:** Australia (~30 million), Papua New Guinea (~11 million), New Zealand (~5 million)

As of September 2026, Oceania has about **47.12 million** people, or **0.57%** of the world total. Most live in **Australia and New Zealand**.

Oceania is the least populous inhabited continent and is growing slowly. More than **two-thirds** of residents live in Australia and New Zealand; about **two-thirds** live in cities. Growth is easing, but the total is still headed toward nearly **58 million by 2050**. The region mixes sparse mainland settlement with small, dense island states.

### 3. Antarctica
- **Permanent population:** **0**
- **Seasonal researchers and staff:** about **4,000–5,000** (not counted in the total)

Antarctica is a scientific workplace, not a residential society. Headcount follows the research calendar and Treaty cooperation.

- The population is **seasonal, temporary, and research-driven**.
- There is **no indigenous population**.
- Winter ~**1,000–1,100**; summer ~**4,400**, plus ship crews.
- Density is extremely low; activity is spread across dozens of stations (the draft cites **37**).

Antarctica has **no indigenous population**. Almost everyone on the continent is a scientist or support worker. Numbers swing with the season: about **1,000–1,100 in winter** and about **4,400 in summer**.

#### Seasonal Variation

Ice and extreme cold keep the population small and temporary.

| Period | Approximate personnel |
| --- | ---: |
| Winter | **~1,100** on land |
| Summer (October–February) | **~4,400** on land |
| Summer, research vessels | **~1,000** more in nearby waters |

Counts include year-round stations and short-term field camps ([World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/continents/antarctica)).

#### Residency and Demographics

There are **no permanent indigenous inhabitants**. Stays are for science and logistics under the **Antarctic Treaty**, so the workforce is international.

- **McMurdo Station (United States):** about **250** in winter and up to **1,000** in summer—the largest station.
- Smaller bases can be tiny; **Troll (Norway)** may hold as few as **six** people.

#### Area and Density

- **Area:** about **14.2 million km²** (5th-largest continent).
- **Density:** roughly **0.00007–0.0035 people/km²**.
- Most of the land sits under ice more than **1 mile** thick, which limits where people can live.

#### Births and Civilian Settlements

- First person born on the Antarctic mainland: **Emilio Marcos Palma** (Argentina), **1978**, at **Base Esperanza**.
- Small civilian communities tied to research include **Villa Las Estrellas** (Chile) and **Fortín Sargento Cabral** (Argentina).

## Total

$$
1.59 + 0.046 \approx 1.636 \text{ billion}
$$

**About 1.64 billion people** live outside Asia, Europe, and the Americas.

Africa is almost the entire total (~97%). Oceania is about 3%. Antarctica adds no permanent residents.

**Final estimate:** approximately **1.64 billion**.
