# Sociology
- 300 Sociology

**Sociology** is the systematic study of human society, social relationships, and institutions. It explores how people interact, how social structures take shape, and how societies change over time.

Sociology informs policy-making, education, healthcare, business, public health, and social work. It helps identify social problems, guide interventions, and promote social justice. Sociologists work as researchers, educators, and analysts across many sectors.

The field is essential for understanding human behavior in social context, recognizing patterns in collective life, and supporting more informed decisions about social improvement.

## Definition and Scope

Sociology examines the structure and dynamics of societies. It investigates patterns of social behavior, cultural norms, institutions, and social processes. Sociologists study how factors such as race, gender, class, religion, and politics shape both individual experience and collective life. A central aim of the field is to understand the interplay between personal actions and larger social forces.

## Key Areas of Study

- **Social institutions.** Organizations and systems such as family, education, religion, the economy, and government, and their influence on social life.
- **Social inequality.** Disparities in wealth, power, and status, and how social stratification affects access to resources and opportunities.
- **Culture and norms.** Shared beliefs, values, language, customs, and symbols that shape behavior and identity.
- **Deviance and social control.** Behavior that departs from societal norms, and the mechanisms that regulate conduct.
- **Social change.** How societies evolve through technological, economic, and political transformation, as well as through collective action.

Sociology uses both **qualitative methods** (such as interviews and ethnography) and **quantitative methods** (such as surveys and statistical analysis) to study social phenomena.

## Theoretical Perspectives

Sociological thought is grounded in several major frameworks:

- **Functionalism** views society as an interconnected system in which institutions contribute to stability and cohesion.
- **Conflict theory** focuses on power relations and social inequality, often highlighting struggles between classes or groups.
- **Symbolic interactionism** examines everyday interactions and the meanings people attach to social symbols and experiences.
- **Feminist theory** analyzes gender inequalities and challenges traditional patriarchal structures.
