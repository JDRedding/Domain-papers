# Anthropology
- 300 Sociology
  
**Anthropology** is the scientific study of humans, past and present. It examines cultural, social, biological, and linguistic dimensions of human life in order to understand diversity, adaptation, and evolution. In short, anthropology bridges ancient and contemporary human life. It connects biology, culture, language, and society in order to illuminate the complexity and diversity of being human.

## Definition and Scope

Anthropology seeks a holistic account of humanity. It studies human origins, behavior, societies, environments, and languages, drawing together biological, cultural, linguistic, and archaeological perspectives. The aim is a comprehensive understanding of what it means to be human across time and place.

Anthropology helps explain human diversity, challenge cultural assumptions, and address practical problems in global health, migration, development, conservation, and conflict resolution. By integrating historical, social, and biological perspectives, it sheds light on resilience, adaptation, and social complexity. It also fosters cross-cultural understanding and informs policies that affect communities worldwide.

## Main Branches

- **Cultural anthropology** studies the cultural life of human societies, including customs, traditions, social norms, religion, and everyday practice. Researchers often use participant observation and ethnography to understand social structures and behavior in context.
- **Biological (physical) anthropology** focuses on human evolution, genetics, primatology, and forensic anthropology. It examines how humans evolved, how populations vary, and how biology shapes behavior.
- **Archaeology** investigates past societies through the excavation and analysis of material remains—artifacts, structures, and landscapes—in order to reconstruct lifeways and cultural change over time.
- **Linguistic anthropology** examines language and communication and their role in shaping culture. It studies how language influences identity, social interaction, and cultural transmission.
- **Applied anthropology** uses anthropological knowledge to address practical problems in areas such as public health, environmental policy, humanitarian work, and business, connecting research to social needs.

## Methods

Anthropologists combine qualitative and quantitative approaches, including:

- Ethnography, interviews, and participant observation in cultural research
- Field surveys, laboratory analysis, and comparative study in biological and archaeological research
- Analysis of language, discourse, and communication patterns in linguistic anthropology
