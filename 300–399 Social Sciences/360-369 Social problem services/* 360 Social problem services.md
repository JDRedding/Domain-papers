**Social problems** are conditions or behaviors that harm individuals or communities, disrupt social norms, and reduce overall well-being. Understanding social problems requires attention both to the structures that produce them and to the behaviors that sustain them. Lasting responses depend on cooperation among governments, organizations, communities, and individuals.

Complex issues such as housing instability, healthcare access, mental health, economic security, and community well-being usually need coordinated action across federal, state, local, and international institutions. The overview below lists major service types and providers.

## Definition and Characteristics

A social problem is an issue that affects a substantial number of people and is widely recognized as harmful or undesirable. Public judgment typically treats it as something that calls for intervention, policy, or broader social change. Common features include:

- **Collective impact.** The harm extends beyond a single person.
- **Public recognition.** Society regards the condition as damaging or unacceptable.
- **Need for coordinated response.** Government, organizations, and communities usually have to act together.

## Types of Social Problems

- **Economic hardship and poverty.** Unemployment, low income, and lack of basic resources can limit education, health, and social mobility.
- **Crime and juvenile delinquency.** Violence, theft, illicit drug markets, and gang activity threaten safety and social cohesion.
- **Health and mental health.** Substance use disorders, suicide, and chronic mental illness place strain on families and health systems.
- **Discrimination and inequality.** Racism, sexism, ageism, and other forms of exclusion undermine justice and restrict opportunity.
- **Family and institutional breakdown.** Domestic abuse, child neglect, divorce, and weakened social institutions can produce emotional and economic instability.
- **Environmental and urban problems.** Pollution, overcrowding, homelessness, and limited access to clean water or safe housing affect both physical and social well-being.

## Causes

Social problems usually have more than one source:

- **Structural inequality.** Uneven economic, political, and social arrangements
- **Cultural and behavioral factors.** Harmful norms or practices
- **Rapid social change.** Urbanization, technological shifts, or migration that unsettle established patterns
- **Policy gaps.** Weak laws or inadequate public response

## Effects

Consequences can include:

- Greater poverty and inequality
- Worse public health and quality of life
- Social instability and mistrust
- Higher costs from crime, healthcare, and social welfare

## Addressing Social Problems

Typical strategies include:

- **Education and public awareness.** Community programs that inform and equip people
- **Policy and legal reform.** Laws and enforcement aimed at reducing inequality and crime
- **Social services and healthcare.** Mental health care, substance-use treatment, and support for basic needs
- **Community engagement.** Volunteer work, local organizing, and efforts to strengthen social ties
- **Economic development.** Jobs, support for small enterprise, and measures that reduce income inequality

## Housing and Homelessness Services

### Federal programs

- **U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD).** Affordable housing, rental assistance, supportive services for people experiencing homelessness, and housing vouchers.
- **Housing and Services Resource Center (HSRC; ACL/HHS partnership).** Guidance, training, and cross-sector tools for homelessness prevention, aging, disability support, behavioral health, and housing transitions.

### National nonprofits

- **National Alliance to End Homelessness.** Research-informed strategies and federal partnerships to reduce housing insecurity.
- **National Fair Housing Alliance.** Work against housing discrimination, plus education and related resources.

### Local and 211 services

- **211 Helpline (United Way).** Referrals to emergency shelter, housing assistance, and programs that help people remain housed.

## Healthcare and Mental Health

### Federal agencies

- **U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS).** Programs addressing social determinants of health, equity, and prevention.
- **Medicaid** (for example, MO HealthNet in Missouri) **and CHIP.** Coverage for low-income individuals and children.

### Community health networks

Local hospitals and federally qualified health centers (FQHCs) often combine primary care, behavioral health, oral health, and social support. Some systems, such as University Health in Kansas City, integrate clinical care with social services.

### Global health initiatives

Organizations such as **The Global Fund** work against HIV, tuberculosis, and malaria and support health infrastructure in underserved regions.

## Mental Health and Behavioral Services

Community mental health centers offer counseling, crisis intervention, and addiction treatment. Other sources of support include:

- **National Disability Rights Network (NDRN).** Advocacy for mental health access for people with disabilities.
- **Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA).** Federal training, grants, and support for providers.
- Local **211** connections to therapy, hotlines, and peer-support networks.

## Economic Stability and Workforce Support

### Programs

- **Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF).** Cash assistance and employment training.
- **Unemployment insurance and SNAP.** Federal supports for income and food security.
- Employment programs offering career counseling, job placement, and adult education.

### Nonprofits

- **United Way Worldwide.** Financial literacy, job training, and economic empowerment work.
- **First Step Alliance.** Support for financial independence and banking access for justice-involved people.

## Child, Family, and Community Services

### Federal initiatives

- **Head Start and Early Head Start.** Early childhood education and family support.
- Child welfare programs covering foster care, child-support enforcement, and adoption.

### Nonprofits

- **Children’s Rights** and **First Star.** Education, life-skills support, and advocacy for children in foster care or other high-risk situations.
- **Center for Children and Youth Justice.** Youth-focused work in juvenile justice systems.

## Social Justice and Advocacy

### Civil rights and equality

- American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU)
- Southern Poverty Law Center (SPLC)
- National Urban League

### Gender, migration, and disability rights

- **Equality Now.** International advocacy for women’s rights.
- **International Organization for Migration (IOM).** Support for migrants’ rights.
- **National Organization on Disability.** Employment and educational access for people with disabilities.

### Criminal justice and reentry

- **Innocence Project**, **Vera Institute of Justice**, and **Prison Policy Initiative.** Legal support, advocacy, and policy work affecting people involved in the criminal legal system.

## Access Points and Navigators

- **211 National Network.** One call for referrals to housing, healthcare, food, transportation, mental health care, legal aid, and emergency help.
- Local social service offices and community action agencies. Personalized help with benefits and local resources.

## Summary

These services operate at several levels:

| Focus area | Key federal programs | National / global nonprofits | Local access points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Housing | HUD, HSRC | NAEH, NFHA | 211, shelters |
| Healthcare | HHS, Medicaid | The Global Fund | Community health centers |
| Mental health | SAMHSA | NDRN | Local clinics, 211 referrals |
| Economic stability | TANF, SNAP | United Way, First Step Alliance | Workforce centers |
| Child and family | Head Start, child welfare | Children’s Rights, First Star | Family support offices |
| Social justice | — | ACLU, SPLC, Equality Now, IOM | Legal aid and advocacy centers |

Federal infrastructure, nonprofit expertise, and local navigators together widen access, respond to community needs, and support equity, inclusion, and prevention at national and international scales.

For practical help in the United States, people can call **211**, use federal agency websites, or contact United Way and local community centers to find programs and apply for services.
