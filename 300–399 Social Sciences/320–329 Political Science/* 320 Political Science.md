# Political Science
- 320	Political science (General)


Political science is the academic discipline that studies government systems, political behavior, and the theoretical and practical dimensions of politics. Its central aim is to understand how societies organize and exercise power. Political science continues to evolve by integrating insights from economics, sociology, psychology, and data science. As a multidisciplinary field, it helps explain both historical and contemporary political dynamics.

## Overview

Political science examines the structure, function, and dynamics of political institutions, as well as the behavior of individuals and groups within those systems. It seeks to explain how political decisions are made, how power is distributed, and how governance shapes societies. The field combines theoretical frameworks with empirical research to analyze both domestic and international political phenomena.

Understanding political science is useful for:

- Informing public debate and civic engagement
- Shaping government policies and legislation
- Preparing for careers in politics, diplomacy, public administration, and international organizations
- Researching political behavior and societal trends
- Addressing global challenges such as climate change, human rights, and security

**Political Theory**  
Focuses on philosophical questions about politics, justice, rights, and the ideal organization of society. It explores classical and contemporary ideas from thinkers such as Plato, Machiavelli, Locke, Rousseau, Marx, and Rawls.

**Comparative Politics**  
Studies and compares political systems around the world in order to identify patterns, similarities, and differences. Common topics include democratization, authoritarianism, political culture, electoral systems, and policymaking.

**International Relations (IR)**  
Concerns the interactions among states, international organizations, and non-state actors. Key topics include diplomacy, conflict resolution, war, international law, cooperation, and global governance.

**Public Policy and Administration**  
Examines the formulation, implementation, and evaluation of government policies. It considers how programs and regulations affect society, including social services, economic policy, education, and healthcare.

**Political Economy**  
Explores the relationship between politics and economics, studying how policies affect economic outcomes and how economic interests shape political processes. Central concepts include development, globalization, and inequality.

**Research Methods and Political Methodology**  
Studies the techniques used to analyze political phenomena, including qualitative methods (interviews, case studies) and quantitative methods (surveys, statistical modeling, experiments).

### Topics

**Systems of Governments and States**  
Compares how political authority is organized: constitutions, federal and unitary systems, presidential and parliamentary designs, monarchies, republics, and authoritarian regimes. The focus is on how institutions allocate power, constrain rulers, and shape the capacity of the state.

**Relation of the State to Organized Groups**  
Examines how parties, interest groups, unions, churches, corporations, social movements, and other associations interact with government. Topics include lobbying, corporatism, pluralism, civil society, and the ways organized interests influence or are regulated by the state.

**Civil and Political Rights**  
Studies the legal and political protections of individual liberty and participation, such as speech, assembly, religion, due process, voting, and equality before the law. This area also considers how rights are defined, limited, enforced, and contested across different regimes.

**The Political Process**  
Analyzes how political outcomes are produced: agenda-setting, elections, campaigns, public opinion, coalition-building, bargaining, and elite and mass participation. It treats politics as a sequence of contests over office, policy, and legitimacy.

**International Migration and Colonization**  
Addresses the political causes and consequences of population movement and imperial expansion. Topics include citizenship, borders, integration, settler and extractive colonial systems, decolonization, and the lasting effects of empire on states and international order.

**Slavery and Emancipation**  
Investigates slavery as a political and legal institution and the struggles that ended or transformed it. Political science here considers property in persons, racial hierarchy, abolitionist movements, constitutional change, and the afterlives of emancipation in citizenship and inequality.

**International Relations**  
Studies relations among states and other international actors, including war and peace, alliances, trade, international law, and global institutions. It asks how power, interests, norms, and institutions structure conflict and cooperation beyond the nation-state.

**Legislative Process**  
Focuses on how laws are drafted, amended, passed, and overseen. Typical subjects include bicameralism, committees, party discipline, agenda control, vetoes, representation, and the relationship between legislatures, executives, courts, and the public.
