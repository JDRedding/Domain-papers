# Economics
- 330	Economics (General)

Economics is the study of how individuals, businesses, and governments allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants. It aims to explain the production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services. Economics is essential for understanding public policy, business strategy, financial markets, and societal development. It informs decisions on taxation, welfare programs, environmental regulation, trade agreements, and investment planning.

Contemporary work also includes behavioral economics, development economics, and environmental and sustainability economics, which address human behavior, poverty, and the ecological effects of economic activity. Economics supplies the frameworks and tools needed to analyze choices, predict outcomes, and design policies that shape individual and collective well-being.

Economics is traditionally divided into two main branches:

**Microeconomics**  
Focuses on individual agents and markets. It examines how consumers make choices, how firms maximize profits, and how prices and quantities are determined in particular markets. Key topics include supply and demand, elasticity, consumer behavior, production costs, and market structures such as perfect competition, monopoly, and oligopoly.

**Macroeconomics**  
Analyzes the economy as a whole. It studies aggregate phenomena such as gross domestic product (GDP), unemployment, inflation, fiscal and monetary policy, and international trade. Macroeconomists consider how governments, central banks, and global trends affect growth and stability.

## Key Concepts

**Scarcity and Choice**  
Resources such as labor, capital, and raw materials are limited, so societies must decide how to allocate them.

**Opportunity Cost**  
The value of the next-best alternative forgone when a choice is made.

**Supply and Demand**  
The basic forces that determine prices and quantities in markets.

**Market Efficiency**  
The extent to which resources are allocated in ways that maximize social welfare.

**Externalities and Public Goods**  
Cases in which private actions affect others, producing market failures that may justify government intervention.

## Economic Systems

Economies differ according to how resources are allocated and who makes economic decisions:

**Market Economy**  
Decisions are guided by supply, demand, and price signals, with limited government intervention.

**Command Economy**  
The government largely controls production, pricing, and distribution.

**Mixed Economy**  
Combines private enterprise with public oversight, blending market mechanisms and state direction.
