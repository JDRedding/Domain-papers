# Law
- 340 Law

**Law** is a system of rules and principles established by society and enforced through institutions. It regulates conduct, resolves disputes, and seeks to secure justice. **General law** is a category of law that applies broadly, without a set time limit, to all persons within a defined territory. It is enacted and enforced under the authority of the legislature that created it.

Law, then, comprises rules, institutions, and practices designed to regulate behavior, resolve conflict, and advance justice. Its branches are many because social life is many-sided. General law is one of its most important public forms: lasting, territorial, and meant to apply evenly to those within its reach.

## How Law Works in Society

Law provides a framework for settling disputes, deterring wrongful conduct, and sustaining civic order. Courts interpret legal rules; law enforcement seeks compliance. Attorneys, judges, and paralegals help people navigate the system and keep it functioning.

## The Legal System in the United States

The United States follows a common-law tradition derived from English law, marked by judicial precedent and an independent judiciary. Key features include:

- **Federal and state law.** The federal Constitution and statutes apply nationwide, while each state maintains its own legislative, executive, and judicial institutions.
- **Separation of powers.** Legislatures make law, the executive enforces it, and courts interpret and apply it.
- **Due process and rights.** Legal procedures are meant to ensure fairness and protect individual rights under constitutional law.

## Definition and Purpose of Law

Law is a body of rules created and enforced by governments or other recognized institutions. Its core purposes include:

- Maintaining social order by making behavior more predictable and limiting chaos
- Protecting individual rights, such as speech, property, and personal security
- Establishing fairness by providing formal ways to resolve disputes
- Promoting public welfare through rules that guide collective life

## What General Law Means

In legal usage, **general law** (also called a general act or general statute) is unrestricted as to time, applies throughout the territory under the enacting legislature, and applies to all persons in the same class. It typically governs relations between citizens and public authorities rather than private agreements between individuals.

Common examples include:

- Paying taxes
- Following traffic rules
- Obtaining business or professional licenses
- Meeting obligations to state agencies, municipalities, or federal regulators, depending on the jurisdiction

This differs from **private law**, which governs relationships between private parties, such as family, contract, and inheritance matters.

## Key Characteristics of General Law

- **Broad application.** It applies to all persons within the relevant class and jurisdiction, not only to named individuals.
- **Continuing force.** It remains in effect until the legislature repeals or amends it.
- **Limited by higher authority.** It is only as powerful as the legislature that enacted it and cannot override superior law, such as federal law prevailing over conflicting state law.
- **Territorial limits.** A state’s general law does not bind residents of other states merely because they live elsewhere.

## Examples

- Traffic regulations, such as speed limits and seat-belt requirements
- Tax codes, including rates and filing rules
- Licensing requirements for certain occupations

## Distinction from Related Categories

- **Private law** governs disputes and relationships among private parties, such as divorce or inheritance.
- **Conflicts of law** addresses legal problems that cross jurisdictional lines and is not always treated as general law.

In short, general law is a main instrument of public regulation. It supplies consistent rules for people within a jurisdiction and is enforced through legislative authority rather than private agreement.

## Types of Law

Law can be classified in several ways.

### By source

- **Statutory law.** Rules formally enacted by legislatures.
- **Case law / common law.** Law developed through judicial decisions and precedent, especially in the United States and other common-law countries.
- **Administrative law.** Rules issued by government agencies to carry out statutes.
- **Constitutional law.** Principles drawn from a constitution, including the structure of government and fundamental rights.

### By subject matter

- **Criminal law.** Offenses against society, enforced by prosecution and often punished by fines or imprisonment.
- **Civil law.** Disputes between persons or entities, such as contracts, property, and torts, typically remedied by compensation rather than punishment.
- **Family law.** Marriage, divorce, child custody, and related matters.
- **Labor and employment law.** The relationship between employers and employees.
- **Specialized fields.** Environmental law, tax law, international law, and other focused areas.
