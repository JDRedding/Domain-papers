# ⌊π⌋ = ⌈e⌉

This is one place where π and e—constants from totally different mathematical universes—produce the same integer under dual rounding. π sits **just above** 3, while e sits **just below** 3. So they “meet in the middle” at the integer 3 when applying opposite rounding operators.

The statement  

$$
\lfloor \pi \rfloor = \lceil e \rceil
$$

is **true**, and here’s the precise breakdown.

---

## Core evaluation  

- **$\lfloor \pi \rfloor$** — the **floor** of π is the greatest integer ≤ π.  
  Since
  
$$
\pi \approx 3.141592653589793
$$
  
  we have
  
$$
\lfloor \pi \rfloor = 3.
$$

- **$\lceil e \rceil$** — the **ceiling** of e is the smallest integer ≥ e.  

  Since
  
$$
e \approx 2.718281828459045
$$
  
  we have
  
$$
\lceil e \rceil = 3.
$$

Thus:

$$
\lfloor \pi \rfloor = 3 = \lceil e \rceil.
$$
