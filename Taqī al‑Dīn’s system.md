Taqī al‑Dīn’s Istanbul observatory work can be expressed as a coherent mathematical grammar built from sexagesimal angle‑measure, proportional reasoning, cyclic correspondences, and symmetry operations — all grounded in precise instrument readings. The observatory was not just an instrument workshop; it was a **mathematical engine** linking:

- sexagesimal geometry
- decimal trigonometry
- mechanical timekeeping
- proportional cosmology
- cyclic metaphysics

His grammar is a **unified mathematical–cosmological language**.

**Core historical facts:** his sextant, his mechanical astronomical clock, his decimal trigonometry, and his solar measurements.

## Instrumental Geometry: Sextant and Angular Extraction

His framed sextant (*mushabbaha bi’l‑manātiq*) provided direct angular distances between stars.

The sextant’s geometry:

- Two sighting rulers define a chord.
- The attached arc gives $\theta$ directly in degrees/minutes/seconds.
- The measurement is then converted to decimal for trigonometric reduction.

This is the **geometric–numeric pipeline** of his observatory.

Taqī al‑Dīn’s system is a **ratio‑driven, cycle‑indexed mathematical language**: angles measured sexagesimally, converted to decimals for trigonometry; celestial periods mapped to terrestrial and bodily cycles through proportional correspondences; and symmetry used as a balancing constraint across domains. ## Sexagesimal → Decimal Angle Grammar

Taqī al‑Dīn measured angles in degrees–minutes–seconds using his sextant and observational clock.

Sexagesimal angle (standard in Taqī al‑Dīn’s tables, later mixed with decimals):

$$
\theta = d^\circ + m' + s''
$$

Decimal conversion he applied to trigonometric functions:

$$
\theta_{\text{dec}} = d + \frac{m}{60} + \frac{s}{3600}
$$

This conversion is historically attested: he was **the first to apply decimal fractions to trigonometric tables**, replacing Ptolemaic chord‑based methods with sine/cosine/tangent.

Core trigonometric functions used (sine, cosine, tangent, cotangent rather than Ptolemaic chords):

$$
\sin\theta,\quad\cos\theta,\quad\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta},\quad\cot\theta=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}
$$

These functions were used directly in his *zīj* rather than inherited chord tables.

**Trigonometric operators:**

- **Sine** — $\sin\theta$
- **Cosine** — $\cos\theta$
- **Tangent** — $\tan\theta=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$
- **Cotangent** — $\cot\theta=\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}$

Circle and cycle constants:

$$
360^\circ = 12\times 30^\circ = 7\times\approx 51.4286^\circ
$$

Clock division he employed (three dials; each minute split into five seconds):

$$
1\,\text{h}=60\,\text{min},\qquad 1\,\text{min}=5\,\text{s}_{\text{obs}}
$$

## Correspondence Types (the “Grammar”)

### Proportion (*tenasüb* / ratio that holds across scales)

$$
\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}\qquad\text{or}\qquad a:b::c:d
$$

A planetary period $P_{\text{planet}}$ maps onto a terrestrial interval $P_{\text{year}}$ or a bodily cycle when the same ratio appears.

This ratio‑logic underlies:

- planetary period ↔ seasonal interval
- lunar mansion count ↔ bodily rhythms
- zodiacal division ↔ political cycles

The ratio does not *cause* the mapping; it **reveals structural identity** already assumed in cosmology.

### Cycle (*devir* / returning period)

$$
T=\frac{360^\circ}{\omega}\qquad\text{or}\qquad n\cdot T_{\text{small}}=T_{\text{large}}
$$

Examples historically used in the tradition:

- 7 planetary spheres ↔ **7‑day week**
- 12 zodiacal signs ↔ **12 months / 12 hours**
- 28 lunar mansions ↔ **28‑day lunar cycle**

Cycles are treated as **nested periodicities**, scaled by integer relations.

### Symmetry (*denge* / two‑sided balance)

$$
f(x)=f(-x)\quad\text{or}\quad A+B=C
$$

Visualized as a triangle, inscribed hexagram, or vesica‑like figure that balances two sides of one order.

These appear in:

- triangular or hexagram figures
- vesica‑like balanced forms
- equal‑area or equal‑arc constructions

Symmetry is the **constraint** that ensures correspondences are not arbitrary.

## Instrument and Observation Formulas

**Sextant angular distance** (*mushabbaha bi’l‑manātiq*): two rulers plus an attached arc give the observed angle $\theta$ between stars directly.

**Observational clock gear example** (dial wheel of 72 teeth for a 12‑hour face):

$$
\frac{72\text{ teeth}}{12\text{ h}}=6\text{ teeth per hour}
$$

Crown‑wheel passage for one hour: 1512 teeth $\implies$ $\approx 2.38$ s per tooth.

**Solar parameters** he obtained with the three‑point method and the clock:

- Obliquity of the ecliptic $\varepsilon\approx 23^\circ 28' 40''$
- Annual motion of solar apogee $\approx 63''$

## Precision Timekeeping as Infrastructure

His astronomical clock — one of the earliest mechanical clocks used for scientific observation — allowed recording in **hours, degrees, minutes, seconds**. Clock structure (from his treatise on mechanical clocks):

- 12‑hour dial with gear ratios such as **72 teeth / 12 hours = 6 teeth per hour**.
- Crown‑wheel timing: **1512 teeth → ≈2.38 s per tooth** (consistent with his minute‑to‑second subdivision).

These ratios are not arbitrary: they form part of the same **proportional grammar** used in astronomy.

## Solar Parameters from His Observations

Using his sextant + clock, he obtained:

- **Obliquity of the ecliptic:** $\varepsilon \approx 23^\circ 28' 40''$
- **Annual motion of solar apogee:** $\approx 63''$

These values match the historical record and were more accurate than some European contemporaries.
