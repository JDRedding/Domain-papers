
##  Gödel universe and related constructions
**Closed timelike curves**

The Gödel universe and the Tipler cylinder differ in detail but share a common mechanism. Rotation enters the metric through cross terms that tilt the light cones. Each solution possesses a critical radius or rotation rate beyond which closed timelike curves appear. In both cases the global structure—an infinite cylinder or a homogeneous rotating universe—is essential; local curvature alone does not produce the loops. Both metrics are exact solutions. The existence of closed timelike curves is therefore a feature of the theory, not an artifact of approximation.

$\log(1+\sqrt{2})$ is the critical radius at which circles of constant $r$ change causal character. Inside that value the orbits remain **spacelike**; at equality they become **null**; beyond it they are **timelike** and close upon themselves. An observer who follows one of the outer circles returns not only to the same spatial location but to the same **spacetime event**, having advanced in proper time while coordinate time has looped.

In 1949 Gödel constructed a rotating‑dust solution of Einstein’s equations in which uniform vorticity tilts the light cones so that the future can lean into the past. The spacetime is homogeneous, so closed timelike curves pass through **every** point. In the coordinates used here, the critical value is  

$$
r_c=\log(1+\sqrt{2})\approx 0.881.
$$

The line element  

$$
ds^{2}=a^{2}\bigl[dt^{2}-dr^{2}+\tfrac12 e^{2r}\,d\phi^{2}-2e^{r}\,dt\,d\phi\bigr]+a^{2}\,dz^{2}
$$

realizes that vorticity. The cross term $-2e^{r}\,dt\,d\phi$ produces a uniform tilt of the null cones. Diagrams of the geometry display the cones, geodesics, caustics, and the refocusing of future and past cones that accompany the time loops.

### **1. The Gödel metric**

Gödel’s universe consists of a pressureless fluid (dust) of density $\rho$ and four‑velocity $u^{\mu}$,

$$
T^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{dust}}=\rho\,u^{\mu}u^{\nu},
$$

together with a cosmological‑constant term

$$
T^{\mu\nu}_{(\Lambda)}=-\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}\,g^{\mu\nu}.
$$

The field equations are

$$
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G\,T^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{dust}}.
$$

The constants $\rho$ and $\Lambda$ are chosen so that the spacetime is homogeneous and the dust rotates rigidly: vorticity is constant, shear and expansion vanish. The scale $a>0$ fixes the curvature. Substituting the metric into the Einstein tensor yields a constant density, a negative cosmological constant, and a four‑velocity aligned with the world‑lines of constant $(r,\phi)$. Every comoving observer therefore measures the same vorticity.

To locate $r_c$, consider the closed curves of constant $t$ and $r$ with $\phi\in[0,2\pi]$. The squared interval along these curves, computed in the full metric, changes signature at  

$$
r_c=\log(1+\sqrt{2}).
$$

Inside the critical cylinder the curves are spacelike; on it they are null; outside they are timelike. Homogeneity implies that the same structure can be translated to any event, so closed timelike curves exist through every point. Equivalent expressions for the same physical radius appear in other charts (${arccosh}3$, $\sinh^{-1}1$, etc.). The circles themselves are not geodesics; an observer who remains on one must accelerate, and the required acceleration diverges as $r\to r_c^{+}$.

### **2. The Tipler cylinder**

A second exact construction admitting closed timelike curves is an infinitely long, rigidly rotating cylinder of matter. A stationary, cylindrically symmetric metric with rotational cross terms $dt\,d\phi$ is solved for rotating dust. If the surface speed approaches the speed of light and the cylinder is infinitely long, closed timelike curves appear in the exterior: a trajectory spiraling around the cylinder can return to an earlier event.

The construction is unphysical. Infinite length cannot be realized; a finite cylinder alters the global causal structure. The required rotation is extreme enough that ordinary matter would violate energy conditions or become unstable. Studies of more realistic cylinders typically find either that closed curves fail to form or that they require exotic matter or special boundary conditions. The example remains valuable as a demonstration that sufficiently strong rotation, combined with a particular global geometry, is compatible with Einstein’s equations and with causality violation.

### **3. Causality**

A closed timelike curve is a path $\gamma(\tau)$ that is everywhere timelike and satisfies $\gamma(\tau_1)=\gamma(\tau_0)$ for some $\tau_1>\tau_0$. Proper time increases along the curve, yet the observer returns to the same event. In the Gödel spacetime the circles with $r>\log(1+\sqrt{2})$ are of this type. Future and past cones can intersect; there is no globally well‑ordered “absolute future.”

Such geometries fail to be globally hyperbolic. No Cauchy surface exists on which data determine the entire spacetime, so a global initial‑value problem cannot be posed. Predictability is limited by geometry rather than quantum mechanics. The same failure of global hyperbolicity appears in typical Tipler‑type constructions.

Hawking’s chronology‑protection conjecture asserts that the laws of physics prevent the formation of macroscopic time machines. Proposed mechanisms include divergences of quantum fields near would‑be chronology horizons and classical or semiclassical instabilities that destroy the geometry before closed curves become available. Many time‑machine metrics violate classical energy conditions. Gödel’s dust‑plus‑$\Lambda$ solution is classical but highly special and does not describe a realistic cosmology. Some extensions of general relativity exclude Gödel‑type closed curves; others still admit them. The field equations therefore do not by themselves protect causality; additional physical restrictions must be imposed.

---

Further work may include an explicit derivation from a rotating‑dust ansatz, a comparison with other Gödel‑type metrics in which the rotation profile or $\Lambda$ is varied, and a detailed map of the causal structure, including the refocusing of null geodesics.
