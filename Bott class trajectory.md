# Bott class trajectory

In rigorous topology/C\*-algebra K‑theory, the *trajectory of the Bott class* is the structured way the Bott element propagates through suspensions, external products, and stabilization maps, generating the 2‑periodic isomorphisms  

$$
K_i(A)\;\cong\;K_{i+2}(A)
$$

and determining how Bott periodicity is implemented at the level of functors, spectra, and C\*-algebraic suspensions.

To avoid conceptual collisions:

- **Bott periodicity** — algebraic topology / operator algebras.  
- **TCFT** — nonequilibrium statistical physics.  
- **LCTM** — longitudinal mixture modelling.  

Only the first matches general mathematical usage.

Let $B$ be the Bott operator. Then:

- **Structure:** $B$ is a degree‑2 generator in the KU spectrum.  
- **Interaction:** external product $x\mapsto x\otimes B$ induces the forward flow.  
- **Dynamics:** repeated application produces the periodic orbit  

$$
K_i(A)\xrightarrow{B}K_{i+2}(A)\xrightarrow{B}K_{i+4}(A)\cdots.
$$
  
- **Stability:** the inverse operator $B^{-1}$ closes the cycle.

Thus the Bott trajectory is the 2‑step periodic orbit of the K‑theory functor under the Bott operator.

---

## 1. The Bott element 
Ggenerator of periodic motion

Let  

$$
\beta\in K_0(S^2)\cong \mathbb Z
$$

be the Bott class. For any C\*-algebra $A$, the Bott map is the external product

$$
\beta_A : K_0(A)\longrightarrow K_0(S^2 A),\qquad x\mapsto x\otimes \beta.
$$

This is the *first step* of the trajectory: a functorial push‑forward along the suspension functor $S^2$ driven by the fixed generator $\beta$.

The inverse map  

$$
\alpha_A : K_0(S^2 A)\longrightarrow K_0(A)
$$

is defined using the inverse Bott element $\alpha\in K_0(S^{-2})$, satisfying

$$
\alpha_A(\beta_A(x)) = x.
$$

Thus the Bott class acts as a *stable propagator* across the 2‑step cycle.

---

## 2. The trajectory 
A repeated suspension–product dynamical system

Define the iterated suspensions

$$
S^{2k}A = C_0(\mathbb R^{2k})\otimes A.
$$

The Bott class induces a chain of isomorphisms

$$
K_0(A)\xrightarrow{\beta_A}K_0(S^2A)\xrightarrow{\beta_{S^2A}}K_0(S^4A)\xrightarrow{\beta_{S^4A}}\cdots
$$

and similarly backwards via $\alpha$.

This chain is the *trajectory*:

- **Forward motion:** repeated tensoring with $\beta$.  
- **Backward motion:** repeated tensoring with $\alpha$.  
- **Cycle:** after two steps, the system returns to the same K‑group up to canonical identification.

Formally, the Bott trajectory is the orbit of $K_0(A)$ under the monoid generated by $\beta$ and $\alpha$.

## 3. Spectrum‑level  
Complex K‑theory is represented by the spectrum $KU$. Bott periodicity states

$$
\Sigma^2 KU \simeq KU.
$$

The Bott class corresponds to the fundamental class  

$$
\beta\in \pi_2(KU)
$$

and its trajectory is the repeated action of $\beta$ on the homotopy groups:

$$
\pi_n(KU)\xrightarrow{\cdot\beta}\pi_{n+2}(KU).
$$

Thus the Bott trajectory is literally the *shift operator* on the spectrum’s homotopy groups.

This is the most abstract and most stable formulation.

---

## 4. C\*-algebraic formulation
: The suspension functor as a flow

For C\*-algebras, the suspension functor $S$ is a *geometric flow*:

$$
S(A)=C_0(\mathbb R)\otimes A.
$$

The Bott class induces a natural transformation

$$
\beta: \mathrm{Id}_{K_0} \Rightarrow K_0\circ S^2.
$$

Thus the Bott trajectory is the orbit of the identity functor under the endofunctor $S^2$ with generator $\beta$.

This is the categorical version of the trajectory.

---

## 5. Classical topology
: Trajectory through unitary‑group stabilization

Bott’s original theorem states that the stabilization maps

$$
U(n)\hookrightarrow U(n+1)
$$

eventually become homotopy equivalences in a 2‑periodic pattern.

The Bott class corresponds to the generator of

$$
\pi_2(U)\cong \mathbb Z,
$$

and its trajectory is the repeated suspension of this generator through the stable unitary group.

This is the geometric origin of the trajectory.
