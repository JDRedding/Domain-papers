# The Sardaukar Chant

The core track is the Sardaukar war-ritual throat-chant from the Salusa Secundus scene in *Dune* (2021). Extended versions circulate widely as 1-hour loops and fan cuts drawn from Hans Zimmer’s official score. They expand or repeat the original material; they do not add new canonical content. The chant does not convey information. It erases individuality, fuses the cohort, binds them through shared pain, readies them for killing, invokes Imperial authority, and frightens anyone who hears it. The grammar is ritual machinery.

Common long-form versions include:

- **Sardaukar Chant – 1 Hour Long** — a full loop of the official OST mix
- **Sardaukar Chant Extended (Dune 2021)** — a widely viewed fan edit
- **Hans Zimmer – Sardaukar Chant (Complete)** — compiled from the full score
- **Sardaukar Chant Extended – 1 Hour** — another long-form loop, often framed as a meditation track

The chant is more than a sound cue. It is the audible form of Sardaukar identity: a ritual language of fanaticism, discipline, shared suffering, and Imperial terror.

---

## Lore: What the Chant Means

In *Dune*, the chant expresses the Sardaukar worldview. It encodes their origins on Salusa Secundus, their warrior-cult, and their function as House Corrino’s instrument of fear.

### Warrior-religion

The chant belongs to a belief system forged by hardship and indoctrination. Canon stresses their zeal and absolute loyalty to the Emperor. Ritual chanting reinforces three pillars:

- **Fanatic devotion** — they see themselves as superior, chosen by suffering
- **Shared trauma** — on Salusa Secundus, six of thirteen children die before puberty; survival becomes sacred
- **Imperial purpose** — their existence is service to House Corrino

### Ritual context

Villeneuve’s film shows Sardaukar kneeling in ranks while priests (“vicars”) anoint each forehead with blood from sacrificial victims. The ceremony:

- Affirms the warrior-cult
- Binds the cohort through death and sacrifice
- Prepares them psychologically for combat

The chant is the sonic half of that rite: a guttural, rhythmic affirmation of unity and violence.

### Political function

The Sardaukar are not only elite troops. They are the foundation of Imperial authority. Their reputation tells the Great Houses that rebellion is futile. Hearing the chant means the Emperor’s wrath is present. It is:

- A symbol of Imperial terror
- A reminder of Corrino dominance
- A psychological weapon

### Environment and culture

Salusa Secundus is a death world. The chant reflects harsh survivalism, discipline born of lethal conditions, and the doctrine that suffering creates strength. Herbert’s logic is consistent: environment creates culture; culture creates power.

### Contrast with the Fremen

The chant proclaims unmatched superiority. The Fremen, shaped by an even harsher world, defeat them. That reversal exposes the limits of manufactured fanaticism and turns the chant into an ironic emblem of a fading empire.

**In short:** the chant is a warrior-cult ritual, a bonding mechanism, a political symbol, a product of Salusa Secundus, and the sound of an empire built on fear.

## Sound: Phonetics and Performance

Spoken Sardaukar in the film is warped English. The chant is something else: a ritual register built from Tuvan-style throat singing, guttural clusters, and elongated low vowels. It is designed to sound non-human, militaristic, and liturgical.

### Phonetic profile

- Guttural consonants: `/q/`, `/kh/`, `/gh/`, `/ts’/`, `/zh/`
- Compressed CVC and CCVC syllables
- Low-frequency throat resonance from overtone singing
- Elongated finals: *guuuuuum*, *guuuuuun*
- Percussive, processional cadence

The result sits closer to Tuvan khoomei or Mongolian overtone chant than to Indo-European speech.

### Canonical lines

From the Salusa Secundus scene:

> Ts’om brovüch’on gokhulzhon dzoq’ayh qaldayh gum  
> Hölgör römbözh åzqådözh t’urukh sündukh gun  
> Mandon gözdöl våbrukh shok’ubukh qotskå gur

Approximate syllabic rendering:

> TS’OM bro-vü-ch’on go-khul-zhon dzo-q’ayh qal-dayh guuuuuum  
> HÖL-gör röm-bözh åz-qa-dözh t’u-rukh sün-dukh g uuuuuun

Signature traits:

1. **Ejectives and aspiration** — `ts’`, `q’`, `k’`; throat-driven `kh`, `gh`
2. **Front-rounded vowels** — `ü`, `ö`, `å`; Turkic/Mongolic color, “alien” to English
3. **Voiced fricatives** — `zh`, `göz`, `rö`, `bözh`; buzzing overtones that fuse with khoomei
4. **Ritual vowel stretch** — *gum* / *gun* / *gur* held as drones, not as ordinary phonemes

Zimmer replaced an earlier linguistic chant with Tuvan-style phonation: subharmonic growl, whistling overtones, sustained drones, and almost no pitch movement. Language becomes intimidation.

### Rhythmic cycle

Each line tends to move:

**violent onset cluster → front-rounded nucleus → guttural coda → drone**

That hammer pattern — cluster, guttural, drone — is the chant’s militaristic pulse.

The phonetics match the culture: harsh consonants for brutality, throat resonance for death-world survival, compressed syllables for military efficiency, alien vowels for isolation, drones for fanatic unison.

The chant is not a natural language with a fixed grammar. It is a constructed ritual register: invented lexemes, khoomei phonation, guttural clusters, elongated vowels. The IPA below records *heard* output from the ritual scene, not a dictionary language.

**Line 3**

### Phonation beyond the segments

Segmental IPA is incomplete without phonation:

- **Khoomei:** creaky subharmonics `[V̰]` plus overtone coloring `[V͎]`. Finals are closer to `/guː̰͎/` (*gum*, *gun*) and `/guː̰ɾ/` (*gur*).
- **Pharyngealization:** compressed throat yields `/qˤ/`, `/kˤ/`, `/tˤ/`, `/sˤ/`.
- **Ejectives:** strong glottalic bursts `/t͡sʼ/`, `/qʼ/`, `/kʼ/`.
- **Front-rounded nuclei:** `/ʏ/`, `/œ/`, `/ø/`, `/ɔ/` carry the alien timbre.

At the IPA level the same architecture repeats: ejective or guttural onset (`/t͡sʼ/`, `/qʼ/`, `/hœl/`) → rounded nucleus → pharyngeal or uvular coda (`/x/`, `/qˤ/`, `/ɣ/`) → elongated drone (`/uː̰͎/`).

## Reconstructed Ritual Grammar

This is not a full natural language. It is a liturgical proto-register: patterned for group chant, not conversation. The reconstruction below follows the film lines, their cadence, and Sardaukar cultural logic.

### Morphology

Agglutinative: dense stacked morphemes.

- **Roots** — violence, unity, death, loyalty
- **Aspect** — ritual phase (pre-battle, anointing, oath)
- **Collective markers** — “we as one body”
- **Imperial markers** — loyalty to Corrino
- **Intensifiers** — extra gutturals for aggression

*römbözh*: `röm-` collective body + `-bözh` consecrated / blood-bound.

### Syntax: a triad

Every line tends to move through three acts, matching Sardaukar ideology:

1. **Invocation** — identity  
2. **Binding** — oath and unity  
3. **Projection** — violence and purpose  

*Ts’om brovüch’on gokhulzhon dzoq’ayh qaldayh gum*

- *Ts’om* — we, the chosen (invocation)
- *brovüch’on gokhulzhon* — we bind ourselves through suffering (binding)
- *dzoq’ayh qaldayh gum* — we strike, endure, ascend (projection)

Template:

`[INVOCATION] + [UNITY-BINDING] + [PURPOSE-PROJECTION] + [DRONE]`

Or: `I(root) + U(root+aspect) + P(root+aspect) + D(vowel-drone)`

### Ritual tense

Not clock time. Phase of the rite:

| Form | Function |
|---|---|
| *gum* | preparatory (before battle) |
| *gun* | sacrificial (blood-anointing) |
| *gur* | ascendant (combat or death) |

Marked by the final guttural plus vowel elongation.

### Collective case

All cases are collective:

- **Nominative** — we as one body
- **Accusative** — we receive suffering
- **Imperative** — we enact violence

Marked by rounding and pharyngealization. *våbrukh*: `vå-` collective nominative + `-brukh` hardened action.

### Phonological rules of the register

1. Lines open with a violent onset: `/t͡sʼ/`, `/qʼ/`, `/kʼ/`, `/hœl/`
2. Roots contain front-rounded vowels: `ö`, `ü`, `å`, `œ`
3. Phrases close on `x`, `q`, `ɣ`, `r`
4. The last vowel is stretched into a unifying drone

### Semantic fields

**Identity**  
*ts’om* chosen · *höl* hardened · *man* survivor

**Unity**  
*röm* collective body · *brovü* bound together · *vå* fused

**Suffering**  
*gokhul* scarred · *dözh* blood-marked · *qotskå* broken and reforged

**Violence / purpose**  
*dzoq’ayh* strike · *t’urukh* advance · *shok’ubukh* purge by violence
