# Precise anchoring

**“Precise anchoring”** most often refers to either the psychological effect of numerical precision in the anchoring-and-adjustment heuristic (especially in negotiations), coming out of the idea of practical techniques for accurately positioning a boat’s anchor.

### Psychological / Negotiation Context
In judgment and decision-making research, people tend to adjust insufficiently from an initial numerical value (the “anchor”). Precise anchors (e.g., $28.75 or €14,875) are generally more potent than round ones (e.g., $30 or €15,000). People adjust less from precise numbers, so final estimates or counteroffers stay closer to the anchor.

Key findings include:
- Precise first offers lead recipients to make more conciliatory counteroffers. This is often attributed to perceptions that the precise offerer is more informed or competent (attribution-of-competence account), or because precise numbers create a finer-grained mental scale that leads to smaller adjustment steps (scale-granularity account). Both mechanisms have supporting evidence.
- Effects can differ for the person making the offer versus the recipient. Precision boosts anchoring power for recipients but can lead senders to choose less extreme opening offers.
- A “too-much-precision” effect appears with experts: moderate precision helps, but extreme precision can backfire by signaling incompetence unless justified with a rationale. Amateurs show a more linear benefit from greater precision.

Practical takeaway in negotiations: Using a precise but plausible number (rather than a round one) can strengthen your opening offer’s influence, especially with less-experienced counterparts. Pair it with a clear rationale for better results against experts.

### Boating / Fishing Context
“Precision anchoring” (or precision anchoring techniques) refers to placing an anchor exactly where intended—important in crowded harbors, deep water, or when fishing specific structures.

Core steps include:
- **Plan ahead**: Study charts for depth, bottom type, swing room, and other vessels. Use GPS, radar, or visual ranges to mark the target spot, leaving extra room for scope and vessel length.
- **Select proper gear**: Match the anchor to the bottom (sand, mud, rock, etc.). Use adequate scope (commonly 5:1 minimum, 7:1 or more in wind/current). Chain-plus-rope rode is common for holding power and shock absorption.
- **Controlled deployment**: In tight spaces, lower the anchor under control rather than free-falling it, so it lands exactly where planned. Pay out rode steadily as the boat drifts or is gently reversed.
- **Set carefully**: After deploying scope, reverse gently then more firmly to dig the anchor in. Monitor for drag using GPS or shore references, especially in deep water where re-setting is costly.

Free-fall systems (on some windlasses) enable rapid drops for precise placement over targets in fishing, trading some control for speed.
