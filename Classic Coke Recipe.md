# Coca-Cola Flavor Architecture 
Classic Reconstruction

The core is that Coca-Cola’s “secret flavor” is not one exotic ingredient. It is a **precise geometric blend of citrus terpenes, spice volatiles, caramelization products, acids, and tannins**, tuned through **acid-catalyzed hydrolysis**. Modern GC-MS work is used to support that structure. Coca-Cola’s taste can be described as a **citrus–spice terpene lattice** — mainly limonene, α-terpineol, fenchol, cinnamaldehyde, and sabinene — supported by:

- phosphoric acid
- caramelization compounds
- caffeine
- wine tannins

The tannins are used to recreate the dryness once associated with coca-leaf extract. In the reported blind tests, a reconstructed 7X concentrate plus syrup was described as **indistinguishable** from commercial Coke.

## Reconstructed formula 

### Scientific reconstruction

GC-MS and hydrolysis experiments are used to argue that:

- **α-terpineol and fenchol** supply leafy-fresh notes; α-terpineol can form from acid-driven breakdown of citrus terpenes
- **Limonene** supports lemon/lime oil as the citrus backbone
- **Acid-catalyzed hydrolysis** during syrup heating partially inverts sucrose and softens harsh citrus terpene notes
- **Wine tannins** are essential to mouthfeel and dryness

## Core flavor architecture

### Citrus terpenes
Limonene, α-terpineol, α-terpinene, fenchol.

These supply the top-note sparkle and the recognizable Coke brightness. GC-MS is described as showing them as dominant peaks.

### Spice volatiles

- Cinnamaldehyde from cassia cinnamon
- Sabinene and carene from nutmeg
- Coriander-derived compounds

These add warmth, depth, and the “cola” identity.

### Caramelization products
Furfural and ethyl levulinate.

These contribute color, burnt-sugar sweetness, and base notes.

### Acid system
Phosphoric acid plus a trace of acetic acid.

This supplies sharpness, balance, and the characteristic bite.

### Tannins
Wine tannins are used to approximate the dryness once produced by coca-leaf extract. That substitution is presented as a major reconstruction insight.

### Alcohol-based concentrate
An essential-oil blend forming the aromatic core:

A chemically grounded flavor architecture, not a manufacturing SOP. Coca-Cola’s profile can be described as two separately prepared systems:

- **Mixture A** — a terpene–spice concentrate (alcohol phase)
- **Mixture B** — a water-phase acid–tannin–caramel matrix

Those two systems are then dosed in very small amounts into a sugar solution and carbonated water.

## Mixture A — Flavor Concentrate
The described preparation diluted **20 mL of the oil blend** into **1 L of 95% ethanol**. Alcohol-based aromatic core.

### Oil blend composition
The blend is dominated by **limonene**, **α-terpineol**, **fenchol**, **cinnamaldehyde**, and **sabinene**. Those volatiles were described as the GC-MS backbone of the Coca-Cola aroma profile.

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Lemon oil | 45.8 mL |
| Lime oil | 36.5 mL |
| Tea tree oil | 8 mL |
| Cassia cinnamon oil | 4.5 mL |
| Nutmeg oil | 2.7 mL |
| Orange oil | 1.2 mL |
| Coriander oil | 0.7 mL |
| Alpha-fenchol (Fenchol isolate) | 0.6 mL |

**Note:** Tea tree oil is **not FDA-approved for food use**.
- Tea tree oil, used for 4-terpineol “coca-like” freshness

## Mixture B — Water-Phase Matrix
This phase supplies **acidity**, **dryness**, **color**, **body**, and the characteristic cola “bite.”

- Glycerin
- Phosphoric acid
- Caffeine
- Sugar, heated to induce partial inversion
- Wine tannins

## Final Assembly
Ratio for **1 L of finished cola**:

- 104 g sugar, dissolved in minimal water
- 1 mL Mixture A
- 10 mL Mixture B
- Cold carbonated water to volume

Combine with syrup, dilute with carbonated water, adjust sweetness/acidity, and chill. Diluted with water to a total volume of **1 L**.

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Durkee caramel color | 185 mL |
| Vegetable glycerin | 150 g |
| 85% phosphoric acid | 43 mL |
| Wine tannin | 10 g |
| Caffeine | 9.65 g |
| 5% vinegar | 10 mL |
| Vanilla extract | 10 mL |

## Blind taste-test claim
The ratio reported as indistinguishable from commercial Coca-Cola in blind tasting.

Across the reported trials, participants could not distinguish LabCoatz’s Lab Cola from commercial Coca-Cola; some preferred the replica.
