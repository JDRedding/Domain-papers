# Stocks

## 1. Clear Stock

The term “stock” means the juices of meats, bones, and vegetables extracted by long and slow simmering. Stock forms the basis of many soups and meat sauces.

Any bones from the meats used for food may be utilized; also all trimmings of roasts, steaks, and other meats.

### Directions

1. Cover the material in the soup kettle with cold water (a quart of water for every pound of meat or bone).  
2. Add a handful of salt and boil gently for several hours.  
3. Do not add spices.  
4. Take the vegetables out promptly when tender.  
5. Skim off the fat and all the dark scum (not the white).  
6. Add boiling water from time to time, as the stock boils away.  
7. When cooked sufficiently, take out the meat and bones and strain through a hair sieve or cloth.  
8. If not perfectly clear, stir in 1 or 2 beaten eggs, put on the fire, and as it boils up, skim off the egg, then strain carefully through a cloth.

Keep the stock in a stone jar and in a cold place. After two or three days boil up again, to prevent turning. Don’t keep too long.

## 2. Vegetable Stock

### Ingredients

- 2 oz kidney beans  
- 2 oz split peas  
- 1 onion  
- 1 carrot  
- 1 stick of celery  
- Some parsley and herbs  
- Pepper  
- Salt  
- 5 cloves  
- 1 blade of mace (outer hull of nutmeg)  
- 2 quarts water  

### Directions

1. Boil all ingredients in 2 quarts of water for three or four hours.  
2. Strain.  
3. If left to stand and the sediment is then skimmed off, it will keep probably as long as desired.

This stock may serve as the basis for many good soups or gravies. A good cook will aim to keep a supply on hand.

## 3. White Stock

### Ingredients

- 4 pounds knuckle of veal  
- Any poultry trimmings or a rabbit  
- 4 slices lean ham  
- 3 carrots  
- 2 onions  
- 1 head of celery  
- 12 white peppercorns  
- 2 oz salt  
- 1 blade of mace  
- A bunch of herbs  
- 1 oz butter  
- 4 quarts water  

### Directions

1. Cut up the veal.  
2. Rub the stewpan with the butter.  
3. Put in the veal, bones, poultry trimmings, and ham.  
4. Moisten with 1/2 pint of water and simmer until the gravy begins to flow.  
5. Add the 4 quarts of water and the remainder of the ingredients.  
6. Simmer for five hours.  
7. Skim and strain carefully through a fine hair sieve.

This stock may be made stronger by using the water in which a young fowl or turkey has been boiled, by increasing the quantity of veal, or by adding an old fowl too tough to be eaten. A young fowl cooked in this stock has a delicious flavor. Bones used once for brown stock can be boiled again for white stock with fresh vegetables.

---

## 4. Brown Stock

Proceed in the same way as for white stock, substituting beef for veal.

The hind shin bone of a beef will yield about 5 quarts of the best stock. Any remnants of gravy, all the trimmings and broken-up bones of other meats, if in good condition, may be put in.

### Directions

1. Let it come to a boil quickly.  
2. Skim off the dark scum (not the white scum appearing afterwards).  
3. Cover tightly and let simmer for four or five hours.  
4. Add a little boiling water from time to time.

## 5. Fish Stock

### Ingredients

- 2 pounds of any inexpensive kind of fresh fish, or the trimmings and heads of uncooked fish  
- 1 head of celery  
- 1 root of parsley  
- 1 blade or two of mace (outer hull of nutmeg)  
- 1 bay leaf  
- A few cloves  
- White pepper and salt, to taste  
- 3 quarts water  

### Directions

1. Put the whole into a saucepan.  
2. Cover and let simmer gently for about two hours.  
3. Strain off the liquor.
