# The Geometry of Becoming
**Formal Reconstruction of the Geometric Grammar in Welling, Böhme, and Gichtel**

**Abstract** 

These are the quantitative identities, equations, principles, or laws in Georg von Welling’s *Opus Mago-Cabbalisticum et Theosophicum*, Jakob Böhme’s major works, or Johann Georg Gichtel’s *Theosophia Practica*. The following notation is a modern structural formalization intended to capture invariant relationships found throughout the symbolic system. 

## Introduction

The great diagrammatic traditions of early modern Christian theosophy have often been dismissed as curiosities of mystical speculation or treated merely as repositories of esoteric symbolism. Yet the visual systems developed by Georg von Welling, Jakob Böhme, and Johann Georg Gichtel exhibit a remarkable internal coherence. Across circles, triangles, cubes, trees, concentric spheres, and numerical correspondences, one finds not a collection of arbitrary symbols but a recurring structural logic concerned with the emergence of order, distinction, and manifestation.

Contrary to modern expectations, these works do not contain mathematics in the familiar sense. Their numbers and geometric forms do function as geometry, serving as a symbolic language through which ontological processes are expressed. The point represents primordial unity, the line expresses separation, the triangle mediates polarity, the circle denotes wholeness and emanation, and the cube signifies fixation, embodiment, and completed creation.

This article proposes a reconstruction of that symbolic language. The goal is not to claim that Welling, Böhme, or Gichtel possessed a hidden theory, nor to impose concepts onto historical texts. Rather, it is to identify the invariant structures repeatedly employed throughout their diagrams and to express those structures using a consistent formal notation. The resulting equations should therefore be understood as interpretive abstractions: they are reconstructions of relationships implicit in the visual grammar, not statements found in the original works themselves.

Viewed in this manner, the diagrams reveal an underlying sequence of generative operations. Unity gives rise to distinction. Distinction generates polarity. Polarity requires mediation. Mediation establishes stable structures. Stable structures become ordered worlds. The recurring geometric forms serve as operators within this process, encoding transitions between levels of reality rather than describing objects located within space. Their primary concern is not geometry as measurement, but geometry as becoming.

What emerges is a symbolic process ontology expressed visually rather than algebraically. The circle, triangle, and cube are not merely figures; they function as formal devices that organize relationships between the unmanifest and the manifest, the simple and the complex, the undifferentiated and the structured. By translating these recurring patterns into contemporary notation, we can expose the latent architecture underlying the diagrams and clarify the conceptual grammar that unites the cosmological, alchemical, and theological systems of Welling, Böhme, and Gichtel.

The sections that follow develop this reconstruction step by step, beginning with the primordial point (Punctum), proceeding through distinction, polarity, mediation, numerical ordering, and stabilization, and culminating in a generalized model of cosmological emergence. The result is not a mathematical theory of nature, but a formalized grammar of generation: a modern representation of how these thinkers visualized the unfolding of reality from unity into world.


### I. Primordial Unity and Distinction

The primordial point (*Punctum*) is represented by

$$
P_0, \qquad \dim(P_0) = 0.
$$

The first differentiation may be written

$$
P_0 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \{A, \neg A\},
$$

where $\Delta$ denotes the primitive act of distinction.

**Interpretation.**

$$
\text{Unity} \;\rightarrow\; \text{Difference} \;\rightarrow\; \text{Multiplicity}.
$$

This captures the recurrent emergence of plurality from an undivided source.

### II. The Cube as Stabilized Manifestation

The cube (*Cubus*) represents Salt, fixity, embodiment, gravity, and completed formation.

Geometrically,

$$
C = [0,1]^3.
$$

Its combinatorial structure is

$$
V = 8, \qquad E = 12, \qquad F = 6,
$$

which satisfies the Euler relation

$$
V - E + F = 2.
$$

The principal symbolic correspondences are

$$
F = 6 \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{Six Days of Creation}, \qquad E = 12 \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{Twelve Pillars}.
$$

The cube therefore functions as the operator

$$
\operatorname{Cubus} : \text{Potentiality} \longrightarrow \text{Stable Form}.
$$

### III. Hexadic Creation and Sabbath Closure

Many diagrams encode six creative stages followed by rest:

$$
1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4 \to 5 \to 6.
$$

Completion is represented by

$$
7 = 6 + 1.
$$

More abstractly,

$$
R = T^6(P_0),
$$

where $T$ is a creative transformation and $R$ is the resulting state of completion. The seventh stage functions as closure rather than as additional construction.

### IV. Concentric Emanation

Cosmological diagrams are naturally represented as nested structures

$$
S_0 \subset S_1 \subset S_2 \subset \cdots \subset S_n.
$$

Successive levels emerge via an emanative transformation $\Gamma$:

$$
S_{n+1} = \Gamma(S_n).
$$

A common hierarchy is

$$
\text{Ungrund} \supset \text{Divinity} \supset \text{Angelic} \supset \text{Planetary} \supset \text{Elemental} \supset \text{Material}.
$$

### V. The Ungrund

The Ungrund may be represented abstractly as $U$. It is not equivalent to the empty set,

$$
U \neq \varnothing,
$$

nor is it a fully determined domain of being. Instead,

$$
U = \{ x \mid x \text{ is possible but undetermined} \}.
$$

It is best interpreted as the reservoir from which determinations emerge.

### VI. Polarity

The cosmology repeatedly employs paired oppositions

$$
(L, N),
$$

where $L =$ Light and $N =$ Night. Their relation is expressed by

$$
L \longleftrightarrow N.
$$

The symbol indicates tension, reciprocity, and mutual definition.

### VII. Triadic Mediation

Oppositions become productive through mediation:

$$
T : (L, N) \to M.
$$

The minimal generative structure is therefore the triangle

$$
\triangle(L, M, N).
$$

The triangle appears repeatedly because it represents the smallest structure capable of reconciling duality.

### VIII. Salt–Sulfur–Mercury

The fundamental alchemical trinity is

$$
\mathcal{T} = (Sa, Su, Me).
$$

The generative operation may be written symbolically as

$$
Sa \otimes Su \otimes Me \to X,
$$

where $Sa =$ Salt, $Su =$ Sulfur, $Me =$ Mercury, and $X$ denotes a manifested entity.

### IX. Numerical Correspondence

The numbers six and twelve dominate much of the visual symbolism:

$$
\frac{12}{6} = 2.
$$

More generally,

$$
N_{\text{pillars}} = 2 \cdot N_{\text{days}}.
$$

These relations are correspondential rather than causal.

### X. Universal Generative Sequence

The recurring symbolic logic of the diagrams may be compressed into

$$
P_0 \to (A, \neg A) \to \Delta \to 3 \to 6 \to 12 \to \Box \to \mathcal{U},
$$

where

- $P_0$ = primordial point,  
- $(A, \neg A)$ = distinction,  
- $3$ = mediation,  
- $6$ = creation,  
- $12$ = ordering,  
- $\Box$ = fixation,  
- $\mathcal{U}$ = manifest cosmos.

Equivalently,

$$
P_0 \to \text{Distinction} \to \text{Polarity} \to \text{Relation} \to \text{Structure} \to \text{World}.
$$

These diagrams do not attempt to describe where things are in space. They encode a grammar of generation, distinction, opposition, mediation, stabilization, and hierarchical emergence. The circle, triangle, and cube function less as geometric objects than as operators acting on being itself. In that sense, Welling, Böhme, and Gichtel can be read as proposing an early symbolic process ontology, expressed visually rather than algebraically.
