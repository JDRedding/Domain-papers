## ⭐ META: WHY THEY NEVER SAIL IN COMPANY  

- **Domain Lock:** Each ship is artifact‑bound to one sea  
- **Operator Exclusivity:** Only one operator can be active per ship  
- **Contract Limit:** PCs can carry only one operator mark

## 🜁 Meta-Structure: Why They Never Sail in Company  
All seven share a **single operator grammar**:

- **Black hull** = liminal boundary  
- **Single ship** = isolated operator  
- **One sea each** = domain exclusivity  
- **Transactional anomaly** = the price of contact  
- **Identity distortion** = the consequence  
- **Navigation hazard** = the signature  
- **Memory or truth** = the binding agent  

Together they form a **septet of maritime operators**, each governing one oceanic domain. A rutter that marks all seven is either:

- a **forgery**,  
- or the log of someone the **Ruby Galley** has already weighed.

--- 

Black hull, one sea, one transaction, identity as the price. What keeps them from collapsing into a fleet is a rule they all obey and none of them state.

**Domain lock.** Each ship can be sighted, hailed, or boarded only inside her own sea. A rutter that places the Open Lead south of the Horn, or the Counting Barque in the Banks fog, is already false. Cross the boundary and the entry stops matching the water.

**No company.** Two black ships never share a horizon, a convoy, or a harbor on the same day. If the party’s course would force a meeting, one of them is not there: the fog lifts, the lead closes, the galley has already weighed and gone. The absence is the confirmation.

**One open contract.** A ship, a captain, or a company can carry only one of their marks at a time. Ledger coin, a ruby, a forgotten name, a heading given, a monsoon answer still unpaid, a count unfinished, oil that will not freeze. Taking a second bargain voids the first in the worst direction: the debt comes due, the stone cracks, the lead seals over the man you pulled out.

**The rutter test.** A chart or log that marks all seven is either a forgery or the book of someone the Ruby Galley has already weighed. In play, that book is the adventure, not the ships. The pages are right about each sea and wrong about the hand that wrote them.

Compact use at the table:

| Sea | Ship | Bargain | Price in play |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arctic | Open Lead | A name of the lost, paid in oil that never freezes | Cold checks worsen in her sight; the rescued sleep until the lead closes |
| North Atlantic | Ledger | Cargo that was never listed, paid in next year’s coin | One disaster rerolled; a kinsman not yet born owes the balance |
| South Atlantic | Empty Prize | She takes the wreck’s name, not the crew | Fail the name save: one crucial noun is gone until carved into floating wood |
| North Pacific | Nootka Hull | A heading, answered in your own tongue | Give it and she is already at the anchorage; refuse and the next gale wears her profile |
| South Pacific | Ruby Galley | Red stones by weight of living men | One true sight from the stone; break it and the weighed knows your beach |
| Indian Ocean | Monsoon Oath | One passage, one true answer at the far anchorage | Lie and the coast leaves the rutter until the truth is spoken |
| Southern Ocean | Counting Barque | She sails abeam until the watch counts | Finish the count and you are on her muster until someone else counts you off |

They still do not sail together. The party meets at most one. The others exist as crossed-out lines, harbor talk, and a book that should not be complete.
