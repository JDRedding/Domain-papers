# Franz Brasser’s 9×9 Lunar Magic Square

Brasser’s 1552 9×9 magic square uses the numbers 1–81. Every row, column, and diagonal sums to 369. It shows how mathematical order arises from balanced relationships among distinct values rather than from uniformity.

- **Distinctness:** every integer 1–81 is unique, occupying a specific cell.
- **Constraint:** every row, column, and diagonal must sum to 369.
- **Order:** emerges from how these distinct values are **related**, not from any uniform sequence.

That’s the core: a finite, discrete set of values, arranged so that **global balance** is maintained through **local pairings and symmetries**—exactly the kind of structure that later traditions read as a model of hidden cosmic order.

The square gave is a genuine normal 9×9 magic square: the numbers 1 through 81 each appear once, every row, column and both main diagonals sum to 369, the grand total is 3321, and the center cell is 41.

$$
M = \frac{n(n^2+1)}{2} = \frac{9\times 82}{2} = 369
$$

It is the classic “Siamese” / de la Loubère construction that Agrippa published as the lunar kamea (order 9) in *De occulta philosophia* (1533). Later editions of Franz Brasser’s merchant arithmetic *Rekenboek* (first printed Lübeck 1552) reproduced a 9×9 square as a demonstration of computational skill; the version that circulated in the 1693/1697 Hamburg printings is the one now associated with Brasser. What began as a practical reckoning exercise thus inherited the older planetary-talisman tradition and, by the nineteenth century, entered systems such as Olney Richmond’s *Mystic Test Book* (1919), where the same array is linked to playing-card layouts, natal dates, and three-dimensional unfoldings of magic crosses and cubes.

The 9×9 square, tied to the Moon in planetary kamea traditions, originated in a Lübeck reckoning master’s practical arithmetic book for commerce but evolved into a symbol of hidden cosmic structure preserved in later editions.

A rich object—this 9×9 is a great example of “order from distinctness.”

## 1. Basic structure and checks

- **Order:** \(n = 9\)
- **Cell count:** \(n^2 = 81\)
- **Values:** \(\{1, 2, \dots, 81\}\) (each used exactly once)
- **Magic constant:**

$$
M = \frac{n(n^2+1)}{2} = \frac{9 \times 82}{2} = 369
$$

- **Total sum:**

$$
S = \frac{n^2(n^2+1)}{2} = \frac{81 \times 82}{2} = 3321
$$

- **Center cell:**

$$
\frac{n^2+1}{2} = 41
$$

- **Planetary kamea association:** Moon (order 9)

## 2. Balanced relationships vs. uniformity

Order emerges here not from uniformity but from complementary pairing: each pair of numbers equidistant from the center sums to 82, the nine 3×3 blocks themselves form a scaled magic square, and the wrap-around “staircase” rule that generated the square guarantees the constant without ever repeating a value. That is the precise sense in which a Lübeck schoolmaster’s commercial textbook could become a compact emblem of hidden cosmic structure.

This square is not “smooth” or uniform—values jump around—but several balancing mechanisms are visible:

- **Complementary pairs:** Many entries occur in pairs summing to 82 (since \(1+81=82\), \(2+80=82\), etc.). These complements are distributed so that when add across rows/columns, the deviations cancel and yield 369.
- **Central anchoring:** The center 41 is itself the mean of \(\{1,\dots,81\}\):

$$
\frac{1+81}{2} = 41
$$

Around this center, numbers are arranged so that “high” and “low” values interlock, keeping each line balanced.

- **Patterned displacement:** The square can be seen as a structured permutation of a more regular pattern (related to Latin-square–like constructions and modular arithmetic). The order is not in the visible sequence of numbers but in the hidden rules that place them.

So the “order” arises from **relational constraints** (pair sums, line sums, symmetry) rather than from any simple monotone progression.

## 3. Planetary kamea: Moon (order 9)

In planetary kamea traditions, each planet is assigned a magic square of specific order; the Moon is associated with the 9×9 square using 1–81 and magic constant 369. The Brasser/Lübeck square is one historical realization of that lunar kamea.

- **Commerce origin:** It appears in a 1552 practical arithmetic book by a Lübeck reckoning master—originally a tool in the context of calculation and pedagogy, not esoteric ritual.
- **Symbolic evolution:** Later occult and astrological traditions reinterpreted such squares as maps of cosmic order: each cell becomes a locus of planetary influence, and the balanced sums encode a “harmonized” universe.

Thus a commercial reckoning device becomes a **cosmogram**—a diagram of hidden structure.

## 4. Connection to Richmond’s *Mystic Test Book*

Quoting an earlier post on Olney Richmond’s 1919 *Mystic Test Book*, the square connects to systems blending playing cards, astrology, birth dates, and 3D geometric unfoldings of magic crosses and cubes for structured divination.

Olney Richmond’s *Mystic Test Book* (1893/1919 editions) builds a system where:

- playing cards are mapped to astrological bodies,
- birth dates determine card patterns and planetary lines,
- geometric unfoldings (magic crosses, cubes, Tarots) provide 2D/3D frameworks for divination.

Within that framework:

- number 9 and lunar effects are treated as especially “mystic,” with emphasis on cycles, reflection, and subtle influence;
- magic squares and crosses act as coordinate systems: each position is a node in a structured field of meaning, much like each cell in the 9×9 lunar kamea.

9×9 square fits naturally into this worldview:

- **Arithmetic layer:** sums, complements, modular patterns.
- **Astrological layer:** Moon as ruler of cycles, tides, emotional flux.
- **Divinatory layer:** each distinct value (1–81) is a unique “card” or “point,” yet all are bound into a single balanced whole.
