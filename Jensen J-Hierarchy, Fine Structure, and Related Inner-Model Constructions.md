# Jensen $J$ -Hierarchy, Fine Structure, and Related Inner-Model Constructions

**Document status.** This specification standardizes definitions, notation, relationships, and requirements drawn from the constructible universe $L$, Jensen’s $J$-hierarchy, rudimentary functions, Gödel operations, fine structure, mice, and core models. It closes indexing ambiguities, incomplete operation lists, imprecise projectum statements, and incomplete $L$ – $J$ correspondences present in informal expositions. Terminology, numbering, and wording are aligned for implementation, formalization, and cross-team use.

**Normative conventions.** Definitions use “is defined as.” Mandatory properties and implementation constraints use “shall.” Optional or equivalent presentations are marked “may.” All structures are interpreted in ZF (or a weak fragment sufficient for the constructions). Transitive sets are identified with their structures $\langle M,\in\rangle$ unless extra predicates are stated.

---

### 1. Purpose and scope

1.1 This specification defines a single consistent presentation of:
- Gödel operations and the constructible hierarchy $L$;
- rudimentary functions and the Jensen hierarchy $\langle J_\alpha\mid\alpha\in\mathrm{On}\rangle$;
- the exact relationship $L=\bigcup_\alpha J_\alpha$;
- the fine-structural invariants of levels $J_\alpha$ (and $J$-structures);
- the notions of premouse, mouse, and core model at the level required for consistency with the $J$-hierarchy;
- constraints on any executable realization of initial segments of $L$ or $J$.

1.2 The $J$-hierarchy is treated as an equivalent (non-normative alternative) stratification of the same class $L$, not as a distinct universe. Forcing over $L$, mice, and core models are specified only to the extent they depend on the $J$-hierarchy and fine structure.

1.3 Out of scope: full iteration strategies for mice with long extenders, complete proofs of $\square$ or covering, and any claim of a terminating algorithm that enumerates the proper class $L$.

---

### 2. Terms and definitions

2.1 **Def($X$)** — the set of all subsets of $X$ first-order definable over $\langle X,\in\rangle$ with parameters from $X$.

2.2 **Rudimentary function** — a function generated by the schemata of Section 5.

2.3 **$\mathrm{rud}(X)$** — the smallest set containing $X\cup\{X\}$ and closed under all rudimentary functions.

2.4 **$J$-structure** — an amenable structure of the form $\langle J_\alpha^A,B\rangle$ (or $\langle J_\alpha,A\rangle$) for limit $\alpha$.

2.5 **Projectum $\rho_M^n$** — the $\Sigma_n$-projectum of a $J$-structure $M$ (Definition 8.2).

2.6 **Standard parameter $p_M^n$** — the $\le_L$-least parameter witnessing the projectum (Definition 8.3).

2.7 **Premouse** — a structure $J_\alpha^{\vec E}$ (or operator analogue) satisfying the first-order axioms of an initial segment of an extender model.

2.8 **Mouse** — an iterable premouse.

2.9 **Core model $K$** — the union of all mice (relative to a given large-cardinal background assumption).

2.10 Terms “constructible,” “amenable,” “sound,” “solid,” and “universal” shall be used only in their standard fine-structural meanings.

---

### 3. Notation

3.1 Ordinals are denoted $\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\rho$. Limit ordinals are denoted $\lambda$.

3.2 The constructible hierarchy is $\langle L_\alpha\mid\alpha\in\mathrm{On}\rangle$, $L=\bigcup L_\alpha$.

3.3 The Jensen hierarchy is $\langle J_\alpha\mid\alpha\in\mathrm{On}\rangle$, $J=\bigcup J_\alpha$.

3.4 $\mathrm{On}\cap J_\alpha=\omega\alpha$ under the indexing of Section 6.

3.5 $\Sigma_n(M)$ denotes the class of relations $\Sigma_n$-definable over $M$ with parameters from $M$.

3.6 Gödel operations are written $\mathfrak{F}_1,\dots,\mathfrak{F}_8$.

---

### 4. Gödel operations

4.1 The eight fundamental operations of Gödel (1940) are defined as follows. (Ordered pairs and triples are Kuratowski pairs.)

$$
\begin{align*}
\mathfrak{F}_1(X,Y)&=\{X,Y\},\\
\mathfrak{F}_2(X,Y)&=\{(a,b)\in X\mid a\in b\},\\
\mathfrak{F}_3(X,Y)&=X\setminus Y,\\
\mathfrak{F}_4(X,Y)&=\{(a,b)\in X\mid b\in Y\},\\
\mathfrak{F}_5(X,Y)&=\{b\in X\mid\exists a\,(a,b)\in Y\},\\
\mathfrak{F}_6(X,Y)&=\{(a,b)\in X\mid(b,a)\in Y\},\\
\mathfrak{F}_7(X,Y)&=\{(a,b,c)\in X\mid(a,c,b)\in Y\},\\
\mathfrak{F}_8(X,Y)&=\{(a,b,c)\in X\mid(c,a,b)\in Y\}.
\end{align*}
$$

4.2 An equivalent 10-operation basis (Jech) may be substituted provided the generated closure coincides on transitive sets.

4.3 Gödel’s normal-form theorem shall be taken as: every $\Delta_0$ function is a composition of Gödel operations.

### 5. Rudimentary functions

5.1 A function $f:V^n\to V$ is rudimentary if it is generated from the following schemata:
- projections and constant functions;
- pairing: $f(\vec x)=\{x_i,x_j\}$;
- difference: $f(\vec x)=x_i\setminus x_j$;
- composition of rudimentary functions;
- bounded union: $f(y,\vec x)=\bigcup_{z\in y}g(z,\vec x)$ whenever $g$ is rudimentary.

5.2 $\mathrm{rud}(X)$ shall be the smallest set containing $X\cup\{X\}$ and closed under all rudimentary functions. Equivalently, $\mathrm{rud}(X)$ is obtained by iterating a finite basis of functions (including the Gödel operations) $\omega$ times.

5.3 If $N$ is transitive and rudimentarily closed, then

$$
\mathcal{P}(N)\cap\mathrm{rud}(N\cup\{N\})=\mathrm{Def}(N).
$$

---

### 6. Jensen hierarchy (normative definition)

6.1 The hierarchy $\langle J_\alpha\mid\alpha\in\mathrm{On}\rangle$ is defined by recursion:

$$
J_0=\emptyset,
\quad
J_{\alpha+1}=\mathrm{rud}(J_\alpha\cup\{J_\alpha\}),
\quad
J_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}J_\alpha
\quad(\lambda\text{ limit}).
$$

6.2 Each $J_\alpha$ shall be transitive. $\mathrm{On}\cap J_\alpha=\omega\alpha$. The sequence is strictly increasing and continuous at limits.

6.3 The defining property relating definability to rudimentary closure shall hold:

$$
J_{\alpha+1}\cap\mathcal{P}(J_\alpha)=\mathrm{Def}(J_\alpha).
$$

(The same identity holds with $\Sigma_1(J_\alpha)$ on the right-hand side for the successor step.)

6.4 Alternative indexing by limit ordinals only ($J_\omega=\mathrm{rud}(\emptyset)$, $J_{\beta+\omega}=\mathrm{rud}(J_\beta)$, $\mathrm{On}\cap J_\alpha=\alpha$) is permitted as a non-normative equivalent presentation; conversion shall use $J_\alpha^{\mathrm{limit-index}}=J_{\omega\cdot\alpha}^{\mathrm{all-ordinals-index}}$ at additively closed points.

6.5 Relativized hierarchies $J_\alpha^A$ and $J_\alpha[A]$ are obtained by replacing $\mathrm{rud}$ with the corresponding $A$-relativized closure.

### 7. Constructible hierarchy $L$ and relationship to $J$

7.1 The hierarchy $\langle L_\alpha\mid\alpha\in\mathrm{On}\rangle$ is defined by

$$
L_0=\emptyset,
\quad
L_{\alpha+1}=\mathrm{Def}(L_\alpha),
\quad
L_\lambda=\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}L_\beta.
$$

Equivalently, $L_{\alpha+1}$ is the intersection of $\mathcal{P}(L_\alpha)$ with the closure of $L_\alpha\cup\{L_\alpha\}$ under the Gödel operations of Section 4.

7.2 The two hierarchies shall satisfy

$$
L=\bigcup_{\alpha}J_\alpha=J.
$$

More precisely:
- $J_\alpha=L_{\omega\alpha}$ whenever $\omega\alpha=\alpha$ or $\alpha$ is additively closed;
- $L_{\omega+\alpha}=J_{1+\alpha}\cap V_{\omega+\alpha}$;
- $J_\alpha=L_\alpha$ for many closed ordinals (in particular admissible ordinals in the appropriate indexing).

7.3 Both hierarchies admit a uniformly $\Sigma_1$-definable well-ordering and satisfy the condensation lemma.

---

### 8. Fine structure

8.1 Fine structure is the analysis of $\Sigma_n$-definability over levels $J_\alpha$ (or $J$-structures). Every $J_\alpha$ ($\alpha>0$) shall carry a uniformly $\Sigma_1$-definable well-ordering.

8.2 The $\Sigma_n$-projectum $\rho_M^n$ of a $J$-structure $M=J_\alpha$ (or $\langle J_\alpha^A,B\rangle$) is the largest $\rho\le\alpha$ such that $\langle J_\rho,A\rangle$ remains amenable for every $A\in\Sigma_n(M)\cap\mathcal{P}(J_\rho)$. Equivalently (and required to be shown equivalent in any implementation), it is the least $\rho$ such that some $\Sigma_n(M)$ subset of $\omega\rho$ does not belong to $M$.

8.3 The standard parameter $p_M^n$ is the $\le_L$-least finite sequence of elements of $M$ that witnesses $\rho_M^n$.

8.4 For each $n$, a $\Sigma_n$-Skolem function for $M$ that is itself $\Sigma_n(M)$-definable shall exist (uniformization).

8.5 Reducts, master codes, cores, solidity, and universality shall be defined from $\langle\rho_M^n,p_M^n\rangle$ in the standard way. Condensation for sound $J$-structures shall hold: the transitive collapse of a sufficiently elementary substructure is again a $J$-structure (or an initial segment thereof).

---

### 9. Mice, core models, and forcing over $L$

9.1 A premouse is a structure of the form $J_\alpha^{\vec E}$ whose extender sequence satisfies the first-order coherence, initial-segment, and amenability conditions of the chosen fine-structural convention (Mitchell–Steel or Jensen $\Sigma^*$).

9.2 A mouse is a premouse that is iterable (every iteration tree of the appropriate length and degree has a well-founded branch). Comparison of mice shall be performed by simultaneous iteration.

9.3 The core model $K$ (relative to a given anti-large-cardinal hypothesis) is the union of all mice. When no inner model with a measurable cardinal exists, $K=L$. $K$ shall satisfy GCH, $\square_\kappa$ at every infinite cardinal $\kappa$, and the appropriate covering lemma.

9.4 Forcing over $L$ is ordinary set forcing with posets in $L$. The generic extension is of the form $L[G]$. Fine-structural analysis of $L[G]$ is permitted but is not required for the core definitions of Sections 6–8.

---

### 10. Executable realization requirements

10.1 The proper class $L$ (equivalently $J$) shall not be required to possess a finite terminating enumeration.

10.2 Any implementation shall compute exact finite initial segments:
- $J_1=V_\omega=$ hereditarily finite sets;
- successive applications of a finite basis of rudimentary (or Gödel) operations to obtain approximations to $J_n$ for concrete finite $n$.

10.3 The implementation shall maintain the invariants of Sections 6.2–6.3 and 7.2 on all computed segments. Relativized constructions $J_\alpha[A]$ for hereditarily finite or recursive $A$ are permitted.

10.4 Output shall include the $\Sigma_1$ well-ordering restricted to the computed segment.

---

### 11. Closed inconsistencies and gaps

11.1 Indexing. Both “all-ordinals” and “limit-ordinals” conventions appear in the literature. This specification adopts the all-ordinals recursion of Section 6.1 as normative and records the conversion rule in 6.4.

11.2 Definability identity. Sources state both $\mathrm{Def}(J_\alpha)$ and $\Sigma_1(J_\alpha)$. Both are correct for the successor step; the specification requires the $\mathrm{Def}$ form and notes the $\Sigma_1$ equivalent.

11.3 Projectum. Amenability-maximality and “first new subset of $\omega\rho$” are required to be treated as equivalent.

11.4 Gödel versus rudimentary bases. The eight operations of Section 4 generate the same closure as the rudimentary schemata of Section 5 on the structures under consideration.

11.5 Rank. $\mathrm{On}\cap J_\alpha=\omega\alpha$ (all-ordinals indexing) is required; statements that $J_\alpha$ has rank $\alpha$ apply only to the limit-ordinals indexing.
