# Palladium pg rule
**One-page core rules summary for the Palladium Megaversal system**  
(Used across *Rifts*, *Palladium Fantasy*, *Heroes Unlimited*, *After the Bomb*, and related lines.)

This is a compact reference, not a substitute for the books. Palladium emphasizes GM rulings over exhaustive codification. The Megaversal system uses roll-under percentile skills, roll-high d20 combat and saves, and HP/SDC/MDC damage tracks. It is built around eight attributes and class-based abilities. This is a compact, table-ready summary of the system’s universal mechanics.

## System Identity

Used across most Palladium RPGs (*Rifts*, *Heroes Unlimited*, *Palladium Fantasy*, and others). Derived from early AD&D, but with its own class, energy, and damage structures. Core mechanics stay consistent even as settings vary widely.

### Attributes (8)
Roll 3d6 for each attribute (humans). A result of 16+ lets you add another d6; a 6 on that extra die can add one more.

**Attributes:** IQ, ME, MA, PS, PP, PE, PB, Spd.

High scores (usually 16–17+) grant bonuses such as skill percentage, strike/parry/dodge, damage, charm/impress, or trust/intimidate. Low scores generally do not impose mechanical penalties.

## Core Dice Mechanics

- **Skill checks (percentile):** Roll d100 ≤ skill% to succeed.
- **Combat rolls (d20):** Roll d20 + bonuses ≥ target to hit.
- **Saving throws (d20):** Roll d20 + bonuses ≥ save target (varies by effect).
- **Damage:** Roll weapon dice (d4/d6/d8/d10) and apply to HP, SDC, or MDC.

## Attributes (3d6 baseline for humans)

All characters share eight core attributes:

- **IQ** — Intelligence; affects skill bonuses
- **MA** — Mental Affinity; personality and charisma
- **ME** — Mental Endurance; willpower; affects psionic saves
- **PE** — Physical Endurance; toughness; affects endurance and saves
- **PP** — Physical Prowess; agility; affects combat bonuses
- **PS** — Physical Strength; affects melee damage
- **PB** — Physical Beauty; appearance
- **Spd** — Speed; running speed

## Damage Tracks

- **Hit Points (HP):** Biological injury. Damage here is life-threatening.
- **Structural Damage Capacity (SDC):** Physical wear and superficial injury. Used for objects and as a buffer for many characters.
- **Mega-Damage Capacity (MDC):** 1 MDC = 100 SDC. Normal weapons cannot harm MDC targets unless they deal 100+ SDC in a single hit.

## Energy Pools

- **PPE (Potential Psychic Energy):** Fuels magic.
- **ISP (Inner Strength Points):** Fuels psionics.

## Character Classes

Characters choose one of three class types:

- **O.C.C. (Occupational Character Class):** Training-based skills and abilities.
- **R.C.C. (Racial Character Class):** Abilities tied to species.
- **P.C.C. (Psychic Character Class):** Focused on psionic powers.

## Skills

Skills come from OCC, background, or education, depending on the game line.

- Each skill has a base % plus level bonuses.
- Roll d100 ≤ modified % to succeed.

Roll d100, succeeding on a result equal to or under the skill percentage.

**Formula:** Base % + OCC/RCC bonuses + IQ bonus + per-level increases.

Many skills exist; the OCC determines which ones are available and at what starting values. Success and failure are often interpreted narratively by the GM.

### Combat basics
- **Melee round:** About 15 seconds. Attacks per round come from Hand-to-Hand skill + level + extras (extra limbs, power armor, etc.).
- **Initiative:** d20 + bonuses each round (or as needed). Highest acts first. Surprise or sneak attacks often automatically win initiative that round.
- **Strike:** d20 + bonuses. A 5+ usually hits, unless the target’s armor has a higher AR that must be beaten. A natural 20 is typically a critical (often double damage).
- **Defense:** Trained fighters can automatically attempt to Parry (same roll as strike; no extra action). Anyone can Dodge (uses an action/attack). Simultaneous attack is possible.
- **Damage:** Weapon dice + PS bonus (and other modifiers). Apply first to armor SDC, then personal SDC, then Hit Points.
- **SDC vs HP:** SDC is soak, stamina, or structure (objects and living beings). HP is serious bodily harm. Reaching 0 HP can mean coma or death, depending on the situation and available medical help.
- Armor has its own AR and SDC and can be destroyed independently.

Ranged combat, called shots, cover, and specific maneuvers (disarm, entangle, etc.) follow similar d20 + bonus logic, with GM judgment.

## Combat Flow (Universal)

1. Roll initiative (d20 + bonuses).
2. Determine actions per melee (varies by class and abilities).
3. Attack roll: d20 + bonuses vs. the target’s dodge/parry or a static number.
4. Defensive options: parry, dodge, or roll with impact (reduces damage).
5. Apply damage to HP, SDC, or MDC as appropriate.

### Other common mechanics
- **Saving throws:** Usually d20 vs a target number (magic, poison, horror factor, etc.), plus bonuses from attributes, OCC, or items.
- **Magic:** Powered by PPE (Potential Psychic Energy). Casting time and effects vary by spell level and tradition.
- **Psionics:** Powered by ISP (Inner Strength Points). A Mind Mage is not a Wizard with a different hat.
- **Perception / Horror Factor:** Common checks in many settings.
- **Advancement:** Level-based. Skills and certain combat bonuses improve; attributes usually do not.

Palladium is designed as a framework: the GM interprets intent, environment, and “what feels right” when the written rules do not cover a specific case. Different books add setting-specific options, but the d20 combat + percentile skills core stays consistent.

## Saving Throws (Common Types)

- Magic
- Psionics
- Poison/Toxins
- Horror Factor

All use roll-high d20 mechanics.

## Alignment System

Seven core alignments across Good, Selfish, and Evil:

- **Good:** Principled, Scrupulous
- **Selfish:** Unprincipled, Anarchist
- **Evil:** Miscreant, Aberrant, Diabolic

### Rifts Mega-Damage combat
*Rifts* uses the same d20 strike/parry/dodge framework, but it runs on a much higher damage scale. Energy weapons, power armor, robots, and many supernatural beings operate in **Mega-Damage (MD)** and **Mega-Damage Capacity (MDC)** instead of ordinary SDC and Hit Points.

#### The conversion
Official rule of thumb:

- **100 SDC (or Hit Points) ≈ 1 MDC**
- **1 point of Mega-Damage ≈ 100 SDC**

Round down when converting. A 450 SDC explosion therefore does 4 MD. A 2 MD blast against an object with 230 SDC destroys it completely.

In practice:

- Ordinary SDC weapons (most 20th/21st-century firearms, knives, fists) do **nothing** to MDC armor, vehicles, or creatures unless a single hit deals 100+ SDC. Even then the converted MD is usually tiny.
- An MD weapon hitting an unarmored human or ordinary car is catastrophic. One point of MD is enough to vaporize or completely wreck most SDC targets.

That is why unarmored characters in *Rifts* are extremely fragile, and why anyone who can afford it lives in MDC body armor, power armor, or behind force fields.

#### What has MDC
Typical MDC things include:

- High-tech military vehicles, tanks, aircraft, and Coalition war machines
- Body armor and power armor (Dead Boy armor, Glitter Boy, SAMAS, etc.)
- Most bionics and robot vehicles
- Force fields
- Many supernatural beings (dragons, demons, and in some cases vampires) whose bodies have been transformed by Rifts Earth’s high magic and ley-line energy

Ordinary humans and most D-Bees remain SDC/HP creatures. They need armor or magic to survive MD combat. When they step out of the armor, they are back to SDC rules.

#### How a fight actually runs
The sequence is the familiar Palladium one:

1. Roll initiative (d20 + bonuses).
2. Attacker rolls to strike (d20 + bonuses). A 5+ generally hits unless the target has a higher Armor Rating that must be beaten.
3. Defender may parry or dodge.
4. If the hit lands, roll damage and subtract it from the target’s MDC (armor first, then the person or vehicle underneath once the armor is gone).
5. Repeat for remaining attacks in the 15-second melee round.

A natural 20 is still a critical hit (typically double damage). Called shots, bursts, and other combat options work as usual.

**Parrying energy blasts** is harder than parrying a sword. You normally need an MDC weapon, shield, or special ability (Cyber-Knight psi-sword, certain magic items). The parrying object takes the damage; if it is destroyed, leftover damage can continue to the defender.

**Power punches / death blows** and similar special attacks still exist, but they interact differently against MDC targets (often requiring two actions and sometimes limited regeneration on supernatural beings).

#### Practical table feel
- Most *Rifts* firefights are MDC vs MDC: power armor vs power armor, Glitter Boy vs Coalition SAMAS, dragon vs robot.
- Collateral damage is huge. Buildings, vehicles, and bystanders that are only SDC get erased quickly.
- Gear matters a lot. A character’s personal SDC and Hit Points are almost irrelevant while they are inside intact MDC armor. Once the armor is breached or they are caught outside it, they are in serious trouble.
- Supernatural MDC creatures often have high MDC totals and regeneration, so fights against them can last longer than fights against machines.

The system is deliberately high-power. A single well-placed MD shot can end an SDC character instantly, which is why the game leans so heavily on armor, vehicles, magic barriers, and cover. Many groups use house rules to soften the 100:1 ratio or let heavy SDC explosives nibble at MDC targets; the official books keep the strict conversion.

### Palladium Fantasy magic
*Palladium Fantasy* treats magic as several different crafts that happen to share one fuel: **Potential Psychic Energy (P.P.E.)**. A Wizard, Diabolist, Summoner, Warlock, and Priest are not just different spell lists. They are different *methods* of shaping the same energy.

#### The fuel: P.P.E. and ley lines
Everyone has some P.P.E. Most people never learn to spend it. Practitioners of magic become living batteries: they store a personal reserve, recover it over time, and can also tap extra energy from:

- Ley lines (invisible rivers of mystic energy)
- Nexus points (where lines cross; effects and available energy often double)
- Willing participants in a ritual
- Blood sacrifice (dark, effective, and common in Summoner work)
- Places and times of power (solstices, eclipses, alignments)

P.P.E. is also why sacred stones, pyramids, temples, and “places of power” matter. They sit on the grid. A mage who can get to a nexus is suddenly much more dangerous than the same mage in a basement.

Psionics remain a separate track, powered by **I.S.P.**

#### Two big families of magic
Later source material (*Mysteries of Magic*) splits the world’s own thinking into two buckets:

**Magical Sciences** — studied, expandable, learned at any time  
Wizardry, Diabolism (wards), Summoning (circles), Alchemy.

**Mystic Arts** — granted or innate, grow with level and patronage  
Warlockry (elemental lords), Priestcraft, Witchcraft.

If the character can buy or discover a new spell, ward, or circle next week, it is a Science. If a god, demon, or elemental lord hands out the next package at level-up, it is an Art.

#### Wizard: invocation magic
The Wizard is the closest thing to a “normal mage.” Incantations and gestures spend P.P.E. to produce an effect. Second Edition advertises **300+ Wizard and Warlock spells**.

Table rhythm:

- Low- and mid-level spells (roughly levels 1–8) are fast: about **two castings per melee** (a 15-second combat round).
- Higher spells eat more time; some must be done as rituals measured in minutes.
- Any high-level invocation can usually be cast as a **ritual** instead: slower, more P.P.E. available from helpers, harder to save against.

Wizards also do scroll conversion and can learn new spells from books, teachers, or captured magic. They are the flexible generalists.

#### Warlock: elemental patronage
A Warlock does not study a giant spell list. They bind themselves to one or two **elemental forces** (Air, Earth, Fire, Water) and receive elemental magic from that relationship. They can speak with and, at higher levels, summon elementals of their life-sign. Their magic is narrower than a Wizard’s and more thematically locked. Think “priest of fire,” not “scholar of the library.”

#### Witch: a pact, not a curriculum
Witchcraft is granted magic from a supernatural patron. In the world’s own terms this is closer to priesthood than to Wizardry, and it carries social and moral weight. The class exists because the setting takes pacts, cults, and Old Ones seriously.

#### Priest and other clergy
Priests spend P.P.E. through faith. Their list and extra abilities depend on the god. They sit next to Warlocks and Witches in the “granted” column: power goes up with devotion and level, not with finding a new grimoire.

#### Diabolist: ward magic (the language of symbols)
A Diabolist does **not** throw Fireballs on demand. They write magic.

A **ward** is a phrase of mystic symbols. Each symbol is a word in a grammar:

- What the effect *is* (inflict, protect, alarm, drain, darkness…)
- What it *targets*
- How it *triggers*
- Whether it is area, permanent, or powered-up

The Diabolist crafts the physical marks (often in blood, bone, wax, wood, or rarer components), spends P.P.E. to energize them, and leaves a trap, alarm, blessing, or curse sitting on a door, a chest, a person, or a room. Activation by the creator is cheap and fast (about one melee action). Permanence and Power wards are expensive and use rare materials such as dragon or demon bone.

Play style: the Diabolist is a prep character. Given an hour and a bag of components they can turn a campsite into a minefield. In a sudden alley fight they are weaker than a Wizard unless they already have wards ready.

They also read old dwarf **runes** better than almost anyone else. That matters in setting history: rune magic helped wreck the world.

#### Summoner: circle magic and the battle of wills
Summoners draw **circles** — protection, summoning, and power — using symbols, colors, components, and a lot of P.P.E. They start knowing protection and summoning circles; **power circles** are rare knowledge they must find, steal, or bargain for.

Summoning is not “Summon Monster III, a badger appears.” The circle is drawn, the ritual is performed, the being appears *inside* the circle, and then there is a **battle of wills**. The creature is trapped, not automatically obedient. The Summoner must beat its resistance (classically, the being must roll over the Summoner’s M.E. three times out of five to resist submission). Fail the contest and you have a hostile demon in your living room. Succeed and you have a bound servant — until the terms, the circle, or your concentration fail.

Power circles are the big guns: All-Seeing, Command, Dimensional Rift, Power Matrix (linking circles), Teleport, Healing, Death, and so on. They take time and rare components. Many only work while the caster stays in the circle.

Diabolists know some circle work; Summoners know symbols but not full ward-crafting. Together they are Palladium’s answer to “magic as engineering and demonology,” not “magic as a daily spell slot.”

#### Alchemy, items, and leftover sciences
The **Alchemist** is usually an NPC shop of last resort: potions, fumes, powders, charms, crystals, curses, and the market for **rune weapons**. Rune weapons are intelligent, permanent, and tied to the lost high art of the Dwarves. After the Elf–Dwarf War and the later purge of magic, true rune-forging is almost gone. What remains is treasure, not a player factory.

Other odd traditions (Necromancer, Illusionist, lost Age of a Thousand Magicks styles) exist in sourcebooks and history. The core book already says many arts were destroyed or forgotten on purpose.

#### Saving throws and combat timing
Typical targets (2nd Edition reference sheets):

| Magic type        | Save vs magic |
|-------------------|---------------|
| Basic spell       | 12+           |
| Circles           | 13+ (protection circles often 16–20) |
| Ritual            | 16+           |
| Wards             | 14+           |
| Faerie magic      | 16+           |

Spell Strength and class bonuses raise the number the victim must beat. High-level ritual magic is meant to be hard to shrug off.

Armor can interfere with spellcasting (extra P.P.E. cost and random penalties to range, duration, or effect). That is a setting statement: men of magic and men of arms are different lives.

#### How it feels at the table
Palladium Fantasy magic is **asymmetric**.

- Wizard: flexible, combat-useful, still P.P.E. limited.
- Warlock / Priest / Witch: themed, granted, reliable within a lane.
- Diabolist: traps, locks, area denial, “I prepared this hallway.”
- Summoner: slow, expensive, terrifying payoff, constant risk of losing the leash.

The system wants planning, components, ley lines, and consequences. It does not want every caster to be a blaster with a fireball quota. The Elf–Dwarf War and the Millennium of Purification exist so that the *weird* magics — wards, circles, runes — feel like surviving fragments of something that once almost ended the world.

### Official Rifts magic conversion
Official conversion lives in **Rifts Conversion Book One** (revised). The Megaversal system is already the same, so you mostly change *scale*, not the method of casting.

#### The master rule: point-for-point
For spells, psionics, energy blasts, and magic weapons:

- **4D6 S.D.C. on a low-tech world → 4D6 M.D. on Rifts Earth**
- The reverse is also true if you take a *Rifts* mage to an S.D.C. world

Range, duration, saving throw, and P.P.E. cost stay the same unless a specific entry says otherwise. Armor of Ithan that gave S.D.C. protection now gives the same number as **M.D.C.** A fireball that did 6D6 S.D.C. does 6D6 M.D.

Mortal wizards do **not** become M.D.C. creatures. They stay Hit Point / S.D.C. meat. Only their *magic* jumps to mega-damage. Creatures of magic and supernatural beings (dragons, elementals, many demons) *do* become M.D.C. bodies when they arrive.

#### P.P.E. on Rifts Earth
Rifts Earth is soaked in ley-line energy, so imported casters usually get a bump:

- **Wizard / spell caster:** keep original P.P.E., then add **1D4×10+20** while on Rifts Earth
- **Beyond the Supernatural Arcanist:** triple the P.P.E. base and add extra per level
- Ley lines and nexuses work as in *Rifts*: extra draw, bigger effects at a nexus, danger of rifts

P.P.E. costs of Fantasy spells are generally **not** rewritten. The environment just makes it easier to *pay* those costs. Some secondary write-ups talk about doubling costs for “non-native” casters; the Conversion Book’s default is: cost unchanged, environment bonus instead.

#### Class-by-class (Palladium Fantasy → Rifts)

**Wizard**  
Treat as a Ley Line Walker–style invocation mage. Spell list ports over. Damage and barriers become M.D. / M.D.C. Skills and OCC abilities stay. They can learn *Rifts* spells if the GM allows crossover study.

**Warlock**  
Imported as its own OCC. Elemental spells convert point-for-point to M.D. Summoned elementals become the *Rifts*/Fantasy elemental stat blocks (often M.D.C. beings). Patron relationship is unchanged.

**Witch**  
Pact magic comes across. Familiar and granted powers scale to M.D. where they deal damage or grant protection. Still socially toxic in most Coalition-adjacent areas.

**Diabolist**  
Wards and symbols work, and they get *stronger* on Rifts Earth: duration and area of effect are generally **doubled**; damage is doubled **or** becomes mega-damage. Recognize-magic and save bonuses stay. They still do not cast Wizard spells.

**Summoner**  
Same story as the Diabolist: circle duration/area doubled; damage doubled or M.D. Battle of wills is unchanged. Specific power-circle notes from the original Conversion Book include:

- Command / Domination: duration, area, and range increased
- Force: becomes an M.D.C. field (example: 200 M.D.C. that renews)
- Death: can be set as S.D.C. or M.D. per level
- Dimensional Rift: still the expensive reality-tear (high P.P.E., e.g. 700)
- Teleport: range doubled
- Power Leech: can also cut ley line / nexus P.P.E. availability in half

The Summoner is the Fantasy cousin of the *Rifts* **Shifter**. Many tables just run them as related dimensional specialists rather than merging the classes.

#### Magic items and rune weapons
- Enchanted weapons that were “indestructible” stay that way
- Damage dice become M.D. on a 1:1 die swap (4D6 S.D.C. sword → 4D6 M.D.)
- Extra powers (spells in the blade, healing, intelligence) stay
- Item S.D.C., if you need a durability number, is often converted by combining or scaling into M.D.C.; some GMs use ×100 for inert objects, but living/magic items usually follow the book’s specific listing
- Classic vulnerabilities (iron vs faerie work, etc.) still apply, now against M.D. iron if that is what hits them

#### Going the other way (*Rifts* → S.D.C. world)
Same 1:1 flip: 4D6 M.D. becomes 4D6 S.D.C. The GM may drop a die if the result would erase every mundane threat. Magic weapons and spell effects otherwise keep their extras.

#### Mixing systems without breaking the table
Conversion Book advice: you *can* drop a Fantasy Diabolist into *Rifts* as alien D-Bee magic. You should **not** freely stack every Fantasy circle *and* every Ley Line Walker invocation *and* Techno-Wizard gadgets on one character unless you want the power curve to explode. Either:

1. Keep the imported OCC pure (foreign magic, rare and misunderstood), or  
2. Rebuild the character as the closest *Rifts* OCC (Walker, Shifter, Mystic, Warlock analogue) and treat old spells as flavor.

Full spell reprints and later World Book magic live in **Rifts Book of Magic**; Conversion Book One is the translation layer, not the complete spell encyclopedia.

**Practical summary:** spells and items change dice type, not identity. Mortal casters stay squishy. Wards and circles get a Rifts-Earth amplifier. P.P.E. usually gets a bonus from the ley-line-saturated planet rather than a new cost table.
