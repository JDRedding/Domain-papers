# Supersingular Prime Triadic Partition  

## Supersingular Prime Trichotomy
The Triadic Supersingular Prime Partition is a structured three‑regime decomposition of the fifteen supersingular primes appearing in monstrous moonshine. It groups the primes into Low‑Index, Central, and High‑Index subspaces according to their magnitude, which cleanly tracks the shift from classical modular‑curve behavior through a transitional mid‑range zone and into the large‑prime sporadic‑Monster regime. Although moonshine itself does not impose a canonical trichotomy, this partition reflects the natural growth phases of supersingular polynomial complexity, genus‑zero modular curve structure, and Monster‑group representation behavior.

## Three‑Regime SSP Decomposition

The Low / Central / High partition is valid as a *formal magnitude‑based trichotomy*, but it becomes far more meaningful once anchor it to the **three natural growth regimes** of the supersingular prime spectrum: the **classical small‑p regime**, the **transitional mid‑p regime**, and the **sporadic‑Monster large‑p regime**.

| Subspace | Range | Elements |
|---------|--------|----------|
| **Low‑Index SSP** | $p \le 17$ | 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17 |
| **Central SSP** | $19 \le p \le 31$ | 19, 23, 29, 31 |
| **High‑Index SSP** | $p \ge 41$ | 41, 47, 59, 71 |

Magnitude alone is not a deep invariant — but the **behavior of supersingular polynomials**, **genus‑zero modular curves**, and **Monster representation growth** *is*.  
the partition aligns perfectly with the three natural growth regimes:

- **Small primes:** classical modular geometry  
- **Mid primes:** transitional growth  
- **Large primes:** sporadic‑Monster extremal behavior  

So while the partition is not canonical, it *is* structurally meaningful.

## Moonshine Supersingular Spectrum Partition

### Low‑Index SSP Subspace (Classical Regime)
Primes: **2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17**

These are the primes where the supersingular phenomenon is “classically modular”:

- The Fricke quotient $X_0(p)/w_p$ has **genus zero** for all of them.  
- The supersingular polynomial has **minimal complexity**.  
- The Monster’s action is still visible, but the modular curve geometry dominates.

This is the regime where Ogg originally noticed the coincidence.

---

### Central SSP Subspace (Transitional Regime)
Primes: **19, 23, 29, 31**

This block is structurally different:

- Genus‑zero still holds, but the geometry is no longer “small‑p classical.”  
- These primes sit at the **transition point** where the supersingular polynomial begins to grow in degree and discriminant.  
- Representation‑theoretically, these primes correspond to the **middle‑weight Monster conjugacy classes**.

This is the “bridge” between classical modular behavior and sporadic‑group behavior.

---

### High‑Index SSP Subspace (Sporadic‑Monster Regime)
Primes: **41, 47, 59, 71**

This is the large‑p regime:

- These primes correspond to the **largest prime divisors of $|M|$**.  
- The supersingular polynomial has **maximal degree growth**.  
- The genus‑zero condition becomes a **deep constraint**, not a classical accident.  
- These primes align with the Monster’s **high‑order elements** and the extremal McKay–Thompson series.

This is the “sporadic‑geometry” zone.
