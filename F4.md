$F_4$ is a Lie group: it is simultaneously a group and a smooth differentiable manifold, with smooth group multiplication and inversion maps. It belongs to the five exceptional simple Lie groups—the non-classical families $G_2$, $F_4$, $E_6$, $E_7$, and $E_8$—that stand outside the infinite classical series $A_n$, $B_n$, $C_n$, and $D_n$.

$F_4$ is the unique (up to isomorphism) compact simply-connected simple Lie group of rank 4 and dimension 52. It admits a transparent geometric realization as the isometry group of the 16-dimensional octonionic projective plane and an algebraic realization as the automorphism group of the 27-dimensional exceptional Jordan algebra of $3\times 3$ Hermitian octonion matrices. These two viewpoints, linked by the Freudenthal–Tits magic square, account for most of its rich structure and explain its central place in Lie theory, Riemannian geometry, and exceptional mathematics.

### Key Properties
- **Lie group and Lie algebra**: $F_4$ is both a Lie group and its associated simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{f}_4$.
- **Rank**: 4 (the dimension of a Cartan subalgebra).
- **Dimension**: 52.
- **Compact real form**: Simply connected, with trivial outer automorphism group. The complexification is the unique simple complex Lie algebra of type $F_4$.
- **Fundamental representation**: 26-dimensional. This is the trace-free part of the natural action of $F_4$ on the 27-dimensional exceptional Jordan algebra (Albert algebra). The adjoint representation is the 52-dimensional one. The four fundamental representations have dimensions 52, 1274, 273, and 26 (corresponding to the nodes of the Dynkin diagram ordered so that the double arrow points from the second to the third node).

The dimensions of the smallest irreducible representations begin:
1, 26, 52, 273, 324, 1053 (twice), 1274, 2652, 4096, 8424, …  
(with several dimensions appearing for non-isomorphic representations).

There are three real forms of $F_4$: a compact one, a split one, and an intermediate form. These arise as the isometry groups of the three real forms of the Albert algebra.

### Geometric Interpretation
The compact real form of $F_4$ is the isometry group of the octonionic projective plane $\mathrm{OP}^2$ (also called the Cayley plane or Cayley–Moufang plane), a 16-dimensional Riemannian symmetric space of positive curvature. Equivalently,

$$
\mathrm{OP}^2 \simeq F_4 / \mathrm{Spin}(9).
$$

Points of $\mathrm{OP}^2$ may be identified with rank-1 idempotents in the Albert algebra. This geometric picture is central to understanding $F_4$ and arises systematically from the Freudenthal–Tits magic square, which organizes the exceptional Lie algebras in terms of the classical division algebras $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{H}$, and $\mathbb{O}$.

The same construction yields the other exceptional groups: the collineation group of $\mathrm{OP}^2$ is a real form of $E_6$, while $G_2$ appears as the automorphism group of the octonions themselves. Non-compact real forms of $F_4$ act as isometry groups of hyperbolic and split-octonionic planes.

### Algebraic Construction
$F_4$ (in its compact form) can be realized as the group of automorphisms of the exceptional Jordan algebra $J_3(\mathbb{O})$ (Albert algebra). This is the 27-dimensional real algebra of $3\times 3$ Hermitian matrices with octonion entries, equipped with the commutative Jordan product

$$
X \circ Y = \tfrac12 (XY + YX).
$$

The Lie algebra is then precisely the derivation algebra:

$$
\mathfrak{f}_4 = \mathrm{Der}\bigl(J_3(\mathbb{O})\bigr).
$$

The group preserves the cubic form (and related invariants) given by the determinant of these Hermitian matrices, or equivalently by combinations of traces ${Tr}(M)$, ${Tr}(M^2)$, and ${Tr}(M^3)$.

An alternative construction of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{f}_4$ proceeds by extending the 36-dimensional Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(9)$ by 16 additional generators that transform as a spinor of $\mathrm{Spin}(9)$, in a manner analogous to the construction of $\mathfrak{e}_8$ from $\mathfrak{so}(16)$. In older literature $F_4$ is sometimes denoted $E_4$.

Maximal-rank semisimple subalgebras of $\mathfrak{f}_4$ include $B_4$, $A_3\oplus A_1$, $2A_2$, $C_3\oplus A_1$, $D_4$, $C_2\oplus 2A_1$, and $4A_1$. The group $F_4$ contains $\mathrm{Spin}(9)$ as a maximal subgroup, corresponding to the stabilizer of a point in $\mathrm{OP}^2$. Other notable subgroups arise from the various real forms and from the magic-square constructions.

Just as the orthogonal group $O(n)$ preserves a quadratic form, $F_4$ preserves a set of three polynomials (in 27 variables, reducible to 26) associated with the Albert algebra; these define a 24-dimensional isoparametric hypersurface in the unit sphere with three distinct principal curvatures.

In mathematical physics $F_4$ appears in contexts involving exceptional geometry, supergravity, and certain grand-unified or string-theory constructions that exploit the octonions and the magic square. Its low-dimensional fundamental representation and its geometric realization on the Cayley plane make it especially accessible among the exceptional groups.

### Root System, Dynkin Diagram, and Weyl Group
The Dynkin diagram of $F_4$ consists of four nodes in a chain, with a double arrow between the second and third nodes pointing toward the shorter simple root. One standard choice of simple roots may be realized by the rows of a $4\times 4$ matrix whose entries involve the usual short and long roots of the system.

The root system contains 48 roots. These can be visualized as the vertices of a pair of dual 24-cells in four-dimensional space: 24 roots of the form $(\pm 1,\pm 1,0,0)$ (and permutations) together with the dual set consisting of 8 roots of the form $(\pm 1,0,0,0)$ and 16 roots of the form $(\pm\frac12,\pm\frac12,\pm\frac12,\pm\frac12)$. The associated root lattice is the $F_4$ lattice, a four-dimensional body-centered cubic lattice that coincides with the Hurwitz integer quaternions.

The Weyl (Coxeter) group $W(F_4)$ has order 1152 and is the full symmetry group of the 24-cell. It is a solvable group of minimal faithful degree 24.
