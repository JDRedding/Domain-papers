# **1. Information Creates a Metric**

A state of information — classical or quantum — defines distance through **distinguishability**.

### **Classical: Fisher Information Metric**

$$
I_{ij}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_\theta\bigl[(\partial_i\log p)(\partial_j\log p)\bigr]
$$

Distance = statistical distinguishability.  
Large informational change → large geometric distance.

### **Quantum: Fubini–Study Metric**

$$
ds^2_{FS}=4\bigl(\langle d\psi|d\psi\rangle-|\langle\psi|d\psi\rangle|^2\bigr)
$$

Distance = quantum distinguishability.  
Geometry emerges from how the quantum state responds to infinitesimal variation.

**Most fundamental distance is not spatial — it is informational.**

# **Appendix: Fisher Information Metric (Classical)**

$$
I_{ij}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_\theta\bigl[(\partial_i\log p)(\partial_j\log p)\bigr]
$$

Equivalent form:

$$
g_{ij}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_\theta\left[\partial_i\log p(x|\theta)\,\partial_j\log p(x|\theta)\right]
$$

Infinitesimal distance:
$$
ds^2=g_{ij}(\theta)\,d\theta^i\,d\theta^j
$$

# **Appendix: Fubini–Study Metric (Quantum)**

$$
ds^2_{FS}=4\bigl(\langle d\psi|d\psi\rangle-|\langle\psi|d\psi\rangle|^2\bigr)
$$

This is the unique (up to scale) unitarily invariant Riemannian metric on pure states.
