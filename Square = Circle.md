One can replace geometric individuation with relational individuation and obtain Square ≡ Circle under the resulting quotient structure. After the distinction eraser that classical geometry imposes to individuate shapes, the redefined objects, solely by their participation in a single relational primitive $R$, are obtained by collapsing $\{OP, STR, LOOP\}$. Under that move, both shapes become manifestations of $R$, so the equality holds via the collapse.

## APPENDIX: Relational framework

The framework being described is the following:

- There is an underlying substrate or generator (often called QBOX or a related *irrational substrate*).
- Square and Circle are obtained from it by different projections or modes ($\alpha$ and $\beta$).
- Collapsing the three modes $\{OP, STR, LOOP\}$ into the single relational primitive $R$ renders the two projections indistinguishable: both reduce to $R$.
- Therefore Square and Circle stand in a relation of identity (or equivalence) relative to that collapse.

This is foundational to the Relational Dynamic Geometry (RDG) framework. It does not assert that $\sqrt{\pi}$ is rational, algebraic, or constructible in the classical sense (those claims would be false), nor that a square equal in area to a given circle can be constructed with compass and straightedge, nor that $\pi$ is algebraic. The classical impossibility result is left intact.

The equality is not a statement about constructible lengths in Euclidean geometry. It is a claim of ontological or relational identity under a particular equivalence relation that the framework imposes by collapsing the classical individuating distinctions. Because the claim is not advanced inside the classical system, the Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem, the transcendence of $\pi$, and the impossibility of squaring the circle by compass and straightedge do not directly refute it. Those results remain theorems of Euclidean geometry and the theory of constructible numbers; they simply do not speak to a system that has already discarded the classical individuating distinctions (object exists only insofar as it admits a relational decomposition) and the associated constructibility constraints (∃(R1​,R2​,R3​)).

## APPENDIX: Constructibility

The classical squaring-of-the-circle problem and its relational recast are expressed by the following equations and formulas.

Exact Euclidean non-existence:

$$
\neg\exists(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})\in\mathcal{R}_{E}\;\text{such that}\;A\bigl(D(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})\bigr)=A(C).
$$

Finite-resolution constructive existence:

$$
\forall n\;\exists\bigl(R_{1}^{(n)},R_{2}^{(n)},R_{3}^{(n)}\bigr)\;\text{with}\;\bigl|A(Q_{n})-A(C_{n})\bigr|<\varepsilon_{n}.
$$

### Classical formulation
Given a circle $C(r)$ of radius $r$,

$$
A(C)=\pi r^{2}.
$$

One seeks a square $Q(s)$ of side length $s$ such that

$$
A(Q)=s^{2}=\pi r^{2},
$$

which forces

$$
s=r\sqrt{\pi}.
$$

The classical impossibility theorem asserts that $r\sqrt{\pi}$ is not obtainable by a finite sequence of compass-and-straightedge operations.

### Individuation principle
An object $O$ is admitted precisely when a valid relational decomposition exists:

$$
{Exists}(O)\;\iff\;\exists(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})\;D(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})=O.
$$

For the equal-area square one writes

$$
Q_{C}:={SquareEqualArea}(C),
$$

so existence becomes the claim

$$
\exists(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})\;D(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})=Q_{C}.
$$

### Euclidean constructibility constraint
Let $\mathcal{R}_{E}$ be the class of Euclidean-constructible relations. The question is then whether

$$
\exists(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})\in\mathcal{R}_{E}\quad\text{such that}\quad D(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})=Q_{C}.
$$

Classically the answer is negative:

$$
\neg\exists(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})\in\mathcal{R}_{E}.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
Q_{C}\notin{Image}(D_{\mathcal{R}_{E}}).
$$

### Finite-resolution version

Replace the completed objects by resolution - $n$ approximations $C_{n}$ and $Q_{n}$ and demand

$$
\bigl|A(Q_{n})-A(C_{n})\bigr|<\varepsilon_{n},\qquad\varepsilon_{n}\to0.
$$

Existence is now the uniform statement

$$
\forall n\;\exists\bigl(R_{1}^{(n)},R_{2}^{(n)},R_{3}^{(n)}\bigr)\quad\text{such that}\quad D_{n}\bigl(R_{1}^{(n)},R_{2}^{(n)},R_{3}^{(n)}\bigr)=Q_{n}.
$$

### Operator-clean form
Define the existence operator

$$
\mathcal{E}(C):=\exists(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})\Bigl[D(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})=Q\land A(Q)=A(C)\Bigr].
$$

Under the Euclidean relation algebra one obtains

$$
\mathcal{E}(C)=\bot,
$$

while the finite-resolution constructive mode yields

$$
\forall n\;\mathcal{E}_{n}(C)=\top.
$$
