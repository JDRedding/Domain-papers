## Relational Origins and Equilibrium Loci

Classical geometry begins with a point set and selects an origin from that set. Relations are subsequently measured relative to the chosen origin.

RDG reverses this order.

Let a primitive relational pair be

$$
+\Delta,
\qquad
-\Delta.
$$

The equilibrium state of the pair is

$$
O=
\frac{(+\Delta)+(-\Delta)}{2}.
$$

The symbol $$O$$ is not a primitive point. It is the zero-net-difference relation generated by the pair itself.

Thus the construction proceeds as

$$
(+\Delta,-\Delta)
\longrightarrow
O
\longrightarrow
\text{orientation axis}.
$$

The origin is therefore derived from the relation rather than assumed beforehand.

### Polar Example

In polar coordinates the classical origin is the point

$$
r=0.
$$

At that location angular distinctions collapse.

Relationally, one may instead consider a pair of opposite radial directions. Their balance defines the zero-difference state. The classical coordinate singularity is then reinterpreted as an equilibrium locus generated by the relational pair.

### Toroidal Example

For the relational toroidal chart the accessibility generator is

$$
D(E,S)=
\cosh E-
\cos S.
$$

The null-closure boundary satisfies

$$
D=0.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
E=0,
\qquad
S\in 2\pi\mathbb Z.
$$

This state represents the equilibrium of the efficiency–step relation. The boundary is therefore interpreted as a relational equilibrium locus rather than as a coordinate singularity inherited from an ambient Euclidean space.

### General Principle

In RDG,

$$
\text{relation}
\longrightarrow
\text{equilibrium}
\longrightarrow
\text{orientation}
\longrightarrow
\text{geometry}.
$$

Origins are not fundamental objects. They are equilibrium states generated by opposing relational increments.
