## 2d6
Core Math Equations 

**Sample space and basic probability**

Total outcomes:

$$
|\Omega| = 6 \cdot 6 = 36
$$

Event “sum equals $k$” (for $k = 2, 3, \dots, 12$):

Let $N(k)$ be the number of outcomes that sum to $k$. Then:

$$
P(\text{sum} = k) = \frac{N(k)}{36}
$$

and

$$
N(k) =
\begin{cases}
k - 1 & \text{for } 2 \leq k \leq 7 \\
13 - k & \text{for } 7 \leq k \leq 12
\end{cases}
$$

(Note: the two expressions agree at $k = 7$, where $N(7) = 6$.)

**Expected value (mean) of the sum**

Each die has expected value:

$$
E[\text{one die}] = \frac{1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6}{6} = 3.5
$$

For two independent dice:

$$
E[\text{sum}] = E[\text{die}_1] + E[\text{die}_2] = 3.5 + 3.5 = 7
$$

**Variance and standard deviation**

For one fair d6:

$$
\text{Var}(\text{one die}) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2
$$

where

$$
E[X^2] = \frac{1^2 + 2^2 + 3^2 + 4^2 + 5^2 + 6^2}{6} = \frac{91}{6}
$$

So

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Var}(\text{one die}) &= \frac{91}{6} - (3.5)^2 \\
&= \frac{91}{6} - \frac{49}{4} \\
&= \frac{35}{12}
\end{align*}
$$

For two independent dice, variances add:

$$
\text{Var}(\text{sum}) = 2 \cdot \frac{35}{12} = \frac{35}{6}
$$

Standard deviation:

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\text{Var}(\text{sum})} = \sqrt{\frac{35}{6}}
$$

(All formulas above are exact for fair, independent d6 dice.)

**Gaussian-like viewpoint for 2d6**

2d6 can be viewed as a small discrete distribution that already looks roughly Gaussian:

- Mean: $\mu = 7$
- Variance: $\sigma^2 = 35/6 \approx 5.833$
- Discrete PMF: $P(S = k) = N(k)/36$, symmetric about 7, with $N(k)$ as given earlier (triangular: 1,2,3,4,5,6,5,4,3,2,1)

It is *not* Gaussian, but it is unimodal, symmetric, and has the classic “bell-ish” shape of a discrete triangular distribution.

### Generalization to $n$ d6

Let $S_n$ be the sum of $n$ independent fair d6. Then:

- Support: $n \leq S_n \leq 6n$
- Mean: $\mu_n = 3.5n$
- Variance: $\sigma_n^2 = n \cdot \frac{35}{12}$
- The exact PMF is the coefficient of $x^k$ in $(x + x^2 + \cdots + x^6)^n / 6^n$, or equivalently the number of ways to roll sum $k$ with $n$ dice divided by $6^n$.

By the **Central Limit Theorem**, as $n$ grows,

$$
\frac{S_n - \mu_n}{\sigma_n} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,1)
$$

so the distribution of $S_n$ (after centering and scaling) converges to a normal distribution. Even for modest $n$ (around 5–10) the visual resemblance is already strong.

### Quick numerical summary

| $n$ | Mean | Std. dev. | Shape note |
|-------|------|-----------|------------|
| 1     | 3.5  | ≈1.71     | Flat (uniform) |
| 2     | 7    | ≈2.42     | Triangular / already roughly Gaussian |
| 3     | 10.5 | ≈2.96     | Noticeably bell-shaped |
| 5     | 17.5 | ≈3.82     | Close to normal |
| 10    | 35   | ≈5.40     | Visually almost indistinguishable from normal for most purposes |

The formulas listed for 2d6 are the $n=2$ case of this general picture. The same mean/variance additivity and the approach to normality hold for any $n$.
