# RPG Technology Levels
***Tech Levels***

RPG technology levels categorize societies and characters by their technological advancement, ranging from primitive societies to futuristic civilizations, and are key for world-building and rules consistency. RPG *technology levels* are structured tiers that define what tools, weapons, infrastructure, and scientific understanding exist in a game world. They’re a world-building backbone: each level determines what players can plausibly build, discover, or interact with.

Technology levels provide a structured way to understand human, societal, or hypothetical civilization progress from simple tools to interstellar energy manipulation, guiding both scientific studies and practical assessments of technological capacity. Below is a deeper breakdown of the major tech-level frameworks used across RPGs — from classic tabletop to modern world-engine design — and how they map into cognition.

## Practical Applications

Understanding technology levels is useful for:

- **Comparative anthropology:** Studying the development of early societies.  
- **Energy policy and sustainability:** Estimating current civilization energy consumption relative to potential capabilities.  
- **Science fiction and futurism:** Conceptualizing human or alien technologies and societal structures.  
- **Technology adoption analysis:** Comparing regions or organizations based on digital access, automation, and infrastructure development.  

### Game Applications

- **Rules Restrictions:** TLs define what weapons, armor, vehicles, and technologies are available to characters. For example, a TL3 campaign forbids firearms beyond simple handguns or early cannons.  
- **World-Building:** TLs help create immersive worlds with consistent development. A TL5 city might have trains and steam machines but lacks cybernetics.  
- **Cross-Campaign Interaction:** Technology levels influence trade, military strategies, and social development. Adventures across multiple TLs often require adaptation or special rules for offsets.

Technology levels categorize the advancement of societies or systems based on their scientific knowledge, energy use, and capability to manipulate their environment.

### Historical and Anthropological Technology Levels

Historically, human technologies are often categorized into eras that reflect dominant tools and energy sources:

- **Stone Age:** Primarily stone tools, no metal use, basic hunting and gathering methods.  
- **Bronze Age:** Use of bronze tools and weapons, early metallurgy, and organized settlements.  
- **Iron Age:** Iron tools and weapons, expanded agriculture, and civilizations with complex trade networks.  

These levels give a framework to understand human social and technological development from early societies through the pre-industrial era.

### Industrial and Modern Technology Levels

Modern frameworks categorize technology based on energy use, industrialization, and scientific capacity:

- **Agricultural / Pre-Industrial Level:** Basic machinery, reliance on manual labor, animal power, limited fossil fuel use.  
- **Industrial Level:** Steam engine, mechanized production, widespread use of coal and later electricity, urbanization, and mass manufacturing.  
- **Post-Industrial Level:** Digital technology, advanced electronics, computing, telecommunications, and service-oriented economies.  

### Energy-Based Technology Levels — The Kardashev Scale

One of the more comprehensive methods to classify technology is the Kardashev Scale, which assesses civilizations by energy-harnessing capacity:

- **Type I Civilization:** Capable of utilizing all energy available on its home planet.  
- **Type II Civilization:** Harnesses the total energy of its star (for example, via Dyson spheres).  
- **Type III Civilization:** Controls energy on the scale of its entire galaxy.  

These levels are theoretical but provide insight into the potential trajectory of technological advancement beyond Earth.

### Variations and Systems

- Some RPGs, like GURPS, assign numeric TLs from 0 to 15+ to cover historical, contemporary, and far-future settings.  
- Fantasy RPGs often mix TLs with magic systems, where magic may substitute for certain technological capabilities.  
- Post-apocalyptic and science-fantasy campaigns may have hybrid TL settings, e.g., surviving medieval tribes coexisting with remnants of TL8 technology.

In summary, RPG technology levels provide a structured way to define what tools, industries, and abilities exist in a campaign. They are vital for consistency, creativity, and the balance of gameplay mechanics across different eras or worlds.

## Core Idea: Tech Levels = Admissibility Bands

A tech level is really an **admissibility band** inside a world’s action space. It constrains:

- What materials exist  
- What energy sources exist  
- What manufacturing processes exist  
- What communication systems exist  
- What computational/logical systems exist  
- What transportation modes exist  
- What medical/biological interventions exist  

In relational terms:  
**TL = a Q□-slice over the Structure layer**, defining what is *legal*, *buildable*, or *discoverable*.

## Advanced: Tech Levels as Geometry

In a relational framework, tech levels are not merely “tiers” — they are **geometry modes**.

- **TL1–4:** Local geometry (terrain, weather, bodies)  
- **TL5–7:** Regional geometry (states, trade, infrastructure)  
- **TL8–9:** Network geometry (information, computation, cyber-space)

This is why TL jumps feel dramatic: each level changes the *shape* of the world.

## Overview of RPG Technology Levels

RPG technology levels (TLs) provide a way to define what equipment, weapons, vehicles, and general infrastructure are available in a game setting. They help gamemasters maintain internal consistency and balance, allowing campaigns to range from medieval fantasy to space-faring futurism.

### Common Technology Level Classification

**TL0 – Stone Age / Primitive**  
Societies rely on stone tools, basic fire, and simple hunting/gathering techniques. No metallurgy is present, and weapons are primitive (clubs, spears, arrows). Agriculture may be minimal or nonexistent.

**TL1 – Bronze / Early Agricultural**  
Copper/bronze tools and weapons appear, along with early farming techniques. Settlements and rudimentary trade are established. Basic animal domestication is common.

**TL2 – Iron Age**  
Iron weapons and tools dominate. Agriculture and urban centers develop further. Writing systems, fortified cities, and organized militaries emerge.

**TL3 – Medieval / Low-Fantasy**  
Steel weapons, castles, and siege technology are common. Agriculture supports larger populations, guilds and feudal hierarchies exist, and horseback riding is militarily significant.

**TL4 – Renaissance / Industrial Proto-Level**  
Firearms start to appear, gunpowder use begins, basic navigation and scientific inquiry spread. Printing presses allow mass information distribution, and trade expands.

**TL5 – Industrial Age**  
Steam-powered machinery, trains, and large-scale factories mark this level. Telegraph and early electricity exist. Societies begin to experience urbanization and mass production.

**TL6 – Early Modern / Modern**  
Internal combustion engines, telephones, basic aviation. Larger-scale industrialization, electricity widespread, chemical and basic medical technologies advance.

**TL7 – Contemporary / Late 20th Century**  
Nuclear power, advanced aviation, space travel capability, computers, and electronics. Mass communication and modern medicine are common.

**TL8 – Near-Future or High-Tech**  
Advanced robotics, AI, personal energy weapons, interplanetary travel. Cybernetics, high-speed computers, and early fusion energy are available.

**TL9+ – Futuristic / Science Fiction**  
Societies have access to interstellar travel, energy shields, nanotechnology, and nearly limitless computing power. TL9 and above often include alien technology or highly speculative innovations.

### The Standard RPG Tech Ladder

This is the most widely used 10-step ladder (GURPS, Traveller, HERO, etc.), expanded with structural meaning so it fits a worldview.

1. **Stone Age**  
   Flint tools, fire, simple shelters. No metallurgy. TL1 societies are pure **SID-Structure** worlds: survival, territory, resource flux.

2. **Bronze Age**  
   Bronze weapons, early cities, irrigation. Writing emerges → information persistence. First **Interaction-layer** complexity: trade networks, proto-bureaucracy.

3. **Iron Age**  
   Iron forging, large armies, roads. Coinage → abstract resource tokens. TL3 introduces **Dynamics-layer** scaling: empires, logistics, supply chains.

4. **Medieval**  
   Steel, castles, feudal systems. Wind/water mills → early automation. Magic systems often mapped here in fantasy RPGs.

5. **Renaissance**  
   Printing press, gunpowder, navigation. Scientific method emerges. TL5 is the birth of **formalized knowledge operators**.

6. **Industrial**  
   Steam engines, railroads, factories. Mass production → exponential resource flux. TL6 worlds introduce **Power-layer** scaling (PED-Power).

7. **Early Modern**  
   Electricity, telegraph, internal combustion. Early medicine, chemistry. TL7 is the first **global-interaction** tech level.

8. **Information Age**  
   Computers, internet, satellites. Cybernetics, genetic engineering. TL8 worlds have **computational SID overlays** — simulation inside simulation.

9. **Near-Future**  
   Fusion prototypes, AI, nanotech. Interplanetary travel. TL9 is where RPGs introduce **soft-singularity mechanics**.

10. **Far-Future**  
    FTL travel, matter manipulation, post-biological life. TL10+ is pure **geometry**: physics becomes editable.

## How Tech Levels Interact With RPG Systems

Tech levels aren’t just flavor — they shape the entire engine.

### 1. Character Creation

Tech level defines available skills, backgrounds, equipment, and social roles. A TL8 hacker is impossible in TL3 because the **Q□-admissibility** forbids it.

#### 2. Economy & Resource Flux

Higher TLs introduce energy abundance, automation, high-value materials, and digital currencies. This maps directly to **MFE flux pools**.

#### 3. Combat & Conflict

Weapons scale with TL:

- TL3 → melee, bows  
- TL5 → firearms  
- TL8 → drones, cyberwarfare  
- TL10 → relativistic weapons  

Each TL adds new **interaction geometries**.

#### 4. Exploration & World Scale

Tech level determines travel speed, map size, communication range, and survival difficulty. TL is a **world-scale multiplier**.
