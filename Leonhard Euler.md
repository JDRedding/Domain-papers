## Euler: Bio 

Leonhard Euler (1707–1783) was a Swiss mathematician whose output is unmatched: more than 800 papers and books, many of which founded entire fields. Born in Basel and educated under Johann Bernoulli, Euler quickly became known for his ability to see structure where others saw scattered ideas. He spent major parts of his career at the St. Petersburg Academy and later at the Berlin Academy, producing groundbreaking work even after losing his eyesight.

Euler’s impact can’t be captured by a single formula, but his famous identity  

$$
e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0
$$ 

is a perfect symbol of his style: connecting ideas that once seemed unrelated. Born in 1707, he shaped graph theory, number theory, calculus, and complex analysis. His deeper legacy is the way he revealed structure across mathematics — a habit that still guides the field today.

Euler’s influence spans nearly every major branch of mathematics:

- He formalized **analysis**, giving us the notation $f(x)$, $e$, $i$, and much of the differential calculus language we still use.
- He advanced **number theory**, introducing arithmetic functions like the **Euler totient** $\varphi(n)$ and deep results on primes.
- He founded **graph theory** with the solution to the Königsberg bridges problem.
- He connected geometry and topology through the **polyhedron formula** $V - E + F = 2$.
- He explored infinite series, integrals, special functions, and the zeta function long before modern analysis existed.

Even in blindness, Euler dictated papers at a pace that astonished his contemporaries. His clarity, productivity, and ability to unify ideas made him a central architect of modern mathematics.

---

### **Euler’s formula**  
The identity $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$ ties together five fundamental constants — $e, i, \pi, 1, 0$. It’s often called the most beautiful equation in mathematics because it fuses exponential growth, rotation, and complex numbers into one compact statement.

### **Euler–Maclaurin formula**  
A bridge between discrete sums and continuous integrals. It shows how to approximate sums using integrals plus correction terms involving **Bernoulli numbers**. This formula underlies numerical analysis and asymptotic expansions.

### **Euler’s constant $ \gamma $**  
Defined as the limiting difference between harmonic numbers and the natural logarithm. It appears in integrals, prime number theory, and special functions — still mysterious, still not known to be rational or irrational.

### **Graph theory**  
Euler’s solution to the Königsberg bridges problem introduced the idea of treating connections as **vertices and edges**, launching graph theory.

### **Polyhedra (Euler characteristic)**  
The formula $V - E + F = 2$ links geometry and topology. It’s the ancestor of the modern Euler characteristic used throughout algebraic topology.

### **Number theory**  
Euler developed the totient function $\varphi(n)$, proved results on primes, and explored multiplicative functions. Much of elementary number theory traces back to him.

### **Infinitesimal calculus**  
Euler standardized notation, clarified derivatives, and made calculus computationally powerful. His influence is visible in every modern textbook.

### **Series for $e$**  
The infinite expansion  

$$
e = 1 + \frac{1}{1!} + \frac{1}{2!} + \frac{1}{3!} + \cdots
$$ 

is one of many series Euler derived, showing his mastery of infinite processes.
