# **The Freudenthal–Tits Magic Square**
*An RDG Interpretive Perspective*

**Abstract**

The Freudenthal–Tits magic square furnishes a unified construction of the exceptional Lie algebras through pairwise combination of the four real normed division algebras $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{H}$, and $\mathbb{O}$. Conventionally treated as an algebraic mechanism relating composition algebras, Jordan algebras, and exceptional Lie theory, the square admits a complementary interpretive reading within Relational Dynamic Geometry (RDG). Under this reading the division algebras are identified with successive modes of relational closure, the entries of the square with emergent symmetry structures generated by pairwise interaction, and the exceptional series with the stabilizing symmetries of maximally rich admissible configurations. The account is offered as an interpretive framework rather than a derivation of the classical construction; its purpose is to exhibit structural parallels between the magic square and RDG principles of relational completion, emergent symmetry, and admissibility.

**1. Introduction**

The Freudenthal–Tits magic square is a $4\times 4$ array of Lie algebras indexed by pairs of real normed division algebras. It generates the full exceptional series

$$
\mathfrak{f}_4,\quad \mathfrak{e}_6,\quad \mathfrak{e}_7,\quad \mathfrak{e}_8
$$

while simultaneously recovering several classical families. The construction is closely tied to the octonions and occupies a central place in the theory of exceptional algebraic structures.

The square originated in independent work of Hans Freudenthal and Jacques Tits in the late 1950s. A notable feature is that the original Tits construction treats its two inputs asymmetrically, yet the resulting array is symmetric; this unexpected symmetry is the source of the designation “magic square.”

In schematic form the classical reading may be written

$$
\text{Division algebra }A\;\times\;\text{Division algebra }B\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Lie algebra }L(A,B).
$$

The present article develops an alternative interpretive framework based on Relational Dynamic Geometry, examining the relational principles that may underlie the emergence of the observed symmetry structures.

**2. Algebraic Structure of the Magic Square**

The rows and columns are indexed by the four real normed division algebras

$$
\mathbb{R},\quad \mathbb{C},\quad \mathbb{H},\quad \mathbb{O}.
$$

The compact real form of the square is

| $A\setminus B$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $\mathbb{C}$ | $\mathbb{H}$ | $\mathbb{O}$ |
|------------------|----------------|----------------|----------------|----------------|
| $\mathbb{R}$   | $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ | $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ | $\mathfrak{sp}(3)$ | $\mathfrak{f}_4$ |
| $\mathbb{C}$   | $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ | $\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)$ | $\mathfrak{su}(6)$ | $\mathfrak{e}_6$ |
| $\mathbb{H}$   | $\mathfrak{sp}(3)$ | $\mathfrak{su}(6)$ | $\mathfrak{so}(12)$ | $\mathfrak{e}_7$ |
| $\mathbb{O}$   | $\mathfrak{f}_4$ | $\mathfrak{e}_6$ | $\mathfrak{e}_7$ | $\mathfrak{e}_8$ |

The final row and column contain the exceptional algebras, which appear precisely when the octonions enter the construction. The algebra $\mathfrak{g}_2$, the derivation algebra of the octonions, is absent from the square itself despite its intimate connection with the underlying algebraic machinery.

**3. Relational Closure as an Interpretive Framework**

Within the RDG framework the division algebras are treated as progressively richer modes of relational closure. The sequence

$$
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{O}
$$

corresponds to the dimensional sequence $1,2,4,8$. Rather than being read solely as an increase in dimension, the progression is interpreted as an expansion of admissible relational structure:
- $\mathbb{R}$: ordered scalar relation,
- $\mathbb{C}$: rotational dual relation,
- $\mathbb{H}$: non-commutative orientation relation,
- $\mathbb{O}$: non-associative relational freedom.

The entries of the square then correspond to pairwise interactions between these closure modes. Each cell is regarded as an emergent symmetry structure generated by the coupling of two modes:

$$
\text{Entry}(i,j)=\text{symmetry generated by interaction of closure modes }i\text{ and }j.
$$

In this reading the square functions as a relational product space rather than a mere classification table.

**4. Emergent Symmetry and Relational Completion**

A defining feature of the Freudenthal–Tits construction is that symmetry appears after the construction rather than being imposed beforehand. Although the Tits recipe treats its inputs asymmetrically, the resulting Lie algebra satisfies

$$
L(A,B)=L(B,A).
$$

From the RDG standpoint this recovered symmetry indicates that the final algebra depends on the completed relational configuration rather than on the particular generative path. Schematically,

$$
\text{projection asymmetry}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{relational completion}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{recovered dual symmetry}.
$$

The symmetry is therefore interpreted as an emergent property of relational closure.

**5. The Exceptional Series as Boundary Stabilization**

The exceptional algebras

$$
\mathfrak{f}_4,\quad \mathfrak{e}_6,\quad \mathfrak{e}_7,\quad \mathfrak{e}_8
$$

appear whenever the octonions participate. Classically the octonions are exceptional by virtue of non-associativity. The RDG reading reframes $\mathbb{O}$ as the boundary of admissible composition structures: it permits relational configurations that cannot be reduced to the closure modes represented by $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, or $\mathbb{H}$. The exceptional Lie algebras then arise as the symmetry structures required to stabilize these maximally rich configurations, and may be regarded as boundary-preserving symmetries associated with maximal admissible relational freedom.

**6. Pairwise Completion and the Role of $\mathfrak{g}_2$**

The magic square systematically evaluates every pairing among the four division-algebra modes and therefore functions as a complete interaction table. Within RDG this process corresponds to a relational completion procedure:
1. generation of all admissible pairings,
2. evaluation of the resulting relational structures,
3. extraction of the stabilizing symmetry associated with each pairing.

The absence of $\mathfrak{g}_2$ from the square itself is thereby clarified. As the automorphism algebra of the octonions, $\mathfrak{g}_2$ belongs to the generative substrate rather than to the set of generated outputs. The distinction may be expressed as

$$
\mathfrak{g}_2=\text{generator symmetry},
$$

while

$$
\mathfrak{f}_4,\;\mathfrak{e}_6,\;\mathfrak{e}_7,\;\mathfrak{e}_8=\text{generated symmetries}.
$$

In RDG terminology, $\mathfrak{g}_2$ is part of the relational substrate; the exceptional series arises as emergent relational geometry.

**7. SID Interpretation**

In terms of the SID triad

$$
S=\text{Structure},\qquad I=\text{Interaction},\qquad D=\text{Dynamics},
$$

the magic square is represented by the cascade

$$
S\times S\;\longrightarrow\;I\;\longrightarrow\;D,
$$

where
- Structure comprises the division-algebra architectures $(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{O})$,
- Interaction comprises the pairwise couplings $(A,B)$,
- Dynamics comprises the emergent Lie algebra $L(A,B)$.

The Lie algebra appearing in each cell is interpreted as the stable dynamical closure of the corresponding interaction.

**8. Q-Admissibility Interpretation**

A further RDG–MFE–Q perspective addresses the uniqueness of the four normed division algebras. Classically this uniqueness is furnished by Hurwitz’s theorem. Under the RDG reading, $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{H}$, and $\mathbb{O}$ are precisely the closure modes that survive Q-admissibility constraints. The square then appears as a relational admissibility lattice in which each entry identifies the largest symmetry preserving a permissible interaction:

$$
Q(A,B)=\text{largest symmetry preserving the admissible interaction between }A\text{ and }B.
$$

**9. Conclusion**

From the conventional standpoint the Freudenthal–Tits magic square is an algebraic mechanism linking division algebras, Jordan algebras, and exceptional Lie groups. From the RDG standpoint it may be viewed as a relational completion lattice in which pairwise closure modes generate emergent symmetry geometries. Within this interpretive framework:
- the rows and columns represent fundamental closure modes,
- the cells represent dynamically stabilized interactions,
- $\mathfrak{g}_2$ functions as part of the generative substrate,
- the exceptional series appears as boundary-stabilizing symmetry, and
- the square as a whole maps admissible relational configurations into emergent geometric structure.

The principal correspondence between the magic square and RDG is not the presence of any single exceptional algebra, including $\mathfrak{e}_8$, but the broader principle that completed interactions can generate symmetries not fully visible within either participating structure alone. This emergence of symmetry through relational completion constitutes the primary point of contact between the Freudenthal–Tits construction and the RDG framework.
