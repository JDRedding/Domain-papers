# Domain-papers
The domain‑papers repository is a ten‑domain container that mirrors an decimal System’s top‑level classes. Each folder represents a knowledge domain, just the territory in which papers, notes, mappings, and analyses will live.

This repository is intended as a **living research library**. Papers may remain here indefinitely as standalone works, or they may later be extracted into dedicated repositories as they mature into larger research programs or software projects.

### Intended Use  
This library is meant for active researchers, collaborators, and future contributors who want to engage with the evolving body of work. It provides:

- A **centralized home** for ongoing research.  
- A **transparent view** into the development of ideas over time.  
- A **flexible structure** that supports both rapid experimentation and long‑term scholarly development.  
- A **reference archive** for the intellectual lineage of major projects.

### Domains

#### 000–099 Knowledge
General works, encyclopedias, information theory, meta‑knowledge, classification theory, and any papers about knowledge systems themselves.

#### 100–199 Philosophy
Logic, metaphysics, epistemology, ethics, philosophical systems, and conceptual frameworks.

#### 200–299 Religion
Comparative religion, theology, myth, ritual systems, and cultural religious structures.

#### 300–399 Social Sciences
Sociology, economics, political science, law, public policy, anthropology, and human social behavior.

#### 400–499 Language
Linguistics, grammar, semantics, language families, writing systems, and communication theory.

#### 500–599 Science
Mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, earth sciences — the natural sciences in their pure form.

#### 600–699 Technology
Engineering, applied sciences, medicine, agriculture, manufacturing, and practical technological systems.

#### 700–799 Arts
Visual arts, music, architecture, design, performing arts, and aesthetic theory.

#### 800–899 Literature
Poetry, drama, fiction, literary criticism, and textual analysis across cultures.

#### 900–999 History
World history, geography, biography, archaeology, and civilizational timelines.

### Workflow Philosophy  
This repository is designed to support the full arc of research activity:

- **Idea capture** — Quick notes, conceptual sketches, and exploratory fragments.  
- **Iterative refinement** — Drafts evolve through multiple passes, accumulating structure and clarity.  
- **Synthesis and integration** — Related works may be merged, reorganized, or reframed as broader theories or systems.  
- **Extraction and expansion** — Mature works may be split into their own repositories, where they become formal papers, frameworks, or software implementations.  
- **Archival continuity** — Even after extraction, early versions and developmental notes remain here as part of the historical record of the research process.

### Core Principles  
- **Ongoing evolution** — The repository is never “finished.” It expands organically as new insights emerge, old ideas are revisited, and connections between works become clearer.  
- **Low‑friction capture** — Early thoughts, fragments, and exploratory sketches are welcome. The library prioritizes capturing ideas quickly rather than polishing them prematurely.  
- **Heterogeneous content** — The repository may contain papers, conceptual frameworks, mathematical notes, diagrams, prototype code, datasets, or reflective commentary. Each piece is allowed to exist at its own level of completeness.  
- **Incubation space** — Some works will mature into larger initiatives. Others will remain as seeds, references, or intellectual waypoints. The library supports both outcomes equally.  
- **Cross‑linking and synthesis** — As themes emerge across documents, they may be reorganized, cross‑referenced, or grouped into higher‑level structures. The library grows not only in size but in internal coherence.
