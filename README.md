## Domain-Papers  
**005.1 Software documentation**

The **Domain-Papers** repository is a ten-domain container that mirrors the top-level classes of a decimal classification system. Each folder represents a distinct knowledge territory—a conceptual region where papers, notes, mappings, and analyses can accumulate, evolve, and cross-pollinate. The structure is intentionally broad and durable. It provides a stable taxonomy for organizing research across disciplines while remaining flexible enough to support emergent, interdisciplinary work.

---

## What is Domain-Papers?

Domain-Papers is the primary research archive behind most of the larger projects on this GitHub account.

Many ideas begin here as notes, sketches, mathematical explorations, or conceptual frameworks. As projects mature, they may be extracted into dedicated repositories while retaining their developmental history within Domain-Papers.

In this sense, Domain-Papers functions simultaneously as:

- A research notebook
- A knowledge library
- A theory incubator
- A historical archive

This repository operates as a **living research library**. Works may remain here indefinitely as standalone artifacts, or they may later be extracted into dedicated repositories as they mature into larger research programs, formal papers, or software projects. The library is designed to support both incubation and expansion without forcing premature consolidation.

## Domain Structure

Each domain corresponds to a 100-range class, forming a complete top-level taxonomy. This structure mirrors classical decimal classification systems while remaining adaptable to modern interdisciplinary research.

### 000–099 Knowledge  
General works, encyclopedias, information theory, meta-knowledge, classification theory, and papers about knowledge systems themselves.  
Key themes: **classification theory**, **meta-knowledge**.

### 100–199 Philosophy  
Logic, metaphysics, epistemology, ethics, philosophical systems, and conceptual frameworks.  
Key themes: **epistemology**, **conceptual frameworks**.

### 200–299 Religion  
Comparative religion, theology, myth, ritual systems, and cultural religious structures.  
Key themes: **comparative religion**, **myth systems**.

### 300–399 Social Sciences  
Sociology, economics, political science, law, public policy, anthropology, and human social behavior.  
Key themes: **social systems**, **anthropology**.

### 400–499 Language  
Linguistics, grammar, semantics, language families, writing systems, and communication theory.  
Key themes: **linguistics**, **semantics**.

### 500–599 Science  
Mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, earth sciences — the natural sciences in their pure form.  
Key themes: **mathematics**, **physics**.

### 600–699 Technology  
Engineering, applied sciences, medicine, agriculture, manufacturing, and practical technological systems.  
Key themes: **engineering**, **applied sciences**.

### 700–799 Arts  
Visual arts, music, architecture, design, performing arts, and aesthetic theory.  
Key themes: **aesthetic theory**, **architecture**.

Poetry, drama, fiction, literary criticism, and textual analysis across cultures.

### 900–999 History  
World history, geography, biography, archaeology, and civilizational timelines.  
Key themes: **civilizational timelines**, **archaeology**.

### Classification as Metadata

Domain-Papers uses a decimal classification structure inspired by the Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC) system. The classification number serves not only as a location within the repository but also as descriptive metadata. Each additional decimal place increases specificity while preserving the document's broader context within the overall knowledge structure. For example:

000 → Computer science, information & general works
001 → Knowledge
004 → Data processing & computer science
005 → Computer programming, programs & data

500 → Science
510 → Mathematics
516 → Geometry
523 → Cosmology
530 → Physics

The classification hierarchy allows documents to be organized, discovered, and related through a shared numerical framework rather than relying solely on folders or keyword tags. As the repository grows, additional decimal subdivisions may be introduced to support new research areas while maintaining continuity with the broader taxonomy.

### Local Extensions

While the repository is broadly inspired by the Dewey Decimal Classification system, selected unassigned or lightly used ranges may be repurposed to accommodate subjects relevant to the research program. These extensions remain compatible with the broader domain structure while providing room for new classifications not represented in the standard schedules.

## Workflow Philosophy

This repository is designed to support the full arc of research activity:

- **Idea capture** — quick notes, conceptual sketches, and exploratory fragments  
- **Iterative refinement** — drafts evolve through multiple passes, accumulating structure and clarity  
- **Synthesis and integration** — related works may be merged, reorganized, or reframed as broader theories or systems  
- **Extraction and expansion** — mature works may be split into their own repositories, where they become formal papers, frameworks, or software implementations  
- **Archival continuity** — even after extraction, early versions and developmental notes remain here as part of the historical record of the research process

## Core Principles

- **Ongoing evolution** — The repository is never “finished.” It expands organically as new insights emerge, old ideas are revisited, and connections between works become clearer.  
- **Low-friction capture** — Early thoughts, fragments, and exploratory sketches are welcome. The library prioritizes capturing ideas quickly rather than polishing them prematurely.  
- **Heterogeneous content** — Papers, conceptual frameworks, mathematical notes, diagrams, prototype code, datasets, and reflective commentary all coexist.  
- **Incubation space** — Some works will mature into larger initiatives; others will remain as seeds or intellectual waypoints.  
- **Cross-linking and synthesis** — As themes emerge across documents, they may be reorganized, cross-referenced, or grouped into higher-level structures.
