# The Bible

**The Bible** is the collection of sacred texts central to Christianity. It comprises the Old and New Testaments and includes religious teaching, history, prophecy, poetry, and moral guidance.

## Overview

The Bible is divided into two main parts: the Old Testament and the New Testament. The Old Testament, shared with Judaism, contains writings that predate Jesus Christ and includes historical narrative, law, wisdom literature, and prophecy. The New Testament focuses on the life and teachings of Jesus Christ, the history of the early Church, and theological writings.

## Structure

### Old Testament

In the Protestant canon the Old Testament typically contains 39 books, including:

- **The Pentateuch (Torah):** Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy—foundational law and the early history of Israel
- **Historical books:** Joshua, Judges, Samuel, Kings, and related works, recounting Israel’s history
- **Wisdom and poetry:** Psalms, Proverbs, Job, Ecclesiastes, and the Song of Solomon—reflections on faith, morality, and human experience
- **Prophetic books:** Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and the twelve minor prophets—messages of warning and hope

### New Testament

The New Testament contains 27 books:

- **Gospels:** Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John—accounts of Jesus’ life, teachings, death, and resurrection
- **Acts of the Apostles:** the history of early Christianity and the mission of the apostles
- **Epistles:** letters by Paul and other apostles on doctrine, ethics, and church life
- **Apocalyptic literature:** Revelation—a prophetic vision of divine judgment and ultimate triumph

## Authorship and Language

The Bible was written over roughly 1,000–1,500 years by many authors, including prophets, kings, scribes, and apostles. Its primary languages are Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek. Over the centuries it has been translated into numerous languages; among the most widely read English versions are the King James Version and the New International Version.

## Themes and Significance

The Bible treats central themes such as faith, morality, redemption, divine justice, and human purpose. It guides religious belief, ethical conduct, and spiritual reflection. Beyond its religious role, it has had a vast cultural influence on literature, art, law, and philosophy.

## Interpretation

Readings differ across denominations and traditions. Some interpret the text literally; others emphasize symbolism or historical context. Biblical scholarship studies literary form, historical setting, and comparisons with other ancient texts in order to clarify what the Bible meant for its first audiences and what it can mean today.

In short, the Bible is both a religious and a historical collection of writings. It stands at the center of Christian faith and offers spiritual guidance, ethical teaching, and a window onto ancient cultures and theology.
