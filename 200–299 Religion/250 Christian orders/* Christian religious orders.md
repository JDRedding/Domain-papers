# Christian religious orders
- 250 Christian orders

**Christian religious orders** are communities of men or women who live under religious vows, follow a particular rule, and dedicate their lives to prayer, service, and spiritual growth. Religious orders have shaped society for more than a millennium by preserving learning, founding hospitals, schools, and missions, and offering spiritual guidance. Their continued presence expresses an ongoing commitment to faith, service, and communal religious life.

## Overview

Religious orders have existed since the early centuries of the Church. They form members for a life devoted to God and often serve society through education, healthcare, or mission. Members typically take vows of poverty, chastity, and obedience; some orders add or adapt commitments according to their charism or rule.

## Types of Orders

### Monastic orders

These orders emphasize prayer, contemplation, and communal life. Members usually live in monasteries or abbeys.

- **Benedictines.** Founded by St. Benedict in the sixth century, they follow the *Rule of St. Benedict* and join prayer to work (*ora et labora*).
- **Cistercians.** A reform of the Benedictine tradition, stressing stricter observance and manual labor.
- **Trappists.** A stricter Cistercian branch, known for silence and austerity.

### Mendicant orders

These orders emphasize preaching, teaching, and service among the people and often depend on charity. Their life is more itinerant than that of monastics.

- **Franciscans.** Founded by St. Francis of Assisi; poverty, humility, and care for the poor.
- **Dominicans.** Founded by St. Dominic; preaching, education, and the contesting of heresy.

### Canons regular

Clergy living in community under a rule, combining elements of monastic life with parish ministry.

- **Augustinians.** Follow the *Rule of St. Augustine*; often engaged in education and pastoral work.

### Clerical and active orders

Some orders, such as the Jesuits, join active ministry to spiritual discipline.

- **Jesuits (Society of Jesus).** Founded by St. Ignatius of Loyola in the sixteenth century; education, missions, and the intellectual apostolate.

## Female Religious Orders

Many women’s orders parallel men’s orders and combine prayer with service.

- **Poor Clares.** A Franciscan women’s order emphasizing cloistered life and poverty.
- **Dominican Sisters.** Education, healthcare, and social work.
- **Carmelites.** Contemplative and active branches, known for prayer and mysticism.

## Other Distinctions

- **Cloistered and active.** Some communities remain largely enclosed for contemplation; active orders work directly among people.
- **Ecumenical range.** Most well-known orders are Catholic, but Eastern Orthodoxy has monastic communities, and some Anglican and Lutheran groups observe monastic principles.
- **Charism.** Each order is marked by a particular spiritual focus or mission—teaching, mission, healthcare, contemplative prayer, and so on.
