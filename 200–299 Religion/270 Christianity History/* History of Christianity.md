# History of Christianity
- 270 Christianity History

**The history of Christianity** begins with Jesus, an itinerant Jewish preacher and teacher who was crucified in Jerusalem. His followers proclaimed that he was God incarnate and that he had risen from the dead. In the two millennia since, Christianity has spread across the world and become the largest religion, with more than two billion adherents. *Christianity* arose in the first century CE as a Jewish movement, became a global religion, and has deeply shaped cultural, political, and social history worldwide. Christian history is a long interaction of faith, culture, and politics: a small Jewish movement that became a major force on the global stage.

## Overview
Christianity began as a mostly urban grassroots movement and grew to more than a million adherents by the third century. In its first century it developed church government and new sacred writings, which were not formalized as a canon until the fourth century. Constantine the Great issued the Edict of Milan in 313, legalizing the faith. Christian art, architecture, and literature flourished in the fourth century, but competing theologies produced lasting divisions. The Nicene Creed of 325, the Nestorian schism, the Church of the East, and Oriental Orthodoxy belong to that history of split and definition.

After the fall of Rome in 476, the West was fragmented. Monks preserved culture and provided social services. Early Muslim conquests devastated many Christian communities in the Middle East and North Africa and contributed to their decline there. The church in the East prospered in parts of Asia and helped form the states of Eastern Europe. The East–West Schism of 1054 marked the separation of Eastern Orthodoxy and the Catholic Church. Despite deep differences, the East later sought Western military aid against the Turks, a request that helped produce the Crusades. Gregorian reform made Western Catholicism more centralized and bureaucratic. Facing internal and external challenges, the Church contested heresy and established inquisitorial courts. Artistic and intellectual work among Western monks contributed to the twelfth-century Renaissance and, later, to the Scientific Revolution.

In the fourteenth century the Western Schism and other late-medieval crises prepared the way for the sixteenth-century Reformation, in which Protestantism took shape. Protestants often advocated religious tolerance and the separation of church and state, and they influenced economic life. Rival royal houses took sides in the European wars of religion. Christianity spread with European colonization of the Americas, Australia, and New Zealand. Different strands of the faith shaped the Enlightenment, the American and French Revolutions, and the Industrial Revolution. Christians took part both in the Atlantic slave trade and in its abolition. Some Protestants developed biblical criticism; others answered rationalism with Pietism and revivals that generated new denominations. Nineteenth-century missionaries laid linguistic and cultural foundations in many societies.

## Origins and Early Development

Christianity began in Roman Judea in the first century CE. It is rooted in the teaching of Jesus of Nazareth, whom Christians confess as the Christ (Messiah) and the Son of God. Jesus preached love, forgiveness, and the kingdom of God. After his crucifixion under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, his followers proclaimed his resurrection and spread his teaching through the region.

The Apostolic Age (c. 30–100 CE) was marked by missionaries such as Paul of Tarsus, who carried the message to Gentile communities across the Roman Empire. Early Christians met in private homes, sometimes under persecution. Their beliefs were recorded in writings that later formed the New Testament.

## Christianity in the Roman Empire

At first treated as a sect of Judaism, Christianity acquired a distinct identity. By the third century it had spread widely, though believers were often persecuted. In 313 CE Emperor Constantine issued the Edict of Milan, granting Christians religious tolerance. Under Theodosius I in 380 CE Christianity became the state religion, and its influence throughout the empire was secured.

## Medieval Christianity

In the Middle Ages Christianity shaped European life. The Catholic Church became a dominant cultural and political institution. Monasticism flourished, and the Church influenced law, education, and art. The Great Schism of 1054 divided Christianity into the Roman Catholic Church in the West and the Eastern Orthodox Church in the East.

## Reformation and Modern Movements

In the sixteenth century the Protestant Reformation began, led by figures such as Martin Luther, John Calvin, and—in England—Henry VIII. It challenged Catholic doctrine and practice, produced a range of Protestant denominations, and brought major religious, social, and political change across Europe.

## Global Spread

From the sixteenth century onward, European colonization and missionary work carried Christianity to the Americas, Africa, and Asia. The faith diversified into many denominations within Catholicism, Orthodoxy, and Protestantism, each with its own theology and practice.

## Christianity Today

In the twentieth century Christianity declined in much of the Western world but grew in the Global South, especially in Southeast Asia and sub-Saharan Africa. In the twenty-first century it is the largest and among the most diverse of the world’s religions, embracing worship and Scripture in more than three thousand languages.

Christianity is now one of the world’s largest religions, with billions of adherents. It continues to influence culture, ethics, politics, and education in many countries, while adapting to social change, interfaith encounter, and contemporary theological debate.
