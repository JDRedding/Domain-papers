# Christian social work
- 260 Christian organization

**Christian social work** joins faith-based values, ethics, and compassion to professional social service. Worship and spiritual life often support and enrich that practice. Spiritual principles may inform practice, but ethical boundaries remain essential. Clients’ beliefs and autonomy are to be respected; care must not impose religious standards. Worship and spiritual discipline strengthen the practitioner’s ethical grounding; they support practice rather than coerce clients. Christian social work and worship are linked in the service of compassionate, ethical, and holistic care. Worship nourishes the worker’s resilience and moral vision; Christian social work turns those values into practical service that seeks justice, dignity, and hope for clients.

## Foundations

Christian social work combines standard social-work principles with Christian ethical and theological perspectives. It affirms the dignity of every person and follows the values of love, justice, mercy, and service modeled in the teaching of Jesus. Practitioners address material and social needs while attending to clients’ spiritual well-being. Scripture often informs ethical decision-making, advocacy, and care for marginalized people.

Key elements include:

- **Faith and practice together.** Christian ethics shape intervention, case planning, and advocacy.
- **Holistic care.** Social, emotional, psychological, and spiritual needs are held together.
- **Social justice.** Work with vulnerable populations, the promotion of equality, and resistance to systemic injustice are treated as extensions of Christian love.

## The Role of Worship

In this setting worship serves both personal and professional ends:

- **Spiritual renewal for practitioners.** Regular worship and prayer help social workers remain grounded, compassionate, and resilient. They can reduce burnout and sustain empathy and moral clarity.
- **Faith in community engagement.** Shared worship and prayer reinforce humility, service, and justice—qualities that inform ethical practice and leadership in social programs.
- **Integration with pastoral care.** Where clients are receptive, liturgy, prayer, or reflection on Scripture may form part of holistic care, offering hope, emotional support, and spiritual resilience (for example, in supportive group sessions).

## Practical Settings

Christian social work commonly takes place in:

- faith-based nonprofits and charities that provide food, housing, counseling, and other community services
- church social ministries in which volunteers and professionals organize outreach and address local needs
- hospitals, schools, and counseling settings that join spiritual guidance to ordinary social-work services
