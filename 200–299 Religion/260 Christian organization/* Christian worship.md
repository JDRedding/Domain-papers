# Christian worship
- 260 Christian organization

**Christian worship** is the expression of devotion, reverence, and adoration toward God. It includes prayer, music, sacraments, and communal gathering, and it aims both to honor God and to draw believers into communion with the divine. Christian worship is a many-sided practice that honors God, nourishes personal and communal faith, and takes form in prayer, music, sacraments, and teaching. It is both a spiritual discipline and a public proclamation of faith.

Worship is not limited to church services (which is covered in other domains beyond Christian organization). It also includes personal devotion, prayer, meditation, and acts of service, so that faith is lived day by day. It fosters spiritual growth, communal bonds, and moral guidance, and so undergirds Christian life.

## Meaning and Purpose

Worship is central to Christian faith. It expresses love, obedience, and devotion to God. It is both personal and communal. Its purposes include glorifying God, nourishing spiritual life, and strengthening faith. Worship leads believers to reflect on God’s character, on salvation in Jesus Christ, and on the teaching of the Bible. It is also a means of gratitude, guidance, and the seeking of forgiveness.

## Forms of Worship

Forms vary by tradition and denomination. Common elements include:

- **Prayer.** Individual or corporate address to God: adoration, confession, thanksgiving, and supplication.
- **Singing and music.** Hymns, contemporary songs, and chants that express praise. Music often gathers the congregation and shapes the atmosphere of worship.
- **Scripture reading.** Biblical passages read to teach, inspire, and guide faith and life.
- **Sacraments.** Rites instituted by Christ, such as baptism and Holy Communion, signifying grace, fellowship, and spiritual renewal.
- **Preaching and teaching.** Sermons that interpret Scripture and offer moral and spiritual guidance.
- **Liturgical acts.** In many traditions, a structured order of service (liturgy) with responsive readings, prayers, and symbolic gestures.
- **Contemplative practices.** Silence, meditation, or other reflective acts that cultivate awareness of God’s presence.

## Denominational Variations

- **Catholic and Orthodox churches** emphasize liturgy, sacraments, and ritual, often with formal prayer, incense, and icons.
- **Protestant churches** emphasize preaching, Scripture, and congregational singing; some adopt contemporary styles.
- **Evangelical and Pentecostal traditions** highlight spontaneous expression, worship music, and charismatic elements such as speaking in tongues or energetic praise.
