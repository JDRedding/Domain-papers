# Christianity

**Christianity** is a monotheistic religion based on the life, teachings, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian theology is the study of its doctrines, beliefs, and practices.

## Overview of Christianity

Christianity arose in the first century CE as a movement within Judaism, centered on Jesus of Nazareth, whom Christians confess as the Son of God and the Messiah (Christ). Followers believe that Jesus’ death and resurrection bring salvation, reconciliation with God, and the promise of eternal life. Today Christianity is one of the world’s largest religions and includes major traditions such as Catholicism, Eastern Orthodoxy, and Protestantism.

Core beliefs include:

- **The Trinity:** one God in three persons—Father, Son (Jesus Christ), and Holy Spirit
- **The Incarnation:** Jesus as fully God and fully human
- **Atonement:** salvation through Jesus’ sacrificial death and resurrection
- **Scripture:** the Bible as authoritative, comprising the Old and New Testaments
- **Resurrection and eternal life:** belief in life after death, with Christ’s resurrection as the pledge of redemption and immortality

## Christian Theology

Christian theology is the systematic study of God, the world, and humanity from a Christian standpoint. It addresses questions of faith, morality, and the relationship between God and creation. Major fields include:

- **Systematic theology:** organizes beliefs about God, Christ, the Holy Spirit, salvation, and the Church into coherent frameworks
- **Biblical theology:** traces theological themes through particular scriptures and across the Bible as a whole
- **Historical theology:** studies the development of doctrine through creeds, councils, and reform movements
- **Practical theology:** applies theological insight to worship, ethics, and ministry

## Key Doctrines

- **Christology:** the person and work of Jesus Christ, including his divinity and humanity
- **Soteriology:** the doctrine of salvation, including faith, grace, works, and redemption
- **Pneumatology:** the Holy Spirit’s role in inspiration, guidance, and sanctification
- **Ecclesiology:** the nature and mission of the Church, the sacraments, and Christian community
- **Eschatology:** the last things, including the second coming of Christ and final judgment

## Diversity Within Christianity

Theology differs across traditions:

- **Catholicism** emphasizes papal authority, tradition, and the sacraments as means of grace.
- **Eastern Orthodoxy** stresses liturgical worship, *theosis* (divinization), and continuity with the early Church.
- **Protestantism** emphasizes *sola scriptura* (Scripture alone) and justification by faith, and includes Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican, Baptist, and other traditions.

Christian theology continues to develop in conversation with philosophy, science, ethics, and global cultures. At the same time, doctrines such as the Trinity, Christ’s resurrection, and salvation remain foundational to the faith.
