# Divine Attributes (Modal Logic)
- Ontological Argument

Standard notation uses **possible-worlds semantics** (Kripke frames).

- $\Box \phi$ = “it is necessary that $\phi$” (true in every accessible world)
- $\Diamond \phi$ = “it is possible that $\phi$” (true in at least one accessible world)
- $G$ = the property of being a maximally great being (omnipotent, omniscient, and perfectly good in every possible world)

Alvin Plantinga’s **modal ontological argument** (simplified form in S5):

$$
\begin{align*}
&\Diamond \exists x\, Gx \\
&\Box (\exists x\, Gx \to \Box \exists x\, Gx) \\
&\therefore \Box \exists x\, Gx
\end{align*}
$$

(The second premise is the characteristic axiom of modal collapse for necessary existence.)

**Divine attributes** are often formalized as:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Omnipotence} &\quad \forall p\, \Diamond (Gp \to p) \\
\text{Omniscience} &\quad \forall p\, (Kp \leftrightarrow p) \\
\text{Perfect goodness} &\quad \forall x\, (Gx \to \text{maximally good}(x))
\end{align*}
$$
