# Christian moral theology
- 240 Christian practices

**Christian moral theology** is the study of human conduct in light of divine revelation. It aims to guide believers toward virtuous lives according to God’s will as known in Scripture, tradition, and reason.  Christian moral theology is a comprehensive ethical framework grounded in revelation and reason. It helps believers live virtuously, judge difficult cases, and grow spiritually in accordance with Christian teaching.

## Definition and Purpose

Christian moral theology—also called moral or ethical theology—examines the principles and norms that govern moral behavior from a Christian standpoint. Its main purpose is to direct people toward a life pleasing to God, conducive to human flourishing, and aligned with the teaching of Jesus Christ. It draws on Scripture, church tradition, reason, and human experience to form a coherent moral framework.

## Sources of Moral Guidance

- **Sacred Scripture.** The Bible is the foundation of moral teaching, through commandments, parables, and the ethical instruction of Jesus and the apostles. Love, justice, and charity are central.
- **Tradition.** The Church Fathers, councils, and later official teaching interpret Scripture and show how moral principles apply in changing circumstances.
- **Reason and natural law.** Many accounts hold that reason can discern objective moral truths in creation, complementing revelation.
- **Magisterium.** In Catholic moral theology especially, the Church’s teaching office offers authoritative guidance on difficult cases.

## Key Concepts

- **Virtue and vice.** Moral theology stresses the cultivation of faith, hope, charity, prudence, justice, temperance, and fortitude, and the avoidance of vices that lead away from God.
- **Conscience.** A well-formed conscience is central to decision. It is educated through Scripture, prayer, and the Church’s moral teaching.
- **Sin and grace.** Sin is treated as a barrier to communion with God; grace is treated as what enables moral goodness and spiritual change.
- **The Commandments and the Beatitudes.** These supply concrete guidance: love of God and neighbor, and the practice of virtue in daily life.

## Approaches and Schools

- **Catholic moral theology** is rooted in natural law, Scripture, and tradition. It is often organized around virtues and commandments and uses principles such as proportionality and double effect in applied ethics.
- **Protestant ethics** centers on Scripture and the command to love God and neighbor, with emphasis on justification by faith and moral responsibility in ordinary life.
- **Eastern Orthodox ethics** emphasizes *theosis* (deification) and the growth of virtue through sacramental life and the guidance of spiritual elders.

## Practical Applications

Moral theology informs personal, social, and professional judgment. Typical fields include:

- bioethics (beginning of life, medical ethics, end-of-life care)
- social justice and economic fairness
- environmental stewardship
- interpersonal ethics and family life

By joining theological principle to everyday choice, it seeks to align action, intention, and character with God’s moral will.
