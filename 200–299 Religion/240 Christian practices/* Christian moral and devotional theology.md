# Christian moral and devotional theology
- 240 Christian practices

**Christian moral theology** asks how believers discern right from wrong and live virtuous lives. **Devotional theology** emphasizes personal spiritual practice and intimate communion with God. Moral theology guides right action according to God’s law; devotional theology forms an inner life of prayer and communion. Together they support an integrated Christian life.

Moral and devotional theology are closely joined. Personal devotion strengthens virtue; ethical living deepens intimacy with God. Acts of charity, for example, fulfill moral obligation and also nourish the soul, showing God’s love in practice. Both disciplines aim to unite interior transformation with outward conduct and so to form a whole Christian life. 

## Christian Moral Theology

Moral theology aims to guide believers toward lives consistent with God’s will, ethical principle, and the teaching of Jesus Christ. Its main elements include:

- **Scripture and commandments.** The Bible—especially the Ten Commandments and Jesus’ teaching in the Gospels—supplies the primary moral foundation.
- **Virtues.** The cardinal virtues (prudence, justice, fortitude, temperance) and the theological virtues (faith, hope, charity) shape ethical character.
- **Sin and conscience.** Distinguishing kinds of sin and forming a rightly informed conscience are central to moral decision.
- **Natural law.** On this view, God’s moral order is knowable by reason, so moral truths can be discerned universally.
- **Ethical decision-making.** Moral theology offers frameworks for complex cases in social justice, bioethics, and personal conduct, read in light of divine law.

## Christian Devotional Theology

Devotional theology cultivates a personal relationship with God through prayer, worship, and spiritual discipline. Core aspects include:

- **Prayer and meditation.** Regular prayer, contemplative reflection, and meditation on Scripture (*lectio divina*) help believers interiorize God’s presence.
- **Sacraments and rituals.** Participation in rites such as the Eucharist, baptism, and confession fosters growth and holiness.
- **Spiritual exercises.** Fasting, retreats, and journaling support transformation of heart and mind, so devotion is more than obligation.
- **Devotional literature and the saints.** Writers such as Thomas à Kempis and Teresa of Ávila guide personal piety and ethical living.
- **Integration with daily life.** True spirituality is expected to issue in moral action, charity, and service.
