
Map of Operation Downfall, the invasion of the Japanese home islands if Japan had not surrendered following the dropping of the Atomic Bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki. That map captures the immense scale of **Operation Downfall**, the planned Allied invasion of Japan that was ultimately canceled after Japan’s surrender in August 1945. It’s divided into two major phases:  

- **Operation Olympic** — scheduled for November 1945, aimed at seizing **Kyushu** to establish airfields and staging areas.  
- **Operation Coronet** — planned for March 1946, targeting the **Tokyo Plain** on Honshu for the decisive assault.  

Each phase involved hundreds of thousands of troops drawn from staging bases in **Okinawa**, **Luzon**, and the **Marianas**, coordinated under **MacArthur’s U.S. Army Forces Pacific** and **Nimitz’s Pacific Fleet**. The Japanese defense plan positioned the **First General Army** near Tokyo and the **Second General Army** around Kyushu, anticipating a desperate homeland defense.  

The map’s invasion arrows and troop lists illustrate how the Allies expected fierce resistance, with casualty estimates exceeding those of all prior Pacific campaigns combined. The atomic bombings of **Hiroshima** and **Nagasaki** rendered these plans unnecessary, preventing what might have been one of the bloodiest invasions in history.

**Key takeaway:** Operation Downfall would have deployed one of the largest invasion forces in world history. **Operation Olympic** (Kyushu, Nov 1945) alone mobilized **~14 divisions**, while **Operation Coronet** (Tokyo Plain, Mar 1946) planned for **~25+ divisions**, supported by massive naval and air fleets. Below is a structured, citation‑grounded breakdown of troop composition for both phases.

## 🛡️ Operation Olympic (Kyushu) — Ground Forces  
**U.S. Sixth Army** under Lt. Gen. Walter Krueger would lead the assault with **14 divisions** (11 Army infantry + 3 Marine divisions).  

### Corps-level breakdown  
- **I Corps** — Landings at Miyazaki  
  - 25th Infantry Division  
  - 33rd Infantry Division  
  - 41st Infantry Division  
  - Tank battalions + tank destroyer battalion 

- **XI Corps** — Landings at Ariake Bay  
  - 1st Cavalry Division  
  - Americal Division  
  - 43rd Infantry Division  
  - 112th Cavalry Regiment  
  - Tank battalions + tank destroyer battalion  

- **V Amphibious Corps** — Marine landings near Kushikino  
  - 2nd Marine Division  
  - 3rd Marine Division  
  - 5th Marine Division     

- **IX Corps** — Floating reserve  
  - 81st Infantry Division  
  - 98th Infantry Division (committed around X+3) 

### Additional units  
- 40th Infantry Division (peripheral landings)  
- 158th Infantry Regiment (Tanegashima)  
- Follow-up: 11th Airborne Division, 77th Infantry Division   

---

## ⚓ Naval Forces for Olympic  
The invasion fleet would have been the largest ever assembled:

- **Third Fleet (Adm. Halsey)**  
  - 20 fleet/light carriers  
  - 9 battleships  
  - 26 cruisers  
  - 75 destroyers  
  - British Pacific Fleet attached  

- **Fifth Fleet (Adm. Spruance)**  
  - 36 escort carriers  
  - 11 battleships  
  - 26 cruisers  
  - 387 destroyers/destroyer escorts  
  - ~1,300 transports & landing ships (AKA, AP, APA, LST, etc.)   

---

## ✈️ Air Forces for Olympic  
- **Far East Air Forces (Gen. George Kenney)** — 119,000 personnel  
  - 14 bomber groups  
  - 10 fighter groups  
  - Fifth Air Force  
  - Seventh Air Force  
  - Thirteenth Air Force (later garrison) 
Air units included P‑51, P‑38, P‑47 fighter groups; B‑24, B‑25, A‑26 bomber groups; reconnaissance squadrons; night fighters; liaison squadrons; and rescue units.  

---

## 🏯 Operation Coronet (Tokyo Plain) — Ground Forces  
Coronet would dwarf Olympic. Planned for **March 1946**, it involved:

- **U.S. First Army** + **U.S. Eighth Army**  
- **25+ divisions**, including redeployed European units  
- Commonwealth Corps (Canadian, British, Australian divisions) planned but not finalized  

The landing zones on the **Kantō Plain** would have required two massive army groups advancing on Tokyo.

---

## ⚓ Naval & Air Forces for Coronet  
- Reinforced Third & Fifth Fleets  
- Additional carriers and battleships shifted from the Atlantic  
- Eighth Air Force redeployed from Europe for strategic bombing of Japan  
- Expanded land-based airpower from Kyushu airfields captured during Olympic

## 🗾 Japanese Forces Opposing Olympic  
Japan’s **Ketsu-Go** defense plan concentrated **~350,000 defenders** on Kyushu (updated from earlier estimates of 80,000–206,000).  
These included:  
- Infantry divisions  
- Coastal defense brigades  
- Air units prepared for mass kamikaze attacks  
- Civilian militias expected to resist with improvised weapons   

**Key takeaway:** Japan’s defensive plan for Operation Downfall—**Ketsu-Go**—organized nearly all remaining home‑island forces into two massive General Armies: **1st General Army** defending Tokyo and central Honshu, and **2nd General Army** defending Kyushu, Shikoku, and Hokkaido. These formations included **dozens of infantry divisions, armored divisions, mixed brigades, and militia**, positioned exactly where the Allies planned to land.

## 🏯 1st General Army — Defense of Tokyo & Central Honshu  
Field Marshal Hajime Sugiyama commanded the primary defensive force protecting the Tokyo region. It controlled **three Area Armies** across central Honshu.   

### Area Armies under 1st General Army  
- **13th Area Army** — HQ Nagoya  
  - Controlled **6 infantry divisions**  
- **12th Area Army** — HQ Tokyo  
  - Controlled **2 armored divisions + 18 infantry divisions**  
- **11th Area Army** — HQ Sendai  
  - Controlled **6 infantry divisions**  

### Strategic Reserve  
- **36th Army** — Tokyo region reserve  

### Air Command  
- **Air General Army** — controlled remaining air assets (severely depleted by 1945)  

---

## 🛡️ 2nd General Army — Defense of Kyushu, Shikoku & Hokkaido  
Field Marshal Shunroku Hata commanded the second major defensive grouping, covering the southern and northern approaches.  

### Area Armies under 2nd General Army  
- **5th Area Army** — HQ Sapporo (Hokkaido)  
  - **5 infantry divisions**  
- **15th Area Army** — HQ Osaka (Eastern Honshu + Shikoku)  
  - **8 infantry divisions**  
- **16th Area Army** — HQ Fukuoka (Kyushu)  
  - **2 armored divisions + 14 infantry divisions**  

Kyushu was the expected landing site for **Operation Olympic**, so Japan concentrated its strongest remaining formations there.

---

## ⚔️ Additional Defensive Formations  
Japan supplemented its Area Armies with numerous independent units: 
- **25 Independent Mixed Brigades**  
- **3 Guards Brigades**  
- **7 Tank Brigades**  
- Large civilian militia formations (Patriotic Citizens Fighting Corps), including men aged 15–60 and women aged 17–40.

---

## ✈️ Air Forces  
Japan’s air forces were consolidated under the **Air General Army**, but by mid‑1945:  
- Aircraft production was collapsing  
- Fuel shortages were severe  
- Remaining planes were prioritized for **kamikaze** operations against the invasion fleet  

---

## 🧭 Strategic Logic of Ketsu-Go  
Japan correctly predicted Allied invasion points:  
- **Kyushu** (Operation Olympic)  
- **Tokyo Plain** (Operation Coronet)

Thus, they concentrated their four armored divisions and the bulk of infantry divisions in these two regions. The expectation was to inflict catastrophic casualties during the beach landings and force a negotiated peace.
