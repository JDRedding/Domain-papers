Italian and Romanian are both Romance languages descended from Latin. Italian belongs to the Italo-Western branch and Romanian to the Eastern Romance branch. They share many similarities, but they also differ because of geography and later historical influences.

Italian and Romanian share a Latin origin and remain close in vocabulary, spelling, and some grammatical structures. They diverged over centuries through geography, neighboring languages, and separate historical development. Romanian’s Slavic and other influences set it apart, but the two languages are still similar enough that comparative study is linguistically and culturally rewarding.

## Learning Considerations

Both languages are often treated as relatively accessible for English speakers. The choice between them usually depends on interest, location, and cultural connection. Italian has a wide presence in Europe and in the arts; Romanian is especially useful for Romania, Moldova, and Romanian-speaking communities abroad.

## Origins and Classification

Romance languages evolved from Vulgar Latin, the everyday speech of the Roman Empire, and form a subgroup of the Italic branch of the Indo-European family.

- **Italian** belongs to the Italo-Western subgroup and is closely related to other Western Romance languages such as Spanish and French.
- **Romanian** belongs to the Eastern Romance group. It developed in relative isolation in Southeastern Europe and absorbed influences from Slavic languages, Turkish, Greek, and Hungarian.

Related languages include Sardinian, Corsican, the Rhaetian languages, and extinct varieties such as Dalmatian. Other Eastern Romance languages include Aromanian, Megleno-Romanian, and Istro-Romanian.

## Mutual Intelligibility and Cultural Ties

Intelligibility is asymmetric. Romanian speakers are often said to understand a large share of Italian, especially in writing (commonly cited figures are about 65% of spoken Italian and 85% of written Italian). Scholars have also noted correspondences between Romanian and southern Italian dialects, including lexical parallels with Calabrian and Sicilian and shared morphosyntactic features such as differential object marking and clitic doubling.

Italian later influenced Romanian culture and language as well, especially during the 19th-century Transylvanian School, which drew on Italian lexical and orthographic models. Those Latinist movements emphasized Romanian’s Roman origins and strengthened cultural ties with Italy.

## Vocabulary, Phonology, and Grammar

Lexical similarity between Italian and Romanian is high, often estimated at about 77%. Many words look and function similarly:

- Romanian *ochi* / Italian *occhi* (“eyes”)
- Romanian *pâine* / Italian *pane* (“bread”)
- Romanian *câine* / Italian *cane* (“dog”)
- Italian *triste* / Romanian *trist* (“sad”)

Romanian also contains Slavic and other loanwords that make parts of its lexicon distinct—for example, *isprăvi* (“to complete”) beside Italian *completare*.

Grammatically, Romanian preserves some Latin features that Italian has largely lost:

- Three grammatical genders (masculine, feminine, neuter)
- Noun cases (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive, vocative)
- A more complex verb system

Italian has two genders and does not mark case on nouns. Both languages use the Latin alphabet, but Romanian adds five special characters—*ă*, *â*, *î*, *ș*, and *ț*—to represent native sounds. Their sound systems correspond fairly closely, which makes pronunciation relatively accessible to speakers of either language.
