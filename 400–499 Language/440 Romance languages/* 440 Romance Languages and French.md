# Romance Languages and French
- 440 Romance languages; French

Romance languages are modern languages that evolved from Vulgar Latin. The major ones include Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, and Romanian, along with regional languages such as Catalan, Galician, and Occitan. French is often noted for its sound and has a long association with art, diplomacy, and literature. It is an official language in many countries and is spoken across Europe, Africa, North America, and the Caribbean. Shared Latin vocabulary also makes it relatively accessible to speakers of other Romance languages.

Romance languages form a branch of the Italic group within the Indo-European family. They all descend from Vulgar Latin, the colloquial Latin spoken by soldiers and ordinary people of the Roman Empire. Over centuries, Latin evolved differently across regions of Europe under the influence of local languages, invasions, and cultural change.

## Overview

Romance languages descend from Latin and include French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, and Romanian. French evolved from the Latin of northern Gaul and developed distinctive pronunciation, grammar, and vocabulary. It still shares core Romance features—gendered nouns, verb conjugation, and a Latin-based lexicon—while also showing unique developments such as nasal vowels and simplified case usage. Placing French in this family helps explain its historical roots, structure, and cultural reach.

Common features include:

- Gendered nouns
- Extensive verb conjugation
- Definite and indefinite articles
- A large core vocabulary derived from Latin

### Historical development

Compared with Classical Latin, French underwent major phonetic, grammatical, and lexical change:

- **Phonetics:** Extensive vowel reduction, nasalization, and the loss of many final consonants.
- **Grammar:** Old French still preserved some Latin noun cases, but Modern French largely replaced case endings with a fixed subject–verb–object word order and greater use of prepositions.
- **Vocabulary:** French retains a large Latin-derived core while also borrowing from Frankish, English, and other languages.

French shares many features with other Romance languages, including verb conjugation across tenses, plural nouns marked with *-s*, and the use of articles. Its pronunciation and some syntactic patterns, however, are distinctively French.

## Regional Romance Languages

Regional varieties include Catalan, Galician, and Occitan. These languages are spoken in particular regions and often carry distinct cultural identities.

## French as a Romance Language

French developed mainly from Gallo-Romance, the Latin spoken in northern Gaul (modern France and parts of Belgium). It was also influenced by Celtic languages and by Frankish, a Germanic language.
