# Carcosa cosmological hierarchy 

## Carcosa-centric pantheon

Here’s the “pantheon” in Carcosa terms, not Lovecraft’s:

- **Top layer: Phenomenon**
  - **The King in Yellow** — not just a being, but a **phenomenon**: play + colour + city + mask.   
- **Locations**
  - **Carcosa** — central city, decaying, mist‑shrouded.  
  - **Lake Hali** — liminal lake, boundary and mirror.  
  - **Alar, Hastur, Yhtill** — barely described cities/places, part of the same yellow geography.  
- **Artifacts & symbols**
  - **The Yellow Sign** — sigil of infection.  
  - **The Pallid Mask** — threshold of ego dissolution.  
- **Concepts**
  - **Madness through art**  
  - **Reality as stage set**  
  - **Identity erosion**  

In some taxonomies, see a chart like:

> **Concept:** The King in Yellow  
> **Great Old One:** Hastur  
> **Location:** Carcosa  
> **Artifact:** Yellow Sign  
> **Phenomenon:** Madness via the play

## Carcosa as a place

**Core idea:** Carcosa is a *fictional city* that behaves more like a metaphysical anomaly than a normal location.
Think of Carcosa as a **non‑Euclidean, reality‑warping city** that exists wherever the King’s influence has fully colonized perception. 

- **Origin layer:**  
  **Ambrose Bierce** first names Carcosa as a ruined, ancient city glimpsed after its destruction.  
- **Chambers layer:**  
  In *The King in Yellow* (1895), Carcosa becomes a **mysterious, cursed city** on the shores of **Lake Hali**, under **twin suns**, with **black stars** and “strange moons.”  
  Text hints that Carcosa is either **another planet**, **another universe**, or a **psychic realm** that overwrites reality for those infected by the play.

## The Carcosa sky: stars, suns, and Hali

- **Twin suns:**  
  Carcosa’s sunsets involve **two suns sinking behind Lake Hali**, a repeated image in *Cassilda’s Song*.    
- **Black stars & strange moons:**  
  The sky features **black stars** and **strange moons**, signaling a cosmos that is familiar in name but alien in physics.  
- **Hyades & Aldebaran:**  
  References to the **Hyades** and **Aldebaran** tie Carcosa to recognizable constellations, but in a way that feels displaced—like our sky seen from a wrong universe.  
- **Lake Hali:**  
  A **misty lake** beside Carcosa; the play places cities like **Alar** and **Carcosa** on its shores. It’s less a body of water and more a **threshold** between worlds.

## The King in Yellow and his aspects
Carcosa’s cosmology is inseparable from **The King in Yellow**—entity, play, and symbol all braided together.   

- **The King in Yellow (entity):**  
  A masked, robed figure—often **tattered yellow robes**, **Pallid Mask**—who embodies **madness, decay, and identity collapse**.  
- **The Pallid Mask:**  
  More a **conceptual boundary** than a simple object: the point where a person’s ego dissolves and Carcosa’s reality replaces theirs.  
- **Hastur connection:**  
  Later mythos writers equate or entangle the King with **Hastur**, turning him into a **Great Old One** or avatar within the broader Cthulhu Mythos—but that’s a *later graft*, not Chambers’ original.

## The Yellow Sign 
Memetic infection

Carcosa doesn’t spread by armies—it spreads by **art**.

- **The play:**  
  The fictional play *The King in Yellow*—especially **Act II**—destroys sanity; reading it reconfigures the reader’s reality toward Carcosa.  
- **The Yellow Sign:**  
  A **non‑human glyph** that marks someone as claimed; it functions like a **memetic anchor**, binding the victim’s perception to Carcosa’s cosmology. 
- **Mechanism:**  
  Carcosa behaves like a **predatory meme**—a conceptual parasite that overwrites the host’s world with its own architecture, sky, and hierarchy.
