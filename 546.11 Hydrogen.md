## Basic Properties

Hydrogen has the chemical symbol $\mathrm{H}$ and atomic number 1, making it the simplest element, composed of one proton and one electron. In its most common form, it exists as a diatomic molecule ($\mathrm{H}_{2}$) under standard conditions, which is a colorless, tasteless, and highly flammable gas. Its atomic weight is approximately $1.008\,\mathrm{u}$, and it has the lowest density of all gases.

## Environmental Implications

Hydrogen production and utilization offer potential to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, especially when produced from renewable sources like water electrolysis powered by solar or wind energy. However, large-scale production, storage, and transportation require careful engineering due to its low density and flammability.

## Physical and Chemical Characteristics

- **State at room temperature:** Gas  
- **Boiling point:** $-252.87^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ ($-423.17^{\circ}\mathrm{F}$)  
- **Melting point:** $-259.16^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ ($-434.49^{\circ}\mathrm{F}$)  
- **Reactivity:** Hydrogen readily reacts with most elements, particularly oxygen, forming water ($\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$), and combines with carbon to produce hydrocarbons. It is also highly reactive in many industrial chemical applications.

One significant chemical property is its ability to act as both a reducing agent and a proton donor, making it essential in a variety of chemical reactions, including hydrogenation in organic chemistry.

## Occurrence and Significance

Hydrogen is the most abundant element in the universe, comprising roughly 75% of all normal (baryonic) matter. It is the primary fuel in stars, where nuclear fusion converts hydrogen into helium, releasing enormous energy. On Earth, hydrogen is rarely found in its pure form due to its high reactivity, but it occurs in water ($\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$), organic compounds, and minerals.

## Uses

- **Energy Carrier:** Hydrogen can serve as a clean fuel in hydrogen fuel cells, producing electricity and water without harmful emissions, making it a key resource in renewable energy technologies.  
- **Industrial Applications:** It is used in ammonia production for fertilizers, refining fuels, and manufacturing methanol.  
- **Scientific Research:** Hydrogen isotopes, such as deuterium and tritium, are crucial in nuclear research and fusion technologies.  
- **Rocket Fuel:** Liquid hydrogen, combined with oxygen, provides high-efficiency propulsion for space vehicles.  

## Isotopes

Hydrogen has three naturally occurring isotopes:

- **Protium (${}^{1}\mathrm{H}$):** Most common; no neutrons  
- **Deuterium (${}^{2}\mathrm{H}$ or $\mathrm{D}$):** One neutron; used in heavy water and nuclear applications  
- **Tritium (${}^{3}\mathrm{H}$ or $\mathrm{T}$):** Two neutrons; radioactive; used in fusion and tracing studies
