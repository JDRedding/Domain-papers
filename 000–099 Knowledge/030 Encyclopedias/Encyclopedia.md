# Encyclopedia
- 030 Encyclopedias/

An encyclopedia is a comprehensive reference work that compiles knowledge on a wide range of topics and organizes it systematically for easy reference and study. Encyclopedias are structured repositories of human knowledge. They are designed to give authoritative, accessible information on many topics and remain essential tools for education, research, and general learning.

## Definition and Purpose

An encyclopedia is a reference book or digital resource that provides concise, factual, and authoritative information across many fields of knowledge. It typically covers subjects in science, history, literature, geography, the arts, and technology. Its primary purpose is to educate, inform, and give readers quick access to verified information. Encyclopedias are widely used for research, study, and general knowledge.

## Historical Context

The idea of the encyclopedia goes back to antiquity. An early example is Pliny the Elder’s *Natural History*. Over time, encyclopedias moved from handwritten manuscripts to printed volumes and then to large online databases. Digital formats made information more accessible and easier to update. They also introduced hyperlinking, multimedia, and collaborative editing, which greatly expanded how encyclopedias can be used.

## Structure and Organization

Most encyclopedias are arranged alphabetically by entry (an A–Z format) so users can find topics easily. Some are organized thematically or by subject category instead.

Each entry usually includes:

- A definition or description of the topic
- Historical context or background
- Key facts, developments, or notable figures
- References or suggestions for further reading

Modern encyclopedias often add images, charts, tables, and maps. Digital versions also use hyperlinks to related topics, which makes the experience more interactive.

## Types of Encyclopedias

- **General encyclopedias** cover a wide variety of subjects for a general audience (for example, *Encyclopædia Britannica*).
- **Subject-specific encyclopedias** focus on one field, such as medicine, law, or philosophy.
- **Print encyclopedias** are traditional multi-volume books.
- **Digital or online encyclopedias** are searchable and often multimedia-rich (for example, Wikipedia).
