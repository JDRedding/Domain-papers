**Museology** is the systematic study of museums, including their design, organization, management, and function. Museology is the academic and practical study of museums, including their history, functions, management, and role in society. Museology combines theory with practical skill so that museums can serve as centers of knowledge, culture, and public enrichment. Museology, also called museum studies, is the discipline concerned with the purpose, operations, and cultural significance of museums. 

It examines how museums collect, preserve, research, interpret, and display objects of historical, artistic, scientific, or cultural value. The field bridges theory and practice: it includes both academic analysis of museums and the skills required for professional museum work.

## Etymology
From *museum* + *-ology*. First recorded in English around 1880–1885. Museology is also called museum studies. It is treated as both an academic field and a professional practice.

## Key Areas

1. **History of museums.** Museology studies the origins and development of museums, from private collections and cabinets of curiosities to modern public institutions, and how their social roles have changed over time.

2. **Collection management.** This covers acquisition, cataloging, documentation, preservation, and conservation, so objects remain available for research and public education.

3. **Exhibition and interpretation.** Museology looks at how museums design exhibitions and interpret collections in order to engage visitors, communicate knowledge, and tell stories. Curatorial strategy, thematic storytelling, and educational programming are central.

4. **Ethics and policy.** Ethical issues include provenance research, protection of cultural heritage, repatriation of artifacts, and public trust. Policy shapes how museums operate while meeting legal, social, and cultural responsibilities.

5. **Education and public engagement.** The field stresses museums as places of learning and community contact, through interactive programs, workshops, digital exhibits, and outreach that reach diverse audiences.

6. **Management and leadership.** Administration, fundraising, marketing, and governance help museums stay sustainable, professionally run, and responsive to social change.

## Areas of Application

People trained in museology work as curators, conservators, educators, exhibition designers, and museum directors. The field also overlaps with archaeology, anthropology, art history, library science, and cultural heritage management.

## Contemporary Trends

Current practice increasingly addresses digital transformation, inclusive representation, community-driven curation, and interactive technology. Digital archives, virtual exhibitions, and online engagement extend museums beyond physical buildings. Sustainability, accessibility, and cultural diversity are also central concerns.
