# Scientific Organizations & Management — 060.6

Leadership and organizational structures in the natural sciences and mathematics include managers who oversee research and development, as well as professional societies, research institutes, and government directorates that support scientific discovery, education, and workforce development.

---

## Natural Sciences Managers

Natural sciences managers plan, supervise, and coordinate activities in fields such as life sciences, physical sciences, mathematics, and statistics. They direct research and development, quality control, and production operations, often working in offices or laboratories.  
*Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics*

### Key Responsibilities

- **Hiring and supervision** — Overseeing engineers, technicians, and scientists.  
- **Research planning** — Directing research, development, and testing activities.  
- **Reporting** — Reviewing and preparing reports on project activities.  
- **Technical guidance** — Collaborating with regulatory, industrial, or research stakeholders.

### Education and Experience

Most natural sciences managers hold at least a bachelor’s degree in a related field and often work as scientists before transitioning into management. Some positions require advanced degrees (master’s, Ph.D., or professional degrees) and more than five years of relevant experience.

## Professional and Mathematical Organizations

Professional societies facilitate research, collaboration, and education in mathematics and the broader natural sciences.

### Mathematical Societies

Organizations supporting research, publications, and conferences include:

- **American Mathematical Society (AMS)**  
- **Mathematical Association of America (MAA)**  
- **National Association of Mathematicians (NAM)**  
- **Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics (SIAM)**  
- **Association for Women in Mathematics (AWM)**  

### Committees and Networks

- **Conference Board of the Mathematical Sciences (CBMS)** — An umbrella organization coordinating multiple member societies to support education, research, and policy development.

### Professional Development

These societies offer mentoring, networking, and competitions—such as the **Putnam Exam**—to support students and early‑career researchers.

---

## Governmental and Research Management Structures

### NSF Directorate for Mathematical and Physical Sciences (MPS)

The **NSF MPS** supports fundamental research in astronomy, chemistry, materials science, mathematics, and physics. It manages research centers, institutes, and infrastructure projects, provides funding, and fosters the U.S. science workforce through education and mentoring programs.

#### Research Focus Areas

Programs span:

- Algebra  
- Number theory  
- Analysis  
- Computational mathematics  
- Statistics and probability  
- Mathematical biology  
- Topology  
- Applied and interdisciplinary mathematics

#### Crosscutting Programs

NSF MPS also implements initiatives aligned with national priorities, including:

- Quantum computing  
- Artificial intelligence  
- Public health modeling  
- Energy research

### University and Corporate Partnerships

Colleges and universities provide advanced STEM education, often partnering with local industries to offer hands‑on experience, internships, and dual‑credit programs.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Natural sciences management** blends technical expertise with leadership and organizational skills to direct R&D and scientific operations.  
- **Mathematics and science societies** play essential roles in research, professional development, inclusion, and policy advocacy.  
- **Government directorates** such as NSF MPS manage large‑scale funding, research infrastructure, and workforce programs that connect theory with practical applications.  
- **Regional STEM alliances** create localized pathways for education, workforce readiness, and youth engagement, bridging academia, industry, and community initiatives.

Together, these management roles and organizational structures form a networked ecosystem that sustains research, educational excellence, and technology‑driven innovation in the natural sciences and mathematics.
