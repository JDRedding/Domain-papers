# 050 General Serial Publications
- 050 Magazines

General serial publications are issued in successive parts at regular or irregular intervals, intended to continue indefinitely, and written for a general audience on broad topics. General serial publications are ongoing periodicals for a broad readership. They include magazines, newspapers, newsletters, and other continuing publications that offer varied content over time.

## Definition and Characteristics

General serials are periodicals released over time as a series. Unlike books or one-off publications, they are continuous: each issue is part of a larger whole. They are typically produced weekly, monthly, quarterly, or annually, though the schedule can vary.

## Distinction from Specialized Serials

General serials target a wide readership. Specialized serials focus on a professional, academic, or technical field—for example, medical journals, scientific reviews, or industry newsletters. Their content is usually more technical and detailed and is written for a niche audience.

Key characteristics include:

- **Successive issues.** Each publication is a new installment in the series, numbered or dated to show its place in the sequence.
- **Indefinite continuity.** There is no planned endpoint; publication continues as long as demand and resources allow.
- **Broad scope.** General serials usually cover wide-ranging topics rather than specialized academic or technical subjects, so they remain accessible to a general audience.
- **Varied content.** They may include articles, essays, news, reviews, stories, illustrations, advertisements, and other features meant to appeal to a broad readership.

## Examples

- **Magazines and periodicals.** Titles such as *Time*, *National Geographic*, and *Reader’s Digest* mix news, features, and cultural content.
- **Newspapers.** Many daily or weekly newspapers are serials that cover current events and general information for the public.
- **Newsletters.** Organizations often produce general newsletters for members, with updates on events, trends, or topics of interest.
- **General journals.** Some journals address broad subjects rather than specialized research and offer commentary on society, culture, or lifestyle.
