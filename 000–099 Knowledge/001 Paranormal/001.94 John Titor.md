# John Titor
- 001.94 — Paranormal claims / pseudoscience
  
Time‑travel folklore, internet mythos.

---

**John Titor** is a mysterious online personality who claimed to be a time traveler from 2036. His posts sparked decades of fascination, detailed predictions, and ongoing debate about whether the story was an elaborate hoax, performance art, or something else. John Titor remains part of internet folklore. Most of his specific predictions — the *2004 civil war*, the *2015 nuclear war* — did ***not*** happen. What lasted was the mix of technical-sounding detail, philosophical talk about time travel, and a story that invited readers to argue, investigate, and keep adding to the myth.

The saga works as both a cautionary tale about taking anonymous online claims at face value and an early example of internet storytelling that blended science fiction, urban legend, and participatory discussion.

In the end, John Titor sits at the intersection of internet hoaxes, early online culture, and the lasting human fascination with time travel. His identity, and the nature of the claims, remain unresolved.

## Origins and Forum Activity

John Titor first appeared in 1998 through faxes sent to radio host Art Bell on *Coast to Coast AM*, claiming that time travel would be invented in 2034 through research at CERN.

The persona began posting more fully under the username **TimeTravel_0** on the Time Travel Institute forums in November 2000. He said he was a U.S. military soldier from 2036, sent back to retrieve an IBM 5100 computer from 1975. According to him, the machine’s hidden capabilities would help solve future computing problems.

By January 2001 he was using the name John Titor and also posting on Art Bell’s Post-2-Post forums. His last known post appeared in March 2001. After that, he vanished from the internet.

## Claims and Time Machine

Titor described a “*stationary mass, temporal displacement unit*” powered by dual top-spin singularities—essentially miniature black holes—capable of bending spacetime.

He said he installed the device in a 1967 Chevrolet Corvette for travel to 1975, then later transferred it to a 1987 four-wheel-drive vehicle during his stopover in 2000. His posts included technical diagrams, operational details, and photographs, which added to the story’s apparent realism.

## Predictions and Worldview

Titor’s predictions included a U.S. civil war beginning around 2004, a nation eventually split into five regions, and a brief nuclear World War III in 2015. He also spoke of societal collapse, Y2K-related crises, and outbreaks such as Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease.

He frequently invoked the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics. In that framing, interacting with the past created divergent timelines—an explanation that later served as a ready-made reason why many of his predictions did not occur in “our” worldline.

One technical detail that later attracted attention was his claim that the IBM 5100 had a hidden ability to debug legacy systems. Programmers familiar with the machine confirmed that such a capability existed, which gave some of his early technical claims an extra layer of credibility.

## Investigations and Speculations

No one has confirmed Titor’s real identity. Investigations have pointed toward Larry Haber, a Florida lawyer, and possibly his brothers as potential creators of the persona. They have consistently denied authorship.

A company associated with the story, the John Titor Foundation, published compilations of the posts. The narrative later inspired literary and pop-culture works, most notably the Japanese visual novel *Steins;Gate*, which uses ideas such as divergent worldlines and a mission to retrieve a vintage computer.

## APPENDIX: Mathematics

**What is actually mathematical:** Kerr, Kerr–Newman, Tipler/van Stockum CTCs, Hawking temperature.  
**What is folklore glued on:** two boxed microsingularities, electron-manifold knobs, offset sinusoid, $2.5\%$ worldline distance, $10$ years/hour. Those last items have **notation**, not a consistent field equation.

### Device statement (his wording)

He called the machine a **stationary-mass temporal displacement unit** (GE Army model **C204**) powered by **two top-spin, dual-positive microsingularities** that produce a **standard offset Tipler sinusoid**.

Components he listed:

1. Magnetic housings for the two microsingularities  
2. Electron-injection manifold (change mass / gravity of the singularities)  
3. Cooling and X-ray venting  
4. VGL — variable gravity lock (gravity sensors)  
5. Four cesium clocks  
6. Three computer units  

Claimed operating numbers:

- System mass $\sim 225\,\mathrm{lb}$ (later retellings go higher)  
- Vertical safe distance $\approx 6\,\mathrm{m}$  
- Peak local pull $\gtrsim 2g$ at full power  
- Travel rate $\approx 10$ years per hour at $100\%$ power  
- Useful jump window $\approx 50$–$60$ years before “confidence” collapses  
- Divergence rule of thumb: $\sim 1\%$ per $60$ years at max power  
- Arrival divergence from his home worldline: $\sim 1$–$2.5\%$  
- “Zero-divergence” target he once quoted: $\approx 0.0002377\%$  

Those last figures are **not** derived from a published metric. They are his claimed calibration numbers.

### Kerr rotating singularity (what “Kerr field / doughnut singularity” means)

Vacuum rotating black hole, Boyer–Lindquist coordinates. Mass $M$, spin parameter $a = J/M$ ($c=G=1$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{d}s^{2}
&= -\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\Sigma}\right)\mathrm{d}t^{2}
-\frac{4Mar\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\phi
+\frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}\,\mathrm{d}r^{2}
+\Sigma\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}
+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\,A\,\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

with

$$
\Sigma = r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,\qquad
\Delta = r^{2}-2Mr+a^{2},\qquad
A = (r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta.
$$

Horizons:

$$
r_{\pm}=M\pm\sqrt{M^{2}-a^{2}}\qquad(a\le M).
$$

Ring singularity:

$$
\Sigma=0 \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad r=0,\;\theta=\pi/2.
$$

That ring — not a torus of finite thickness — is the “doughnut” people map onto his diagrams. Inner region of Kerr also admits closed timelike curves (Carter’s “time machine” region where $g_{\phi\phi}<0$).

**Electron injection** is the charged generalization: **Kerr–Newman**, extra charge $Q$:

$$
\Delta = r^{2}-2Mr+a^{2}+Q^{2}.
$$

Adding charge changes horizon radii. That is the real equation behind “inject electrons to enlarge the inner horizon / change mass and gravity.” In actual GR you cannot freely “tune” a micro-Kerr–Newman object in a box; the posts treat $M,a,Q$ as knobs.

### Tipler sinusoid (what the “offset Tipler” refers to)

Tipler’s time machine is an **infinitely long, rapidly rotating cylinder**. In van Stockum / Tipler-type metrics, the azimuthal Killing vector becomes timelike when rotation is fast enough. Schematic CTC condition for an infinite dust cylinder of radius $R$ and angular speed $\omega$:

$$
\omega R > \tfrac12
$$

(in geometric units used in that literature). Around such a source the light cones tip; a closed loop in $\phi$ can have $\Delta t<0$.

A crude “sinusoid” picture of a standing gravity wave between two rotors (Titor’s verbal model, not a GR solution) is just

$$
\Phi(z,t)\sim A\sin(kz-\omega t+\varphi)
$$

with an **offset** $\varphi$ between the two singularities so the distortion field is not centered on either mass. There is no unique published $\Phi$ from Titor; “offset Tipler sinusoid” is a slogan glued onto Tipler + two Kerr rotors.

### Worldline divergence (his many-worlds bookkeeping)

He treated Everett many-worlds as a tree of worldlines $A,B,C,D,\ldots$ and a scalar “divergence” $D$.

Typical path he described:

- Home line $A$ (2036)  
- Jump to 1975 $\to$ line $B$ with $D(A,B)\approx 2\%$  
- Presence starts $C$  
- Jump to 2000 starts $D$ (the forum worldline)

Return home: reverse along $D\to C\to B$, then forward on $A$.

Claimed scaling (paraphrase of his posts):

$$
D(\Delta\tau)\approx \alpha\,\frac{|\Delta\tau|}{60\,\mathrm{yr}},\qquad \alpha\sim 1\%
$$

at max power, with a hard “breakaway” near $60$ years where landing confidence collapses. He also said remaining on a visited line makes $D$ grow with dwell time. None of this is Everett’s actual math.

Everett many-worlds has **no** unique $2.5\%$ metric. A branch weight for a projector $\Pi$ is

$$
p=\lVert\Pi\lvert\psi\rangle\rVert^{2},
$$

not a percent “distance” between histories. His $D$ is a story parameter.

---

### Clocks and “10 years per hour”

Four cesium clocks + VGL: sample local gravity in pulses; if the sample drifts too far, abort or rewind to last good sample.

If $\tau$ is proper time on the vehicle and $T$ is coordinate “calendar” displacement he claimed:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\approx 10\,\frac{\mathrm{yr}}{\mathrm{h}}\quad\text{at }100\%\text{ power}.
$$

That is an **engineering claim**, not a geodesic equation. In GR a traveler’s proper time is

$$
\tau=\int\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}},
$$

and you do not get a free “years per hour” knob without specifying the metric and the path.

---

### Radiation he waved at (X-ray venting)

A real microscopic horizon would Hawking-radiate. Temperature of a Schwarzschild hole:

$$
T_{H}=\frac{\hbar c^{3}}{8\pi GMk_{B}},\qquad
P\sim\frac{\hbar c^{6}}{15360\pi G^{2}M^{2}}.
$$

For tiny $M$, $T_{H}$ and power explode. He refused size, mass, and Hawking details (“those I must keep to myself”). That omission is why the microsingularity story is not a calculable device.
