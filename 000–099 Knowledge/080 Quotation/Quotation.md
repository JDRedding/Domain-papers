# Quotation
- 080 Quotation

A **quotation** is either the exact words taken from a speech, text, or other work, or a statement. Context decides which sense is meant. 

## **Language and scholarship:** 
An exact reproduced words, properly marked or cited.

| Form | Part of speech |
|---|---|
| quotation | noun |
| quote | verb or informal noun |
| quotational | adjective |
| quotationally | adverb |
| quotation marks | noun (plural) |

Etymology: from Medieval Latin *quotātiō*, from *quotāre* “to quote”; recorded in English from about 1525–1535.

## General definition

In ordinary English, a quotation is a passage or expression reproduced word for word from another source. People use quotations to:

- Cite spoken or written words accurately
- Add authority or support to a statement
- Illustrate a point with an example

Quoted words are usually placed in quotation marks to show that they are not the writer’s own.

## Literary and linguistic use

In writing, quotations are set off from the surrounding text. The main types are:

- **Direct quotation.** A verbatim repetition of someone’s words.  
  Example: She said, “Knowledge is power.”
- **Indirect quotation.** A paraphrase of the original statement.  
  Example: She said that knowledge gives people power.

Related senses include:

- The act or habit of quoting
- An explicit allusion in one work to a passage or element from another
- A short acknowledgment of a source (a citation or credit)
- A misquotation, when the wording is incorrect
- An epigraph, a quotation placed at the beginning of a work

Quotations matter in academic writing, journalism, and creative work because they give credit to sources.
