# Zen and the Art of the Internet - Glossary

This glossary is only a tiny subset of all of the various terms and other things that people regularly use on The Net. For a more complete (and very entertaining) reference, it's suggested you get a copy of The New Hacker's Dictionary, which is based on a VERY large text file called the Jargon File. Edited by Eric Raymond (eric@snark.thyrsus.com), it is available from the MIT Press, Cambridge, Massachusetts, 02142; its ISBN number is 0- 262-68069-6. Also see RFC-1208, A Glossary of Networking Terms.

## **A**

### **:-)**  
A “smiley” used to portray mood in text. Tilt your head left 90° to see the face. Indicates happiness or sarcasm.

### **address resolution**  
Conversion of an Internet address to its physical address. On Ethernet, this requires broadcasting on the LAN.

### **administrivia**  
Administrative tasks related to mailing lists, digests, news gateways, etc.

### **anonymous FTP**  
Public file‑retrieval service (“anon FTP”). See Section 3.2.2.

### **ANSI**  
American National Standards Institute; disseminates standards like ASCII and represents the U.S. in ISO.

### **archie**  
Search service indexing anonymous FTP archives. See Section 3.3.1.

### **archive server**  
Email‑based file transfer service.

### **ARPA**  
Advanced Research Projects Agency; predecessor to DARPA.

### **ARPAnet**  
Early long‑haul packet‑switching network funded by ARPA; foundational to the Internet.

### **asynchronous**  
Byte‑by‑byte transmission not tied to timing signals.

### **auto‑magic**  
Something that happens “automagically” — pseudo‑automatic and too complex to explain simply.

---

## **B**

### **backbone**  
High‑speed network connection linking slower circuits; also used for central hubs.

### **bandwidth**  
Capacity of a medium to transmit signals; informally, the “size” of the Net.

### **BITNET**  
“Because It’s Time Network” — NJE‑based educational network.

### **bounce**  
Returned email due to delivery error.

### **btw**  
“By the way.”

---

## **C**

### **CFV (Call For Votes)**  
Initiates voting for a Usenet newsgroup.

### **ClariNews**  
Fee‑based Usenet newsfeed from ClariNet Communications.

### **client**  
User of a network service; or a computer relying on another for resources.

### **Cyberspace**  
Term coined by William Gibson in *Neuromancer* for the “world” of computers and its society.

---

## **D**

### **datagram**  
Basic Internet information unit containing source, destination, and data.

### **disassembling**  
Converting binary programs into human‑readable machine code.

### **DNS**  
Domain Name System; converts hostnames to IP numbers.

### **domain**  
Part of naming hierarchy; words separated by dots.

### **dotted quad**  
Four numbers separated by periods (e.g., 147.31.254.130).

---

## **E**

### **email**  
Electronic mail.

### **email address**  
UUCP or domain‑based address (e.g., brendan@cs.widener.edu).

### **ethernet**  
10 Mbps LAN technology developed by Xerox; widely adopted and non‑proprietary.

---

## **F**

### **FDDI**  
Fiber Distributed Data Interface; 100 Mbps fiber‑optic network standard using token ring access.

### **flame**  
Violently argumentative email or Usenet post.

### **FQDN**  
Fully Qualified Domain Name (e.g., lisa.cs.widener.edu).

### **FTP**  
File Transfer Protocol; standard Internet file transfer method.

### **FYI**  
“For your information”; also a series of RFCs for new users.

---

## **G**

### **gateway**  
Dedicated computer connecting multiple networks and routing IP datagrams.

---

## **H**

### **header**  
Packet portion containing addresses and error‑checking fields; also part of email/news messages.

### **hostname**  
Name assigned to a machine.

### **IMHO**  
“In my humble opinion.”

---

## **I**

### **Internet**  
Concatenation of many TCP/IP networks into one logical network.

### **Internet number**  
Dotted‑quad IP address; resolver translates names to numbers.

### **interoperate**  
Ability of multi‑vendor systems to work together via common protocols.

### **ISO**  
International Organization for Standardization.

---

## **K**

### **kernel**  
Core of an OS containing device drivers, memory management, scheduler, and system calls.

---

## **L**

### **LAN**  
Local Area Network; high‑speed network over short distances.

---

## **M**

### **mail gateway**  
Machine connecting dissimilar mail systems.

### **mailing list**  
Email‑distributed discussion group.

### **mail path**  
Series of machine names used to route email.

### **medium**  
Material supporting data transmission (copper, fiber, microwave, etc.).

### **multiplex**  
Dividing a medium into multiple logical channels.

---

## **N**

### **net.citizen**  
Inhabitant of Cyberspace; expected to behave well.

### **netiquette**  
Etiquette for online behavior.

### **network**  
Group of machines connected to transmit information.

### **NFS**  
Network File System; makes remote files appear local.

### **NIC**  
Network Information Center.

### **node**  
Computer attached to a network.

### **NSFnet**  
National backbone network funded by NSF.

---

## **P**

### **packet**  
Unit of data sent across packet‑switching networks.

### **polling**  
Connecting to another system to check for mail or news.

### **postmaster**  
Person responsible for mail problems and user queries.

### **protocols**  
Formal descriptions of message formats and rules for communication.

---

## **R**

### **recursion**  
Language facility allowing functions to call themselves.

### **resolve**  
Translate Internet name to IP address.

### **RFD**  
Request For Discussion; period for debating newsgroup creation.

### **route**  
Path network traffic takes.

### **router**  
Device sending packets based on network state.

### **RTFM**  
“Read The Fantastic Manual” (usually more vulgar).

---

## **S**

### **SMTP**  
Simple Mail Transfer Protocol; standard for email transfer.

### **server**  
Computer sharing resources with others.

### **signal‑to‑noise ratio**  
Amount of useful information vs. total activity.

### **signature**  
Small message appended to email or Usenet posts.

### **summarize**  
Condense responses into one coherent message.

### **synchronous**  
Fixed‑rate transmissions with synchronized devices.

---

## **T**

### **TCP/IP**  
Protocol suite supporting telnet, FTP, SMTP, etc.

### **telnet**  
Remote terminal protocol.

### **terminal server**  
Specialized computer connecting many terminals to a LAN.

### **TEX**  
Free typesetting system by Donald Knuth.

### **twisted pair**  
Two insulated copper wires twisted to reduce noise.

---

## **U**

### **UUCP**  
Unix‑to‑Unix Copy Program; store‑and‑forward system.

---

## **W**

### **WAN**  
Wide‑Area Network spanning large distances.

### **workstation**  
High‑power networked personal computing device.

### **worm**  
Self‑replicating program; the Internet Worm was a famous example.

### **wrt**  
“With respect to.”
