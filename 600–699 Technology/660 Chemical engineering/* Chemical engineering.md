# Chemical engineering
- 660 Chemical engineering

**Chemical engineering** is the branch of engineering that designs, optimizes, and manages processes that turn raw materials into useful products, with attention to safety, efficiency, and environmental cost. Chemical engineering is a broad applied discipline. It joins science, engineering method, and design to make the products and processes of modern life—safely and at scale—and it matters in manufacturing, health, energy, the environment, and new technology.

## What Chemical Engineering Is

Chemical engineering draws on chemistry, physics, biology, and mathematics to invent and run processes that make chemicals, fuels, pharmaceuticals, foods, and advanced materials. Engineers in the field design systems of reaction, heat and mass transfer, fluid flow, and materials processing so that laboratory chemistry can be carried out at industrial scale.

### Skills

Useful strengths include:

- firm grounding in chemistry, physics, and mathematics
- analysis and problem-solving
- modeling, simulation, and process software
- knowledge of safety rules and environmental law
- clear communication and work across disciplines

## Core Areas

- **Process design and optimization.** Development of processes from laboratory scale to plant: reactors, distillation columns, heat exchangers, piping, and control systems.
- **Thermodynamics and transport phenomena.** Energy, momentum, and mass transfer used to understand and improve processes.
- **Reaction engineering.** Kinetics and reactor design aimed at yield, selectivity, and safety.
- **Materials science.** Properties and processing of polymers, metals, ceramics, and composites.
- **Biochemical engineering.** Application of the same methods to living systems: fermentation, biopharmaceuticals, and biofuels.
- **Environmental engineering.** Pollution control, water treatment, carbon capture, and cleaner manufacture.

## Applications

Chemical engineers work across many industries:

- **Energy and fuels.** Petroleum refining, biofuels, and other energy technologies
- **Pharmaceuticals.** Drug manufacture and quality control
- **Food and beverage.** Scale-up of ingredients, drinks, and flavors
- **Materials and polymers.** Plastics, composites, adhesives, and specialty chemicals
- **Environment.** Wastewater, air pollution, and greener process design

## Education and Careers

Entry usually requires a bachelor’s degree in chemical engineering or a closely related field, and in some roles professional licensure. Master’s and doctoral degrees are common in research, teaching, and specialized industry.

Typical titles include process engineer, plant manager, quality engineer, environmental engineer, pharmaceutical process engineer, and research scientist. Workplaces range from plants and biotech laboratories to consulting firms.
