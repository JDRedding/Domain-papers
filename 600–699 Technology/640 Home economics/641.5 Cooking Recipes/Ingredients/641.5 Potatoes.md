# Potatoes
600–699 Technology/641.5 Cooking Recipes

Potatoes are a versatile, nutrient-rich staple with a rich history spanning thousands of years, offering countless culinary possibilities from simple roasted dishes to innovative desserts. Potatoes were first cultivated in the Andes Mountains of South America, particularly in present-day Peru and Bolivia, over 7,000–8,000 years ago. They were domesticated by the Incas and Mayans, and introduced to Europe in the 16th century by Spanish explorers. The potato has since become a global staple and was even the first vegetable grown in space.

### Varieties

There are over 4,000 potato varieties worldwide, ranging in color, taste, and texture. Common types include:

- **Russet** — starchy; ideal for baking and frying  
- **Yukon Gold** — creamy; good for mashing and roasting  
- **Red** — waxy; suitable for salads and soups  
- **Fingerling** — small, firm; gourmet use  
- **Colorful varieties** — purple or blue potatoes with high antioxidant content  

### Nutrition

Potatoes are composed of about 80% water and 20% solids, rich in carbohydrates, fiber, and essential vitamins and minerals such as vitamin C, B6, potassium, magnesium, and phosphorus. They are naturally fat-free and cholesterol-free.

A medium-sized baked potato provides roughly 161 calories, 36.6 grams of carbohydrates, 4.3 grams of protein, 3.8 grams of fiber, and 1,640 mg of potassium — more than a medium banana. Potatoes support heart health, digestion, and immunity, and when cooled after cooking, they contain resistant starch that benefits digestive health.

### Storage and Safety

Store potatoes in a cool, dark, and well-ventilated space to prevent sprouting and decay. Avoid storing near onions, as this accelerates spoilage. Do not consume green or sprouted portions due to solanine toxicity, which can cause nausea, abdominal pain, and headaches.

Potatoes should be procured in the fall, as soon as gathered, and stored in a dry cellar or storeroom, where they will be protected from frost and light to prevent freezing and sprouting. Potatoes that have been in cold storage too long, as a rule, turn dark and form black lumps inside and when cooked have a dull, watery appearance, and are therefore not fit to be eaten.

The inside of a good potato, properly boiled, should be white, or of the natural color; if baked, white and mealy, not dark.

### Cultural and Scientific Impact

Potatoes have played major roles in agriculture, economy, and culture. They were crucial during European famines and have inspired culinary innovations worldwide. The potato can also be used creatively outside cooking, including in biodegradable plastics and as a source of electricity in potato batteries.

## Culinary Uses

### Classic Dishes

- **Mashed Potatoes:** Boil potatoes, mash with butter and milk, season with salt, pepper, or garlic for a creamy, comforting side.  
- **Roasted Potatoes:** Coat bite-sized chunks in olive oil and herbs, and roast at high heat until golden and crisp.  
- **Baked Potato:** Bake russets at 425°F for 45–60 minutes with skin on; serve with toppings like cheese, sour cream, chives, or black beans.  
- **French Fries & Chips:** Fry or bake sliced potatoes for crispy snacks or sides.  
- **Potato Salad:** Boiled potatoes mixed with mayonnaise, mustard, or vinaigrette and herbs for a refreshing side.  

### Creative and Gourmet Recipes

- **Potato Chip Omelette:** Incorporates crushed chips into a classic omelette for crunch and flavor.  
- **Cacio e Pepe Tater Tots:** Tater tots enhanced with Grana Padano cheese and a spiced aioli.  
- **Chocolate Spud Bundt Cake or Potato Chip Cookies:** Utilizes mashed potatoes in desserts to add moisture and texture.  
- **Potato Diamonds:** Fingerling potatoes cooked in whey and butter, topped with luxury ingredients like caviar.  

### Cooking Tips

- Leave skins on to retain nutrients.  
- Wash potatoes only before cooking.  
- Store in cool, dark places to extend shelf life.

## Potato Recipes

Potatoes’ versatility makes them ideal for virtually any meal, from breakfast to dinner, and they can be incorporated into both traditional and innovative dishes. Combining nutrient density, affordability, and global availability, potatoes remain a culinary staple and a fascinating ingredient to explore in recipes.

---

### Boiled Potatoes

Peel the potatoes and let stand in cold water for at least half an hour before boiling. Put in a kettle, cover with cold water, and boil until done, from thirty to forty minutes. When cooked, drain, remove the cover, and shake the potatoes in the kettle to let the steam escape, which will make them light and mealy.

---

### Baked Potatoes

Select large, smooth potatoes, wash, and bake without removing the skins. They bake more quickly if a thin slice is cut from each end.

---

### Mashed Potatoes

Peel, wash, and boil until done; drain and mash thoroughly, season with salt and pepper, butter and milk or cream. Stir until light, put in a dish with small bits of butter over the top, and serve.

Mashed potatoes pressed through a colander or potato press on the middle of the dish form a mound of light flakes like rice, and if surrounded with chops, have a dainty appearance.

---

### Potatoes Baked with Meat

Peel and wash medium-sized potatoes, put in with roast meat, and roast for thirty or forty minutes, basting frequently.

---

### Fried Potatoes

Slice cold potatoes extra thin and put in a frying pan with hot melted butter. Fry to a golden brown and keep from scorching, tossing or turning them over with a broad knife. Serve hot.

---

#### French Fried Potatoes

Peel and cut potatoes into narrow strips lengthwise, wash and drain, dry upon a napkin, then plunge into hot fat and fry to a nice brown. Take out with a wire skimmer, drain in a colander, sprinkle with salt, and serve hot.

---

### Creamed Potatoes

Cut into cubes (dice shape) or slice cold boiled potatoes, not too well done. Heat in white sauce made with milk or cream, dish, sprinkle with parsley, and serve. The parsley may be cooked in the sauce.

---

### Potatoes au Gratin

*(o gra-tang)*

Chop up Irish potatoes and cook in cream. Then place in a porcelain or silver dish, sprinkling melted butter and grated cheese over the top; bake and serve in the same dish.

---

### Stewed Potatoes

Take cold boiled potatoes, cut into small pieces, and place in a granite baking pan. To 1 quart of potatoes add a scant 1 1/2 cupful of butter, 1 large cupful of sweet milk into which 1 small tablespoonful of flour has been stirred, 1 onion chopped very fine, and salt and pepper to taste. Mix all well together, pour into a buttered baking dish, and bake half an hour.

---

### Lyonnaise Potatoes

Slice and cut into small cubes (dice shape) cold boiled potatoes. Melt butter or drippings in a frying pan, fry 1 small chopped onion to a light yellow color, put in the potatoes, sprinkle with salt and pepper, and cook gently about five minutes, constantly stirring with a fork. Do not break the dice nor allow to brown. Just before dishing up, stir in an ample quantity of finely chopped parsley. Drain in a heated colander and put in a heated dish, as they must be served hot.

---

### Duchess Potatoes

Boil 2 pounds of mealy, peeled potatoes until done; drain perfectly, put in the oven for a minute to dry. Rub quickly through a wire sieve, mix in 3 yolks of eggs, 2 oz. melted butter, and 1 tablespoonful of cream. Season with salt, pepper, and grated nutmeg. When smooth, pour out on a floured board, divide into 12 equal parts; give each part a square shape, trace some design on each with a knife or other tool, put them on a buttered paper in a shallow pan, brush the surface with egg, and bake in a hot oven to a nice color. Serve hot.

---

### Herring Potatoes

Boil potatoes in salted water, peel and slice while still hot. Fry sliced onions to a nice yellow in butter, work in a little flour, add vinegar, salt, pepper, and bay leaf (leaf of laurel tree) to taste, and finally the hot sliced potatoes with enough boiling water to nearly cover the potatoes. Cut fine 1 or 2 herrings, removing all bones carefully, stir in the potatoes without breaking, then add a little cream. When hot, serve with boiled beef or mutton. Should be quite juicy.

---

### Ham Potatoes

Cut the less valuable parts of a boiled ham into small cubes (dice shape). In a well-buttered baking dish put a layer of freshly boiled potatoes, sliced while still hot, then a layer of the ham with fried sliced onions and bits of butter, then again potatoes and so on until all is in, having potatoes and butter on top. Put in the oven until heated through, then pour in 3 eggs, well beaten with 3 cups of milk (for four persons) and a little salt and nutmeg. Sprinkle with bread crumbs, heat again in the oven, and serve. Chopped herring may be mixed with the ham. In this case no salt will be needed.

---

### Princess Potatoes

Substitute well-watered and chopped herring for the ham in the preceding recipe, omit the beaten egg and milk, and use more butter. Excellent with boiled beef and mutton.

---

### Curled Potatoes

Pare large, smooth potatoes and place in cold water for a short time, then continue paring like an apple, keeping the parings as long and thick as possible. Fry in a wire basket in hot lard and when golden brown, place on paper to absorb the grease. Mostly used to garnish fish or steak.

---

### Paris Potatoes

These are cut out of large potatoes with a special tool called a scoop. They are perfectly round little balls. In parboiling them they should be brought to the boil quickly, then fried or baked ten minutes and served as garnish.

---

### Saratoga Chips

Peel raw even-sized potatoes, slice very thin, dry carefully on a cloth. Put in a frying basket and plunge into boiling hot fat. When almost done, lift the basket out for three minutes to allow the fat to get hot again. Then plunge the basket in once more and leave it till the potatoes have a golden brown color. Take out, shake well, sprinkle with fine salt, and serve at once.

If not eaten at once they will keep several days. If they get soft, put them in a hot oven for a few seconds.

---

### Potato Puffs

Pare and boil 4 large mealy potatoes. When done mash thoroughly (if necessary put through a colander or sieve). Beat until light and creamy. Add 1 well-beaten egg and about 1/2 cupful sweet cream. Pepper and salt to taste. Beat thoroughly, shape into balls with a spoon dipped in melted butter. Place the balls on a buttered pan and bake in a hot oven for about fifteen minutes.

---

### Potato Croquettes

To 3 cupfuls of mashed potato add 2 beaten eggs, a little chopped parsley, and salt and pepper to taste; mix thoroughly and form into croquettes, roll in flour, and fry in hot lard or drippings to a nice golden brown. Serve on a hot dish, garnish with parsley. Two tablespoonfuls of cream mixed in forms a nice addition.

---

#### Potato Croquettes No. 2

Peel 5 medium-sized raw potatoes, cut into quarters, rinse, put on the fire in cold salted water, and boil till almost done. Drain, spread on a flat dish, put in the oven one minute to get perfectly dry, and rub through a sieve. Make a thin paste of 1 1/2 cup of water, 1 whole egg, 1 yolk, a little butter and flour; stir in the potato and 2 oz. of grated Parmesan cheese; season with pepper and a little nutmeg, and spread on a buttered dish to cool. Shape into croquettes on a floured board, egg and crumb, put in a frying basket, fry in hot fat to a nice golden color, drain on cloth or paper, and sprinkle with salt. Serve hot, garnished with parsley.

Any good leftover cooked meat, chopped fine, may be mixed with the potatoes.

---

### Warmed-Over Potatoes

Use cold potatoes, boiled in their skins or baked the day before, but not overdone. Peel off the skins without cutting, and cut in slices 1/4 inch thick. Put in a chopping bowl, season with pepper and salt, run the chopper over them one way so as to cut each slice once or twice, then run it across once at right angles, cutting all into small dice.

Put good butter (the size of an egg for 1 quart of potato) in a saucepan on the fire; when it begins to melt pour in 1 cup of fresh milk. As soon as boiled up, put in the potato, stir gently with a fork. When moistened, cover up and put on a warm part of the stove. After a few minutes stir carefully from the bottom, then cover tightly. Serve hot.

---

### Stuffed Potatoes

Bake large smooth potatoes until done, then with a sharp knife cut off one end, hold the potato with a napkin, and scoop out the inside; mash, season with butter or cream, salt and pepper, or prepared paprika sauce, and return to the shells. Fasten on the covers with toothpicks and bake for five minutes longer. Any good cold meat such as chicken or veal can be mixed with the potato, and a beaten egg forms a nice addition.

---

### Scalloped Potatoes

Peel and slice small potatoes, wash, and place a layer in a baking dish; season with salt and pepper, and put small bits of butter on the top. Continue these layers until the dish is full. Then pour in enough milk to almost cover the potatoes, put in the oven, and bake three-quarters of an hour.

For a change, sprinkle grated cheese or chopped ham on each layer.

---

### Potato Cakes

Take cold mashed potatoes and form into small cakes with the hands. Put 1 spoonful of drippings into a hot frying pan, put in the cakes, and fry both sides to a nice brown; serve at once.

---

### Baked Potato Balls

Take warm mashed potatoes, form into round balls with the hands, roll in flour, place in rows in a baking pan, and bake in a hot oven for fifteen minutes. Serve with drawn butter sauce.

---

### Potato Soufflé

Work the yolks of 2 eggs into 2 cupfuls of hot, well-mashed and seasoned moist potatoes. Allow to cool a little and stir in lightly the 2 whites of egg beaten stiff. Put in a pudding dish and brown in a hot oven.

---

### Potatoes à la O’Brien

Boil 1 quart peeled Irish potatoes one-half hour, mash smooth, add 1 small raw chopped onion, 1 small chopped pepper, 1/8 pound butter, and 1 1/2 pint cream, and beat with a fork five minutes over the fire to keep hot. Serve at once.

---

### Sweet Potatoes

Wash and trim the sweet potatoes. Usually baked in the skins, and require from an hour to an hour and a quarter according to thickness. They may be boiled from forty to fifty minutes, and when soft should be placed in the oven for a few minutes to dry. Leftover sweet potatoes can be sliced and fried brown in butter.

Another good way is to pare the potatoes whole and boil till tender, but not cooked too much. Then take out, cut in halves lengthwise, put in a dripping pan with sufficient butter, pepper, salt, and 1 teaspoonful of sugar, and bake to a brown.
