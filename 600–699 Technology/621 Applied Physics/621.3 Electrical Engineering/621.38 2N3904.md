# 2N3904
- 621.38 electronics technology

The **2N3904** is a widely used, industry‑standard silicon NPN bipolar junction transistor (BJT) designed for **low‑power amplification** and **medium‑speed switching**.  

It’s a “jellybean” device — cheap, reliable, and available from nearly every manufacturer — making it a staple for engineers and hobbyists alike.

---

### 📐 Outline & Pinout
The TO-92 pin order is conventionally stated as C–B–E when looking at the underside (leads toward you) with the flat side oriented in the standard way used by many datasheets. When the flat side faces you and the leads point downward, the order is frequently shown as Emitter–Base–Collector (left to right) on some manufacturers’ drawings. Always verify the particular datasheet or mark the leads — especially because the complementary 2N3906 and the 2N2222/PN2222A family can reverse or rearrange the outer pins depending on the package and maker.

| Dimension | Typical Value |
|------------|----------------|
| Body width | 4.5 mm |
| Body height | 4.5 mm |
| Lead length | ≈ 15 mm (varies) |

**Pinout (underside view):**  
C – B – E (Collector, Base, Emitter)

This TO‑92 layout is standard for the 2N3904; note that similar BJTs like the **2N2222** use different lead orders.

### ⚙️ Electrical Specifications
| Parameter | Typical Value | Notes |
|------------|----------------|-------|
| **VCEO** | 40 V | Collector‑Emitter voltage |
| **VCBO** | 60 V | Collector‑Base voltage |
| **IC (max)** | 200 mA | Continuous collector current |
| **hFE** | 100–300 | DC current gain at 10 mA |
| **fT** | 300 MHz | Transition frequency |
| **Noise figure** | 5 dB | Suitable for low‑noise preamps |

Also available as **MMBT3904** in SOT‑23 surface‑mount form.

- **V<sub>CEO</sub>** = 40 V, **V<sub>CBO</sub>** = 60 V, **V<sub>EBO</sub>** = 6 V  
- **I<sub>C</sub> continuous** = 200 mA (peak often listed ~300 mA)  
- **h<sub>FE</sub>** at I<sub>C</sub> = 10 mA, V<sub>CE</sub> = 1 V: min 100, max 300 (typical ~200–250 in the mid-range)  
- **f<sub>T</sub>** ≥ 300 MHz (IC = 10 mA, V<sub>CE</sub> = 20 V)  
- Noise figure typically ~4–5 dB under the usual low-current test conditions  
- Power dissipation ~625 mW in free air (TO-92)

### 🔧 Key Advantages
- **Dual‑use versatility** — reliable in both analog and digital domains.  
- **Low noise** — ideal for sensor and audio front‑ends.  
- **Complementary pairing** — works seamlessly with the 2N3906 PNP transistor.  
- **Cost‑effective ubiquity** — available from nearly every distributor worldwide.

---

### 🧰 Typical Applications
- **Audio preamps** and small‑signal amplifiers  
- **Sensor conditioning** and interface circuits  
- **Relay/LED drivers** for low‑current loads  
- **Logic level shifting** and digital switching  
- **Low‑frequency RF stages** in receivers/transmitters  

---

### 🔄 Complementary Pair: 2N3904 + 2N3906
The **2N3906** PNP transistor mirrors the 2N3904’s characteristics, enabling:
- Push‑pull emitter followers  
- Class‑AB small‑signal amplifiers  
- Bidirectional level shifters  
- Symmetric analog front‑ends

### 🧠 Design Insight
Practical design

- For saturated switching the forced-beta rule of thumb (IB ≈ IC/10 to IC/20) is safer than relying on the minimum hFE alone, because gain falls at higher current and temperature.
- The simple base-resistor equation given is correct; remember to include the voltage drop of whatever is driving the base and any series resistance in the drive path.
- It pairs cleanly with the 2N3906 for complementary stages. The surface-mount equivalent (MMBT3904 / SOT-23) is essentially the same die in a smaller package and is still very common.

For switching applications:

$$
I_B = \frac{I_C}{h_{FE}}
$$

Example: \(I_C = 100\,\text{mA}\), \(h_{FE} = 200 \Rightarrow I_B = 0.5\,\text{mA}\)

Base resistor sizing:

$$
R_B = \frac{V_{drive} - V_{BE}}{I_B}
$$

This ensures saturation and reliable switching.
