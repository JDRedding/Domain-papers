# Salads
600–699 Technology/641.5 Cooking Recipes

Salads are versatile dishes typically composed of raw or cooked vegetables, fruits, proteins, grains, or combinations thereof, offering both freshness and nutritional benefits. Contrary to the common idea, salads are not difficult to make, if the recipes are carefully followed. Pure vinegar, salt, pepper, and mustard prevent the fermentation of fresh vegetables in the stomach, but they should not be used to excess, and good oil is very nutritious and aids digestion. A salad, therefore, should form part of every midday or evening meal.

Salads are highly adaptable, allowing you to create nutritious meals that suit any taste, dietary goal, or occasion while providing a convenient way to increase daily fruit and vegetable intake.

Salads should be cold and never served or put on the table without dressing of some kind. French dressing is generally preferred. Of the different oils, olive is the best. Cotton-seed oil and beech oil are cheaper and preferable to adulterated and rancid olive oil. Pure melted butter is also recommended. Lettuce forms the basis for most all salads. Serve crisp.


## Preparation Tips

- **Freshness:** Use fresh, seasonal ingredients for the best taste and maximum nutrients.  
- **Texture Balance:** Combine crunchy elements (nuts, seeds, raw vegetables) with softer textures (avocado, cooked grains, or cheese).  
- **Flavor Mix:** Mix mild greens with bold herbs like cilantro, parsley, or basil. Incorporate acid (lemon, vinegar) and healthy fats (olive oil, nuts) to enhance flavor.  
- **Dressing Moderation:** Dressings can add calories; lighter vinaigrettes or homemade yogurt dressings are often healthier than creamy store-bought options.  

## Health Benefits

- **Rich in Nutrients:** Salads are high in vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants from vegetables and fruits.  
- **Supports Digestion:** Fiber from greens, vegetables, fruits, and legumes promotes digestive health.  
- **Weight Management:** Low-calorie salad bases with lean proteins can aid satiety while reducing caloric intake.  
- **Heart Health:** Ingredients like leafy greens, avocado, olive oil, and nuts contribute to cardiovascular wellness.  

## Types of Salads

- **Green Salads:** Focused on leafy greens such as lettuce, spinach, arugula, kale, and mixed greens. Common additions include cucumbers, tomatoes, bell peppers, and avocado. These are often served with a variety of dressings like vinaigrette or creamy ranch.  
- **Vegetable Salads:** Include raw, cooked, or roasted vegetables beyond leafy greens, such as carrots, cauliflower, broccoli, and beans. They can be tossed with olive oil, lemon juice, herbs, or light dressings to enhance flavor.  
- **Fruit Salads:** Composed mainly of fresh or canned fruits like berries, apples, melon, grapes, and citrus. Often paired with yogurt or a light honey dressing for sweetness.  
- **Protein-Based Salads:** Incorporate proteins such as chicken, tuna, eggs, tofu, or legumes (e.g., chickpeas, kidney beans) to make a more filling meal or side dish. Examples include Cobb salad or tuna salad.  
- **Grain or Pasta Salads:** Made with cooked grains like quinoa, couscous, rice, or pasta, mixed with vegetables, herbs, and sometimes proteins for a hearty option suitable for meals or picnics.  

#### Tips for Variety

- Add different proteins each week (eggs, fish, chicken, tofu) to prevent monotony.  
- Incorporate seasonal fruits and vegetables to maintain flavor and freshness.  
- Experiment with international influences, such as Mediterranean (feta, olives, cucumber) or Asian (ginger dressing, sesame seeds, cabbage) styles.

### The Merry Widow Salad

Use a glass bowl about six inches across in which insert another one two sizes smaller, allowing about one inch space between the two bowls, which should be filled with cracked ice to come up even with the top or a little higher, to make a contrast. Care must be taken not to let the water run over the edge of the outside bowl.

Place in the smaller bowl a lining of crisp lettuce leaves, then several pieces of the solid white meat of fresh lobster or crab. Between each piece of the meat, put asparagus cut the proper length so that the tips will hang a little over the edge of the glass. In the center place small chopped carrots, turnips, French peas, and French string beans (already prepared). Pour a little French dressing over the salad, then put one spoonful of mayonnaise or some other equally good dressing in the center.

To make more decorative, stick sprigs of parsley in the ice and limes or lemons cut in halves or quarters with the peeling on or off as preferred.

### Merry Widow Salad No. 2

Line a salad dish with crisp fresh lettuce; then take 3 pieces of sliced orange and arrange around the side of the dish to come up to the edge, to form a contrast with the lettuce. Fill in with slices of one banana cut round and about six strawberries. Cover with whipped cream (cream beaten to a froth) and place on top one or two red cherries.

If the fruits are choice and the directions carefully followed, this will be a novel and appetizing dish. Ordinarily regarded as a portion for one, but really sufficient for two.

### Lettuce and Tomato Salad

Clean and wash the lettuce, shake dry, and put in the ice box. Peel the tomatoes and put on ice. When ready to serve, dress the lettuce with vinegar, oil (or melted butter), salt, and pepper. Arrange the lettuce in the salad bowl, quarter or slice the tomatoes, and arrange in the middle of the bowl. Serve with mayonnaise dressing.

### Wilted Lettuce Creamed

Take 3 thin slices of salt pork and put in the frying pan. Heat until the grease fries out, then turn into the pan 1 cup of sour or sweet cream, 3 tablespoonfuls of vinegar, and 1 tablespoonful of sugar, and let them get hot. Wash the lettuce, place in a separate dish, pour the hot cream over it, and serve.

### Spinach Salad

Take 1/2 peck of fresh, crisp spinach. Wash thoroughly in several waters, put in a steamer, and steam for about ten minutes. Turn into a colander and drain; then chop fine, season with salt, pepper, and 2 tablespoonfuls of melted butter. Mix well and press into small molds or cups. When cold, place each form on a lettuce leaf and put 1 spoonful of good salad dressing on each.

### Potato Salad

Boil small potatoes in the skin. When quite done, drain, peel, and slice thin while hot. Fry some lean bacon (2 or 3 oz. for every pound of potato), cut dice shape. When almost brown, put in 1/2 small onion chopped fine, cook a moment, and pour it all over the warm potatoes. Mix well by shaking and tossing, and season with salt and pepper.

Make a mixture of 2 parts of mayonnaise with 1 part of vinegar and pour over the potatoes. Mix well by shaking and tossing. Sprinkle with chopped parsley and serve as it is, or cold, but never ice-cold.

Instead of mayonnaise, some good stock may be used.

### Potato Salad No. 2

Cut into 1/2-inch cubes (dice shaped) 2 quarts of cold boiled potatoes, 1 large Spanish onion, 2 heads of celery, and 4 hard-boiled eggs. Season with salt, pepper, and a little cayenne.

Melt in a stewpan a lump of butter the size of an egg, add 1 tablespoonful of flour; cook, but do not brown; then add 1 cupful of milk or water. Beat the yolks of 2 eggs with 1 tablespoonful of sugar and 1 teaspoonful of mustard; add 2/3 of a cupful of vinegar, and stir all in with the sauce in the stewpan. Let it come to a boil, stirring all the time, and set away to cool. When cold, pour over the remainder of the salad, mix well, and serve.

### Cabbage Salad

Chop 1/2 medium-sized head of cabbage extra fine; add 4 teaspoonfuls of celery seed, or 1 head of celery well cut. Beat in a bowl the yolks of 2 eggs, add 1 teaspoonful each of sugar, butter, pepper, mustard, and 2/3 of a cup of vinegar. Set the bowl in hot water and stir until thick. Set aside, and when cold, pour over the cabbage. Mix well and serve.

---

### Cabbage Salad No. 2

Take 2 quarts of finely chopped cabbage, and season with 2 level teaspoonfuls of salt, 2 of white sugar, 1 of black pepper, and 1 of ground mustard. Rub the yolks of 4 hard-boiled eggs until smooth; add 1/2 cupful of butter slightly warmed; mix thoroughly with the cabbage; then add 1 teacupful of good cider vinegar. Serve with whites of eggs, sliced and placed on the salad.

### Cucumber Salad

Peel the cucumbers thinly, cut into thin slices, pour over 2 parts of oil and 1 part of vinegar well mixed, season with pepper, salt, and a little chopped parsley, mix well by tossing or stirring, and set on ice a short while. Just before serving, dust over some more pepper. Lettuce or celery may be used instead of parsley.

It is the wrong idea to suppose that cucumbers are unwholesome, or that the juice should be squeezed out before eating them. People with whom cucumbers do not agree, of course, should not eat them. Cucumbers should be fresh and not too ripe.

### Cucumber and Celery Salad

Cut white crisp celery stalks into long shreds and put in ice water for half an hour. Peel and cut 1 cucumber the same way, leaving out the seed part, and put with the drained celery in a salad bowl. Season with salt, pepper, and mayonnaise. Heap up in the center neatly, surround with red radishes, garnish with slices of hard-boiled eggs, and sprinkle with a little chopped parsley and gherkins. One part vinegar and 2 parts oil will take the place of the mayonnaise.

### Carrot Salad

Take young carrots, wash, boil until tender, then slice very thin. In each individual salad dish put a spoonful of the carrot on a bed of crisp lettuce leaves (the white leaves of head lettuce look best), and put a spoonful of salad dressing on top of each one.

### Egg Salad

Stir together 1 teaspoonful of mustard, 1 teaspoonful of salt, two-thirds cupful of vinegar, and a lump of butter the size of a walnut. Cook in a double boiler, stirring all the time, and when cooked, stir in 1 1/2 cupful of hot sweet milk, then set aside to cool. When ready to serve, add 1/2 dozen hard-boiled eggs chopped fine and garnish with nice crisp lettuce leaves and sliced red radishes, pickled beets, capers, olives, etc.

---

### Scrambled Egg Salad

Select nice, even-sized tomatoes, cut in halves, scoop out the pulp, and fill the hollows with scrambled egg well seasoned. When cold, spread enough mayonnaise on each to cover the egg, and if you have it put a thin layer of aspic on top. Arrange neatly in a circle on a cold dish, garnish with gherkins and beets cut in fancy shapes, fill the center with lettuce and sliced tomatoes all cut in fine strips and seasoned with pepper, salt, oil, and vinegar, and serve cold.

### Chicken Salad

Remove the skin from 2 boiled chickens, and cut the meat fine with a knife; cut up 2 or 3 heads of celery and add to the chicken; season with salt, common pepper, or cayenne, or a little of both if preferred; pour over the whole cold mayonnaise dressing, mix, and serve.

---

### Chicken Salad No. 2

For 1 pair of boiled fowls allow 3 heads of celery. Take the skin from the chickens, pick the meat from the bones, chop fine, and mix with cut celery; cut the white meat in 1 1/2-inch cubes (dice shape) and add. Boil the livers, rub through a sieve, and put in a bowl rubbed with a bit of onion; add the yolks of 5 hard-boiled eggs rubbed to a paste, 4 tablespoonfuls of salad oil or melted butter, 2 tablespoonfuls of prepared mustard, 1 of sugar, 1 heaping teaspoonful of salt, a little cayenne pepper, 1 level teaspoonful of grated lemon peel, and 1 teaspoonful each of vinegar and thick cream. Beat well together and mix with the chicken just before serving.

### Apple Salad

Pare 6 or 8 tart apples and cut into extra thin slices; cover the bottom of a salad dish with a layer of these slices and sprinkle with a little powdered sugar and a dust of cinnamon, then another layer of apples and so on until all are used. Pour over this a pint of unfermented grape juice and place on the ice for one hour before using. Or, alternate the layers of apple with layers of banana.
