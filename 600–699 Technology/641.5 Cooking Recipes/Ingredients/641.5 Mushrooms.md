# Mushrooms
600–699 Technology/641.5 Cooking Recipes

Many people are prejudiced against fresh mushrooms because there are so many poisonous varieties. But mushrooms contain a large percentage of nitrogen, are therefore nourishing, and most of the edible ones have a delicious flavor. Care must be taken, however, that they are entirely sound and fresh, because many cases of illness have resulted from eating mushrooms that were decayed or worm-eaten.

The common mushroom of the market is *Agaricus campestris*, found in the fall on rich, moist pastures in all parts of the world, and frequently cultivated. If boiled, they make a nice addition to soups, gravies, catsup, and many fancy dishes. Best fried: remove the stalks, scald a few seconds, dry with a cloth, flour, season with pepper and salt, and fry five or six minutes in smoking hot fat. With steak or mutton chops they are excellent stewed with butter, lemon juice, powdered herbs, and salt and pepper.

Another important edible mushroom is the morel (*Morchella esculenta*), found in April and May. It is best stewed. Remove the foot of the stalk, wash, and dry with a cloth. Cut in two or more pieces according to size. Put in boiling water, a few at a time, boil five minutes, and drain. Then put on the fire with a little stock, butter, salt, pepper, and if desired, onion; cook five minutes and serve on buttered toast, garnished with slices of lemon. Morels are often filled with a light bread forcemeat (chopped spiced meat), wrapped in thin slices of salt pork, and baked half an hour, or stewed in stock one hour.

## Other Edible Varieties of Mushrooms

- **The Horse Mushroom** (*Agaricus arvensis*): firm, delicious, and yielding abundant gravy. Its conical top has a red lining.  
- **The Hedgehog Mushroom** (*Hydnum repandum*): with a yellow, concave top.  
- **The Chanterelle** (*Cantharellus cibarius*): yellow, funnel-shaped, and of a fruity smell.  
- **The Edible Pore Mushroom** (*Boletus edulis*): having a red top and, instead of the lamellae, vertical tubes that separate easily from the cap and from each other. Boleti with a red tinge on the under surface should not be eaten.

Those who like the flavor of mushrooms may dry them by putting on the stove in a saucepan. When the steaming stops, place on a shelf in a warm place until dry as bone, pulverize in a mortar, and keep in sealed jars in a dry place.

## Deviled Mushrooms

Chop 1 pint of mushrooms, season with salt and pepper, and a little lemon juice. Mix the yolk of 1 hard-boiled egg with a raw one, stir in a cup of bread crumbs and 1/2 tablespoon of butter. Fill baking shells with the mixture, cover with bread crumbs and bits of butter, and bake until browned.

---

## Broiled Mushrooms

Select large mushrooms, peel and cut off the lower part of the stalks, wash, drain on a cloth, and season with salt and pepper. Broil on a double gridiron over a brisk fire and baste with oil. Dish on thin toast, pour melted butter over, and serve.
