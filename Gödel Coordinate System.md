## **Gödel Coordinate System**

The Gödel coordinate system is the spacetime chart used to express **Kurt Gödel’s rotating universe metric**, a homogeneous, stationary cosmological model that admits **closed timelike curves (CTCs)**. It makes the symmetry and rotation of Gödel’s universe explicit.

- They provide one of the earliest exact solutions showing that **general relativity permits time travel** under certain conditions.  
- They illustrate how **rotation affects spacetime geometry**.  
- They serve as a testbed for studying **causality, global structure, and exotic GR solutions**.

---

## *Gödel Universe*

Gödel’s 1949 solution to Einstein’s field equations describes a universe filled with pressureless dust and a cosmological constant. The model is:

- **Homogeneous** — every spatial point is equivalent.  
- **Stationary** — the geometry does not evolve in time.  
- **Rotating** — spacetime itself exhibits global rotation.

This rotation produces unusual causal behavior, including the existence of **closed timelike curves**.

---

## **Coordinate System Structure**

Gödel introduced coordinates \((t, x, y, z)\) chosen to expose the symmetries of the spacetime:

- **t** — proper time of observers comoving with the cosmic fluid.  
- **x** and **y** — spatial coordinates encoding rotational structure.  
- **z** — spatial coordinate along the rotation axis.

The metric in these coordinates is commonly written:

```
ds^2 = a^2 [ -dt^2 - 2 e^x dt dy + (1/2) e^(2x) dy^2 + dx^2 + dz^2 ]
```

where **a** is a constant determined by the matter density and cosmological constant.

---

## **Coordinates Work**

Key features of the Gödel chart:

- The off‑diagonal term **−2 e^x dt dy** encodes spacetime rotation.  
- The factors **e^x** and **e^(2x)** show how spatial geometry varies with the \(x\)-coordinate.  
- The metric is **stationary** (no \(t\)-dependence).  
- The metric is **homogeneous** (curvature is the same at every point).

These properties make Gödel’s universe one of the clearest demonstrations of rotation in general relativity.

---

## **Physical Interpretation**

Gödel coordinates reveal several geometric and causal phenomena:

- **Global rotation** — inertial frames twist as one moves through space.  
- **Light‑cone tilting** — increasing \(x\) causes light cones to tip due to rotation.  
- **Closed timelike curves** — beyond a critical radius, the tilt becomes extreme enough that timelike paths loop back on themselves.

---

## **Alternative Coordinate Forms**

Gödel’s metric can also be expressed in cylindrical coordinates \((t, r, \phi, z)\):

```
ds^2 = -dt^2 - 2 H(r) dt dφ + D(r) dφ^2 + dr^2 + dz^2
```

Here, **H(r)** and **D(r)** encode rotation and curvature.  
This form makes the circular nature of Gödel’s CTCs more explicit.
