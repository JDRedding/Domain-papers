# LLMs Can’t Jump, Relational Version
- 509 — Philosophy & theory of science
- 006.3 — Artificial intelligence / machine learning.

The Scientific Invention Position Paper "LLMs can’t jump," argues that scientific invention is 
a distinct, foundational mode of scientific activity - not reducible to discovery or theory‑
building - and that philosophy of science must treat invention as a first‑class epistemic and 
methodological category. It proposes a framework for understanding invention as a creative, 
constructive, constraint‑driven process that generates new scientific tools, concepts, models, 
and practices.

---

## **0. Global Frame**

The paper’s thesis becomes:

> “LLMs are structurally incapable of the abductive ‘jump’ required for scientific invention.”

In RDG terms:

$\textbf{LLMs lack} J : E \rightarrow A$

Where:
- **E** = Sense‑Experience substrate  
- **J** = Abductive Jump operator  
- **A** = Axiomatic generator  

LLMs implement:
- **Induction** = $\(I : (E \rightarrow R)\) $ 
- **Deduction** = $\(D : (A \rightarrow S)\) $ 

But not:
- **Abduction** = $\(J : (E, R) \rightarrow A\)$

Thus the paper’s claim is that **scientific invention = J**, and **LLMs = I + D only**.

---

# **1. SID‑Mode**
Structure–Interaction–Dynamics
## **1.1 Structure (S‑operators)**

### **S1. Scientific discovery is a 4‑node cycle**
$E \xrightarrow{J} A \xrightarrow{D} S \xrightarrow{V} E'$

Where:
- **E** = embodied sensory substrate  
- **J** = abductive operator  
- **A** = axioms  
- **D** = deduction  
- **S** = theorems  
- **V** = verification  

Einstein’s diagram (quoted):

> “jumping from Sense Experience (E) to Axioms (A) via a Jump (J), and then deducing logical consequences.”

### **S2. LLMs instantiate only two operators**
$\{I, D\} \subset \text{LLM}$
$J \notin \text{LLM}$

### **S3. Abduction requires embodied simulation**
$J = J(E_{\text{simulated}}, R_{\text{surprising}})$

Einstein’s “happiest thought” is the canonical example.

---

## **1.2 Interaction (I‑operators)**

### **I1. Induction fails when data is scarce**
The paper states:

> “Einstein was not compressing a noisy dataset… there was no supervised error signal.”

Thus:
$I(E_{\text{scarce}}) = \varnothing$

### **I2. Deduction fails when axioms are missing**
$D(A_{\text{incorrect}}) \rightarrow S_{\text{incorrect}}$

Einstein’s 1913–1915 struggle is modeled as:
$D(A_{\text{Entwurf}}) \rightarrow \text{contradictions}$

### **I3. Abduction requires counterfactual physical manipulation**
$J(E_{\text{counterfactual}}) = A_{\text{new}}$

Einstein’s elevator thought experiment is:
$E_{\text{acceleration}} \equiv E_{\text{gravity}}$

Thus:
$A_{\text{Equivalence Principle}} = J(E_{\text{acceleration}}, E_{\text{gravity}})$

---

## **1.3 Dynamics (D‑operators)**

### **D1. Scientific invention is a dynamical expansion of the operator set**
$\Delta A = J(E)$

### **D2. LLM dynamics are closed under symbol manipulation**
$\text{LLM} : \Sigma \rightarrow \Sigma$
$J \notin \Sigma$

### **D3. Abduction requires a world‑model dynamic**
$E_{\text{sim}} = W(\text{agentic intervention})$

Where:
- **W** = physically consistent world model  
- **agentic intervention** = counterfactual manipulation  

The paper states:

> “it must possess the capacity for counterfactual intervention… to conceptually cut the cable.”

Thus:
$J \subset W$

---

# **2. PED‑Mode**
Power–Evaluation–Dynamics
## **2.1 Power (P‑operators)**

### **P1. Induction gives predictive compression power**
But:
$P(I) \approx 0 \text{ when } E \text{ is scarce}$

### **P2. Deduction gives verification power**
But:
$P(D) = 0 \text{ if } A \text{ is wrong}$

### **P3. Abduction gives generative conceptual power**
$P(J) = \text{creation of new operators}$

Einstein’s abductive leap created:
- curved spacetime  
- equivalence principle  
- geodesic motion  
- metric field equations  

---

## **2.2 Evaluation (E‑operators)**

### **E1. Induction evaluates via statistical fit**
Unavailable for GR.

### **E2. Deduction evaluates via logical consistency**
But consistency ≠ invention.

### **E3. Abduction evaluates via physical plausibility**
$E(J) = \text{coherence with embodied experience}$

Einstein’s quote:

> “The words or the language… do not seem to play any role in my mechanism of thought.”

Thus:
$J \not\subset \text{symbolic language}$

---

## **2.3 Dynamics (D‑operators)**

### **D1. Invention is a dynamic reconfiguration of the conceptual space**
$\mathcal{C}_{\text{new}} = \mathcal{C}_{\text{old}} \cup \Delta A$

### **D2. LLM dynamics cannot expand the conceptual space**
$\Delta A_{\text{LLM}} = 0$

### **D3. World‑model dynamics enable abductive expansion**
$\Delta A_{W} > 0$

---

# **3. Tri‑Mode Mapping**

## **3.1 Mode 1: Symbolic Mode**
LLMs operate entirely here:
$\text{LLM} = \text{Symbolic Transformer}$

Capabilities:
- Induction  
- Deduction  

Missing:
- Abduction  
- Embodied grounding  
- Counterfactual manipulation  

---

## **3.2 Mode 2: Embodied Mode**
Einstein’s thought experiments live here:
$E_{\text{simulated}} \in \text{Embodied Mode}$

LLMs lack:
- proprioceptive priors  
- physical invariants  
- sensory coherence  

Thus:
$J \notin \text{LLM}$

---

## **3.3 Mode 3: World‑Model Mode**
The paper argues future AI must integrate:
$W = \text{physically consistent, agentic world model}$

Only then:
$J \subset W$

Thus:
$\text{Scientific AI} = \text{LLM} + W$

---

# **4. Final Synthesis**

The paper’s thesis becomes:

$\text{LLMs cannot invent because invention = J, and J requires } W.$

Or more explicitly:

$\boxed{
\text{Scientific Invention} = J(E_{\text{embodied}}, R_{\text{surprising}}) \\
\text{LLMs} = \{I, D\} \\
J \notin \text{LLM} \\
J \subset W_{\text{agentic}} \\
}$

Thus the formal conclusion:

**LLMs cannot jump because they lack the operator that generates axioms from embodied counterfactual simulation.**
