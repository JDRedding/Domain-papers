🔍 The **core idea** of Connes’s rigidity conjecture is that certain highly rigid groups should 
produce **distinct** von Neumann algebras — meaning the algebra should “remember” the group. The 
recent breakthrough shows this is **false**.

- It overturns a long-standing belief in operator algebras.  
- It shows that **von Neumann algebras can collapse group structure much more than expected**.  
- It reshapes the landscape around **Connes’s embedding problem** and **soficity**.

---

### **Concise takeaway**
Connes predicted that **property‑(T) groups** (strongly rigid groups) would have von Neumann 
algebras that uniquely determine the group up to finite ambiguity.  

The new result constructs **infinitely many non‑isomorphic property‑(T) groups** that all 
share **exactly the same** II₁ factor, disproving the conjecture.

---

### 🧩 **What the conjecture claimed**
- Property‑(T) groups have strong rigidity: small perturbations collapse back to the original 
structure.  

- Connes conjectured that this rigidity should extend to their **von Neumann algebras**, meaning:
  - If two property‑(T) groups have isomorphic II₁ factors,  
  - then the groups themselves should be “almost the same” (finite-to-one classification).

This would make the II₁ factor a powerful invariant.

---

### 💥 **What the new result shows**

The construction produces:

- A single II₁ factor \( M \)
- And **infinitely many** groups \( G_1, G_2, G_3, \dots \)
- Each \( G_i \) is property‑(T), non‑amenable, and **pairwise non‑isomorphic**

- Yet all satisfy  
  \[
  L(G_i) \cong M
  \]

This means the II₁ factor **cannot** distinguish between these groups — the rigidity fails.
