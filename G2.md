# G₂
**G₂** is the smallest of the five exceptional simple Lie groups (G₂, F₄, E₆, E₇, E₈). “Exceptional” means that their Dynkin diagrams do not belong to any of the four infinite classical families Aₙ (special linear), Bₙ (odd orthogonal), Cₙ (symplectic), or Dₙ (even orthogonal). There are exactly five such diagrams, and G₂ is the lowest-dimensional and lowest-rank among them. It has rank 2 and dimension 14; its Dynkin diagram consists of two nodes joined by a triple bond. This encodes a root system of 12 roots lying in the Euclidean plane, arranged in a highly symmetric star-like configuration with two root lengths and angles of 30° and 150°.

### Historical development
The 14-dimensional simple Lie algebra was discovered by Wilhelm Killing in a letter to Engel dated 23 May 1887, while he was attempting to prove that every simple Lie algebra is classical. Engel and Cartan subsequently related the algebra to the symmetries of certain 2-dimensional distributions and to the stabilizer of a generic 3-form in seven variables. In 1908 Cartan noted that the automorphism group of the octonions is 14-dimensional; in 1914 he identified this group with the compact real form of G₂. In older literature the algebra is occasionally denoted E₂. The finite groups $G_2(q)$ appeared in Dickson’s work on linear groups; the twisted Ree groups were introduced by Ree in 1960–61.

### Finite groups of Lie type
The algebraic group G₂ defined over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$ yields the finite Chevalley groups $G_2(q)$. Their order is

$$
|G_2(q)|=q^6(q^6-1)(q^2-1).
$$

They are simple for all $q\neq 2$; when $q=2$ the group has a simple subgroup of index 2 isomorphic to $^2A_2(9)$. These groups were first constructed by Dickson in 1901 (odd characteristic) and 1905 (even characteristic). In addition, there exist twisted Ree groups $^2G_2(q)$ of order

$$
q^3(q^3+1)(q-1),\qquad q=3^{2n+1}.
$$

The smallest of these, $^2G_2(3)'$, is the simple group of order 504. The Ree groups arise from a Steinberg endomorphism that exists only in characteristic 3.

### Root system, Cartan matrix, and Weyl group
A convenient realization of the roots uses the vectors in a 2-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\pm(e_1-e_2),\quad
\pm(e_1-e_3),\quad
\pm(e_2-e_3),\\
\pm(2e_1-e_2-e_3),\quad
\pm(-e_1+2e_2-e_3),\quad
\pm(-e_1-e_2+2e_3).
\end{align*}
$$

One standard choice of simple roots is $\alpha=e_1-e_2$ and $\beta=-e_1+2e_2-e_3$. The Cartan matrix is

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & -1 \\ 
-3 & 2
\end{pmatrix}
$$

(or its transpose, depending on which root is taken to be long). The Weyl/Coxeter group is the dihedral group of order 12, generated by the reflections in the hyperplanes orthogonal to the simple roots. This group acts transitively on the long roots and on the short roots separately.

### Basic structural facts
- Rank: 2  
- Dimension: 14  
- Fundamental representations: dimensions 7 and 14  
- Weyl group: dihedral of order 12  

The 7-dimensional representation is irreducible and corresponds to the natural action on the imaginary octonions; the 14-dimensional representation is the adjoint representation. The dimensions of the smallest irreducible representations begin

$$
1,\ 7,\ 14,\ 27,\ 64,\ 77,\ 182,\ 189,\ 273,\ 286,\ \dots
$$

(with two non-isomorphic representations of dimension 77). All finite-dimensional characters are given by the Weyl character formula.

### Geometry: G₂-structures and special holonomy
G₂ is one of the groups that appear in Berger’s classification of possible holonomy groups of Riemannian manifolds. A Riemannian 7-manifold whose holonomy is contained in G₂ (or equal to G₂) is called a G₂-manifold. Equivalently, it carries a positive 3-form $\varphi$ that is parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection. The dual 4-form $*\varphi$ is likewise parallel. When the holonomy is exactly G₂ the manifold is Ricci-flat and has a parallel spinor, hence is supersymmetric in the sense of M-theory.

More generally one studies G₂-structures that are not necessarily torsion-free. The intrinsic torsion of a G₂-structure takes values in a 49-dimensional representation that decomposes into four irreducible pieces under G₂; the vanishing of certain torsion classes corresponds to closed or coclosed 3-forms, nearly parallel structures, etc. Compact G₂-manifolds with holonomy exactly G₂ were first constructed by Joyce; many further examples have been produced by gluing, resolution, and deformation techniques. These manifolds are of central interest both in differential geometry and in theoretical physics, where they serve as compactification spaces for M-theory that preserve a fraction of supersymmetry and yield four-dimensional effective theories with $N=1$ supersymmetry.

### Real and complex forms
The name “G₂” refers to three simple Lie groups associated with the same abstract root system:
- The complex form. Viewed as a real Lie algebra it has dimension 28; its maximal compact subgroup is the compact real form.
- The compact real form (dimension 14). It is simply connected, has trivial center, and admits no outer automorphisms. It may be identified with the group of algebra automorphisms of the octonion algebra $\mathbb{O}$:

$$
G_2^{\mathrm{compact}}\simeq{Aut}(\mathbb{O}).
$$
  
  Equivalently, it is the subgroup of ${SO}(7)$ that stabilizes a fixed nonzero spinor in the 8-dimensional real spin representation.
- The split (non-compact) real form (dimension 14). Its maximal compact subgroup is ${SU}(2)\times{SU}(2)/\{\pm1\}$, its fundamental group has order 2, and it admits a simply-connected double cover that is not algebraic.

These three real forms have distinct geometric and representation-theoretic properties; the compact form is the one that appears most frequently in Riemannian geometry.

### Characterizations and constructions
Several independent constructions realize G₂:
- As the automorphism group of the octonions (compact form), or of the split octonions (split form).
- As the stabilizer, inside ${GL}(7)$, of a generic alternating 3-form on a 7-dimensional vector space. Engel showed that the isotropy group of such a 3-form is isomorphic to the complex form of G₂.
- Via the derivation algebra of a suitable non-associative algebra (a general method that produces all the exceptional Lie algebras).
- Through Clifford-algebra constructions that produce explicit matrix generators and relate G₂ to calibrations.
- As a subgroup of ${Spin}(7)$ or ${SO}(7)$ preserving additional structure; the homogeneous space ${Spin}(7)/G_2\simeq S^7$ recovers the 7-sphere.

These characterizations make the appearance of G₂ in both algebra and geometry inevitable rather than accidental.

Because G₂ is the automorphism group of the octonions, it sits at the beginning of the exceptional series in a very concrete way: the remaining exceptional groups F₄, E₆, E₇, E₈ can be built from octonion-related structures (Jordan algebras, Freudenthal triple systems, etc.). In representation theory the 7-dimensional representation of G₂ embeds into the 8-dimensional spin representation of ${Spin}(7)$, and the adjoint appears inside $\mathfrak{so}(7)$. In physics, G₂-holonomy manifolds and G₂-instantons appear in heterotic string theory and in M-theory, while the finite groups $G_2(q)$ and the Ree groups occur in the classification of finite simple groups and in various combinatorial and geometric constructions.

In summary, G₂ is the smallest exceptional simple Lie group. Its triple-bond Dynkin diagram, its 12-root system, its identification with ${Aut}(\mathbb{O})$, its role as a special holonomy group in dimension 7, and the associated finite groups of Lie type together give it a uniquely rich position at the intersection of Lie theory, non-associative algebra, differential geometry, and theoretical physics.
