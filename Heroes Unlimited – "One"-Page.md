# Heroes Unlimited 
**"One"-Page Core Rules**  

Heroes Unlimited builds characters from fixed archetypes (mutants, aliens, cyborgs, magic heroes, gadgeteers, etc.), each with its own advancement track and power access. This creates fast flavorful characters but also uneven balance and a “TV dinner” feel — pre‑packaged options rather than custom‑built systems.

Choose Heroes Unlimited if want:
- Quick, archetype‑driven character creation.
- Classic comic‑book feel.
- High‑powered heroes without deep mechanical tuning.

Heroes Unlimited uses Palladium’s Megaversal rules, which are class‑based and swingy, with some hero types vastly outperforming others. HU supports high‑powered characters but often becomes unbalanced and mechanically clunky.

### Attributes
Character Creation
- Roll attributes (3d6 with bonuses).
- Choose a hero category (mutant, experiment, alien, cyborg, robot, magic, psionic, etc.).
- Each category grants specific powers, skills, and level progression 
Fast to generate concepts but inconsistent in mechanical depth.

### Notes & Higher Values
- **Below 16**: No bonuses (average human range is roughly 9–12).
- **Above 30** (common with powers or certain categories):
  - **IQ, ME, MA, PB**: Bonuses normally stop at the 30 value.
  - **PS**: Continues +1 damage per point (e.g., 31 = +16, 40 = +25). Extraordinary / Superhuman / Supernatural Strength use their own damage scales on top of (or instead of) the normal bonus.
  - **PP**: Strike/parry/dodge usually stop at +8 (at 30). Some books give +1 initiative every 4 points beyond 30 (34, 38, etc.).
  - **PE**: Coma/Death and Magic/Poison saves continue scaling in many groups (roughly +2% and +1 every 2 points), but confirm with your GM.
- All bonuses are cumulative with Hand-to-Hand, physical skills, Weapon Proficiencies, and powers.
- **PS** also determines carrying/lifting capacity (roughly PS × 10 lbs normal carry; higher multipliers for super strength types).
- **Spd** movement: Spd × 20 yards/meters per melee round is a common rule of thumb (exact speed chart available in the book).

### Hit Points & S.D.C.
- **Hit Points** = PE + 1d6 (+1d6 per level of experience).  
- **S.D.C.** = base by power category (common ranges: 20 for many “normal” or Empowered types; 30–40+ for Supersoldiers, certain Aliens, Physical Training, or bionic/robot bodies) + physical skill bonuses + power bonuses.  

Damage is subtracted from **S.D.C. first** (superficial; heals faster), then **Hit Points** (serious). At 0 HP the character is dying (coma/death save applies).  

Natural or worn Armor Rating (A.R.): attacker must roll higher than the A.R. to damage the body underneath.

### Quick Notes
- **Major vs Minor Super Abilities**: Majors are broader and usually stronger (multiple effects). Minors are narrower but still potent. Full lists and descriptions are in the core book + *Powers Unlimited* sourcebooks.
- **S.D.C.** numbers above are bases only. Physical skills, powers, and category bonuses add more.
- **Starting with simpler categories** (Physical Training, Special Training, limited Hardware) is recommended for new groups, as the one-pager suggests.
- Many categories allow **player choice** instead of pure random rolls for powers — confirm with your GM.

### Skills & Education
Education level (determined by category or roll) provides skill slots.  
- Skills are **percentile**: roll d100 ≤ skill percentage to succeed.  
- IQ bonus and experience improve skills.  
- **Hand-to-Hand** skills cost skill slots and are critical:  
  - **Basic** (1 slot): +2 attacks at level 1, +2 roll with impact, +2 pull punch.  
  - **Expert** (2 slots): +2 attacks at level 1, better parry/dodge progression.  
  - **Martial Arts** or **Assassin** (3 slots): +2 attacks at level 1 + initiative and stronger combat maneuvers.  
- Characters with **no** Hand-to-Hand skill get only the base 2 attacks and must spend an action to parry or dodge.  
- Physical skills (Boxing, Athletics, Body Building, Running, etc.) add attacks, attribute bonuses, and S.D.C.

**Skill Framework**  
*(Skills are percentile: roll d100 ≤ skill percentage to succeed. IQ bonuses and experience improve them. Maximum normal skill is 98%.)*

### Education & Skill Selection
- Roll or choose an **Education Level** (High School, Trade School, College years, Military, etc.). This determines how many **Skill Programs** (scholastic packages) you get and the bonus % applied to those skills.
- Skill Programs group related skills (e.g., Espionage, Medical, Pilot, Rogue, Science, Technical, Weapon Proficiency).
- You also get a number of **Secondary Skills** (no education bonus — only IQ bonus).
- **Hand-to-Hand** and most **Physical skills** cost skill slots and are often restricted from Secondary Skills.
- Some Power Categories (Physical Training, Special Training packages, Hardware, etc.) grant fixed skills or override normal education.

**Typical pattern**: Higher education = more Skill Programs + bigger % bonuses to scholastic skills. Secondary skills are always weaker.

### Skill Check Basics
- Base % comes from the skill entry (e.g., First Aid 45%+5%/level).
- Add **IQ bonus** (from Attribute Chart) to **all** skills (scholastic and secondary).
- Add any education / program / category bonuses.
- Add +5% (or the listed amount) per level of experience.
- Critical success usually on 01–05 or similar (GM call); failure on 96–00 or higher than skill %.

### Combat (Melee Round ≈ 15 seconds)
**Attacks per melee**: Base 2 + Hand-to-Hand bonuses + Boxing/certain powers.  

1. **Initiative**: d20 + bonuses. Highest acts first. Successful sneak attacks or long-range ambushes may seize the initiative for the round.  
2. **Attack**: d20 + strike bonuses.  
   - Modified result of 4 or less usually misses.  
   - Higher result hits. Must exceed target’s A.R. to damage the body.  
3. **Defense**: Parry or Dodge (d20 + bonuses). Equal or better than the attacker’s roll succeeds (**defender wins ties**).  
   - Trained characters can often parry without spending an action; untrained characters must spend one.  
   - Dodge always costs an action.  
4. **Damage**: Roll dice for the attack/weapon/power + PS bonus. Subtract from S.D.C., then Hit Points.  
   - **Critical Strike** (usually natural 19–20, or improved by higher Hand-to-Hand): double damage.  
   - **Roll with Impact** (d20 + bonuses): success halves damage but costs an action.  

**Other useful combat notes**  
- Pull Punch reduces damage on purpose.  
- Knockout/Stun and Death Blow appear at higher Hand-to-Hand levels.  
- Super abilities, powers, and many ranged attacks each cost one or more melee actions.  
- Ranged combat uses the same d20 strike/parry/dodge system (with range modifiers).

### Key Combat Notes
- **No Hand-to-Hand**: Limited attacks (typically 1 at level 1, plus a few more at higher levels). Parry and Dodge both cost an action. No automatic parry.
- **Trained characters** (any of the four styles above) can usually parry without spending an action; Dodge always costs an action.
- **Critical Strike**: Double damage (or triple at high Expert levels) on the listed natural rolls.
- **Death Blow**: Instant kill or massive damage on the listed roll (must be declared; not always available).
- **Body Flip/Throw**: 1D6 damage + opponent loses initiative and one attack.
- Boxing, Wrestling, and certain powers stack on top of these bonuses.

This matches the HU2 tables used in actual play.

**Core Combat Maneuvers & Modifiers**  
*(Melee round ≈ 15 seconds. Base attacks = 2 + Hand-to-Hand bonuses + Boxing/powers. Highest initiative acts first; then players alternate actions.)*

### Combat Sequence
1. **Initiative** — Everyone rolls d20 + initiative bonuses. Highest goes first. Ties are re-rolled or resolved by the GM. Successful sneak attacks or long-range ambushes can seize initiative for the round.
2. **Attack** — Attacker rolls d20 + strike bonuses.
   - Modified result of **4 or less** = automatic miss.
   - **5+** = potential hit.
   - A **natural 20** is always a hit and a Critical Strike (double damage) unless the defender also rolls a natural 20.
3. **Defense** — Defender may Parry or Dodge (d20 + bonuses).  
   - Equal or higher than the attacker’s roll succeeds (**defender wins ties**).  
   - **Parry** blocks the attack. Trained characters can often parry without spending an action; untrained characters must spend one.  
   - **Dodge** moves out of the way and always costs one action.
4. **Damage** — If the hit lands, roll damage dice + PS bonus (and any other damage bonuses). Subtract from S.D.C. first, then Hit Points.

### Key Maneuvers

| Maneuver | How It Works | Action Cost | Notes |
|----------|--------------|-------------|-------|
| **Critical Strike** | Natural 19–20 (or better range from high Hand-to-Hand) | None | Double damage. Some high-level styles can do triple. |
| **Roll with Impact** | d20 + bonuses; match or beat the attacker’s strike roll | 1 action | Success = half damage. Works against punches, kicks, falls, blunt weapons, explosions, etc. Does **not** work against bullets, energy blasts, or edged weapons. |
| **Pull Punch** | Declare before striking. Roll d20 + Pull Punch bonuses; need 11+ | None (if successful) | Reduces damage to half, quarter, 1 point, or 0 (player’s choice). Failure = full damage. |
| **Knockout / Stun** | Available at higher Hand-to-Hand levels on high natural rolls | None | Renders target unconscious or stunned for a short time. Can often be resisted or reduced by a successful Roll with Impact. |
| **Death Blow** | Available at high Hand-to-Hand levels; must be declared | None | Instant kill or massive damage on the listed natural roll. Extremely rare and usually only available to Assassin or very high-level characters. |
| **Body Flip / Throw** | Learned via Hand-to-Hand | 1 action | Typically 1D6 damage + opponent loses initiative and one attack. |
| **Power Punch** | Optional heavy attack | 2 actions | Double damage dice (PS bonus still applies once). |

### Armor Rating (A.R.)
- **Worn Armor** (vests, body armor, etc.):  
  - Strike roll **≤ A.R.** → damage goes to the armor’s S.D.C. only.  
  - Strike roll **> A.R.** → damage bypasses armor and hits the character’s S.D.C./HP.  
  - When the armor’s S.D.C. reaches 0, it is destroyed and offers no further protection.
- **Natural A.R.** (super toughness, thick hide, certain powers):  
  - Strike roll **≤ A.R.** → no damage at all.  
  - Strike roll **> A.R.** → full damage to the character’s S.D.C./HP.  
  - Natural A.R. does not degrade.

### Ranged Combat Notes
- Same d20 strike / parry / dodge system as melee.
- Common range modifiers (varies slightly by book/weapon):  
  - Point-blank / close (within 60 ft): normal strike (5+ hits).  
  - Longer ranges: higher minimum to hit (often 8+ or with cumulative penalties).
- Most energy blasts, bullets, and lasers cannot be parried with bare hands (exceptions exist for certain powers, force fields, or high-tech shields).
- Called shots (specific body part, weapon, etc.) usually require a higher roll (often 12+).

### Quick Reminders
- Untrained characters have no automatic parry and limited attacks.
- Super abilities, energy blasts, and many powers each cost one or more actions.
- All bonuses (PP, Hand-to-Hand, skills, powers) stack.
- Damage always goes **S.D.C. first**, then **Hit Points**. At 0 HP the character is dying (coma/death save applies).

| **Super Abilities** | Many powers increase in range, duration, damage, or effects (“per level of experience”) |
| **Magic / Psionics** | Spell strength, ISP, or number of powers may improve (varies by category) |
| **S.D.C.** | Does **not** automatically increase (only via skills, powers, or special category bonuses) |

### Typical Experience Thresholds (approximate — exact tables vary by category)
Most superhero categories use relatively steep tables. A common pattern looks like:

| Level | Approx. XP Needed | Notes |
|-------|-------------------|-------|
| 1 | 0 | Starting level |
| 2 | ~2,000–2,500 | First real jump |
| 3 | ~4,000–5,000 | — |
| 4 | ~8,000–10,000 | — |
| 5 | ~15,000–20,000 | Mid-tier |
| 6+ | Continues rising | Full tables are in the core book by category |

*(Physical Training, Special Training, and Hardware often use slightly different tables than pure super-powered categories.)*

### Awarding Experience Points (GM Guide)
Common awards (from the book’s Experience Point Table):

| Action | XP Award |
|--------|----------|
| Clever or useful idea | 25–100 |
| Good role-playing | 25–100 |
| Defeating a minor threat | 100–300 |
| Defeating a significant opponent | 200–500+ |
| Completing a mission / major goal | 400–1,000+ |
| Saving lives / heroic act | 100–300 |
| Learning something new or solving a puzzle | 50–150 |

The GM awards XP at the end of a session or adventure based on actions, creativity, and success (not just combat).

### Quick Notes
- Skills and Hand-to-Hand improve **immediately** upon leveling.
- Many super powers scale automatically (“+1d6 damage per level,” “+10 ft range per level,” etc.).
- Some categories (Hardware, certain Special Training packages) improve more through skills and gadgets than raw power scaling.
- Experience is individual — not shared equally unless the GM decides otherwise.
- High-level characters (8+) are rare and extremely capable; most campaigns stay in the 1–6 range for a long time.
