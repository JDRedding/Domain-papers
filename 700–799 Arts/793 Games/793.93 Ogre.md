# *Ogre*
— components, units, movement, combat, scenarios, and strategy. 

## Core takeaway
**Ogre is an asymmetric wargame built around a single unstoppable cybernetic tank versus a combined‑arms defender force, with gameplay defined by subsystem-targeted combat, terrain‑modulated movement, and scenario‑driven force balance.** 

## 🛡️ Units (Ogre vs defenders)
### Ogres
Subsystem-based war machines with discrete treads, main batteries, secondaries, AP guns, and missiles.

- **Mark I** — ~4 armor units, 1 main battery, move 3.  
- **Mark III** — 17 armor units, main battery 6–8, secondaries, missiles, move 3.  
- **Mark V** — 25 armor units, heavier weapons, 3 missile racks.  
- **Mark VI** — ~40 armor units, three main batteries, ignores rough terrain penalties.  
  *“Superheavy Ogres scale up to 40 armor units three main batteries treads that ignore rough terrain penalties.”* 

Damage is subsystem‑targeted: weapons destroyed individually; treads reduce MP as they are lost.

### Defenders
A combined‑arms roster with explicit attack/defense/movement values:

- **Tanks** — light (0.5 AU), heavy (2–4 attack), missile tanks (range 3), superheavy (6 attack split, defense 4).  
- **Howitzers** — immobile or mobile, attack 6 at range 4.  
- **GEVs** — fast hovercraft with primary + secondary movement phases.  
- **Infantry** — squads (3 = 1 AU), close assault specialists.  
- **Command Post** — defense 1–4, objective of the game.

*“lighter, faster elements like GEVs…howitzers provide remote suppression…infantry masses for close assaults.”*

## 🗺️ Setup & Scenarios
Standard Mark III scenario:

- Defender: **20 infantry squads + 12 armor units**, with placement restrictions.  
  *“20 infantry squads and 12 armor units…maximum of 20 attack strength points in the central zone.”* 
- Ogre enters from the south.
- Victory: Ogre destroys CP or exits north; defender wins by destroying Ogre while preserving CP.

Variants include defender’s choice, multiplayer, solitaire, and advanced urban/reinforcement scenarios.

Point balancing: Mark V = 100 points; heavy tank = 8 points.

---

## 🚚 Movement (terrain‑dependent, subsystem‑dependent)
Hexes = 1,500 meters. Movement phases include recovery, primary movement, disable checks, fire, and GEV secondary movement.

Key terrain rules:

- **Roads** — 1 MP, +1 MP bonus if staying on road.  
- **Woods/Rough** — 1 MP for Ogres; 2 MP for GEVs with disable risk.  
- **Rivers** — GEVs cross for 1 MP; infantry/Ogres for 2 MP; others need bridges.

Ogre tread mechanics:  
*“starting with 3 MP…river crossings cost 2 MP…MP totals decrease with tread damage.”* 

Overruns occur when entering enemy hex; Ogre may ram twice per turn.

Subsystem targeting for Ogres: weapons destroyed individually; tread hits reduce MP.

Close assault doubles attacker strength; infantry bayonet rules apply.

## 🧠 Strategy & Tactics

### Defender priorities
- Use howitzers to soften the Ogre early.  
- GEVs for flanking and tread‑sniping.  
- Infantry to block paths and force inefficient movement.  
- Terrain to slow the Ogre.

### Ogre priorities
- Central entry to split defenders.  
- Sideslip/jink to avoid concentrated fire.  
- Missile strikes on high‑value targets (howitzers).  
- Preserve treads until final charge.

Balance evolution: early editions favored defenders; later editions adjusted tank/GEV speeds and point costs.
