# **E₇**
**Exceptional Propagation Through a Quartic Hinge**



$E_7$ is an exceptional simple Lie algebra of rank 7 and dimension 133. Its root system contains 126 roots, and its most important representation is the 56-dimensional fundamental representation. From a relational-propagation viewpoint, $E_7$ is distinctive because its closure is organized around a quartic invariant (and the associated Freudenthal triple system) defined on that 56-dimensional space. Rather than viewing $E_7$ merely as a symmetry object, we can interpret it as the endpoint of a propagation chain whose hinge is the preservation of a higher-order relational form. For relational dynamics and propagation purposes, the most useful entry point is not the dimension count or the abstract Lie group, but the *hinge* itself: the structure that organizes admissible transformations and closes the system.



## Structural Seed

The generative sequence begins with the extended (affine) Dynkin diagram $\widetilde{E}_7$:



$$
\widetilde{E}_7
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
\text{Simple Roots}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
\text{Cartan Matrix}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
\text{Root System } \Phi(E_7)
$$



**Structural Properties**

- Rank = 7
- Root count = 126
- $\dim \mathfrak{e}_7 = 133$



This is the structural side of the propagation. The Dynkin diagram encodes the simple-root inner products; the Cartan matrix is the numerical realization of those relations; and the root system is their global geometric completion.



## Relational Completion

The root relations propagate into the full $E_7$ root system:



$$
\text{Simple Roots}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
126 \text{ roots}
$$



A convenient realization places the roots in the hyperplane $\sum x_i = 0$ inside $\mathbb{R}^8$: all permutations of $(1,-1,0,\dots,0)$ together with vectors containing four $+\frac12$ and four $-\frac12$. Equivalently, they are the roots of $E_8$ orthogonal to a fixed root. The associated Weyl group has order

$$
2{,}903{,}040 =
2^{10}\cdot 3^4\cdot 5\cdot 7.
$$



The root system supplies the geometric realization of the structural seed and determines the adjoint representation:

$$
133 = 126 + 7.
$$



## The $E_7$ Hinge

Unlike the affine-propagation viewpoint often associated with $E_8$-based constructions, $E_7$'s distinctive hinge is an invariant-preserving structure:



$$
\text{Freudenthal Triple System}
\quad + \quad
\text{Quartic Invariant } Q.
$$



This invariant lives on the 56-dimensional fundamental representation (minuscule, self-dual, pseudo-real). Symbolically:



$$
\text{56-dimensional representation}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
\text{Quartic form } Q(x)
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
\text{Preserved by } E_7.
$$



The central propagation question becomes:

> *Which transformations preserve $Q(x)$?*

That preservation requirement is the hinge.

The 56 can be realized as

$$
\bigwedge^2 V \oplus \bigwedge^2 V^*
$$

with $V = \mathbb{R}^8$ or $\mathbb{C}^8$. Under $\mathrm{SL}(8)$ it decomposes as

$$
28 \oplus 28^*.
$$

The quartic invariant, together with the natural symplectic form on the 56-dimensional representation, characterizes the $E_7$-preserving structure.



## Dynamic Closure

The quartic organizes the admissible transformations:



$$
\text{56-D representation}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
\text{Quartic preservation}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
E_7 \text{ action}.
$$



The dynamics are therefore constrained by invariant-preserving propagation. The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{e}_7$ is the infinitesimal shadow of this action:



$$
\mathfrak{e}_7
\qquad
(
\dim = 133 =
126 \text{ root generators} +
7 \text{ Cartan generators}
).
$$



The full exceptional Lie group $E_7$ is the global symmetry closure of this construction: the maximal continuous transformation structure preserving the defining quartic geometry.

## Further Mathematical and Geometric Notes

The compact real form has dimension 133. The split real form $E_{7(7)}$ appears as the continuous U-duality group of 4-dimensional $\mathcal{N}=8$ supergravity.

Algebraically, the same quartic structure organizes the geometry of the degree-2 del Pezzo surface (the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at seven points), whose 56 lines correspond naturally to the fundamental representation.

The Dynkin diagram itself consists of a chain of six nodes with a single branch attached to the third node. The Cartan matrix has determinant 2, consistent with the adjoint form having

$$
\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.
$$
