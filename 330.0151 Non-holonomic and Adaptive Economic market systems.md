## 1. Counterparty Conservation (Clearing Condition)
   
Every matched trade conserves counterparties: buy volume equals sell volume. Net signed order-flow imbalance $I(t)$ drives price changes, while the matched component cancels.

$$
\Delta V_{\text{buy}}(t) = \Delta V_{\text{sell}}(t) \quad \text{(matched trades)}
$$

Unsigned volume $V(t) = V_{\text{buy}}(t) + V_{\text{sell}}(t)$.  
Signed order-flow (imbalance):

$$
I(t) = V_{\text{buy}}(t) - V_{\text{sell}}(t)
$$

This is a conservation / clearing identity, not a dynamical law. Stochastic arrivals produce residual noise that generates microstructure.

## 2. Stochastic Order-Flow Models (Hawkes Processes)
   
Self- and cross-exciting point processes capture order clustering and persistence (core vs. reaction flow).

Intensity of event type $i$:

$$
\lambda_i(t) = \mu_i + \sum_j \int_0^t \phi_{ij}(t-s)\,dN_j(s)
$$

$\mu_i > 0$: baseline intensity  
$\phi_{ij}(\cdot)$: excitation kernel (often exponential $\alpha e^{-\beta u}$ or power-law)  
$N_j$: counting process of type-$j$ events  

Near-critical (nearly unstable) scaling of Hawkes processes yields fractional signed order flow, rough volume, and rough volatility with related Hurst indices (e.g., core-flow Hurst $H_0 \approx 3/4$ implies volume Hurst $H_0 - 1/2$ and volatility Hurst $2H_0 - 3/2$).

## 3. Limit-Order-Book Queue Dynamics (Cont–de Larrard style)
   
Best bid and ask queues $(q^b_t, q^a_t)$ evolve as interacting continuous-time Markov chains (or more general point processes). Price jumps when a queue hits zero.

In the heavy-traffic / diffusion limit the rescaled queues converge to a Markovian jump-diffusion in the positive orthant. Conditional probability of an up-move given current queues admits closed-form expressions via Laplace transforms or first-passage times.

A simple Poisson baseline (zero-intelligence):

Limit-order arrival rate at distance $i$ ticks: $\lambda(i)$ 
Market-order rate: $\mu$  
Cancellation rate proportional to queue size: $\theta(i) \cdot q$

## 4. Price Dynamics Emerging from Microstructure
   
Price changes occur only on queue depletion (or via impact of market orders). At coarser scales the mid-price $P_t$ behaves approximately as a random walk / Brownian motion:

$$
dP_t = \sigma\,dW_t + \text{jumps from depletions}
$$

or, with microstructure noise,

$$
Y_t = P_t + \varepsilon_t
$$

where $\varepsilon_t$ is the noise (bid-ask bounce, discreteness, etc.).

Square-root market-impact law (empirically robust and compatible with the above):

$$
I(Q) \propto \sqrt{|Q|}
$$

(where $Q$ is meta-order size). This reconciles persistent order flow with long-run diffusive prices.

## 5. Continuous Order-Book Density (SPDE Approximation)
   
Centered order-book density \(u(t,x)\):

$$
\partial_t u(t,x) = \mathcal{L}u + \sigma(x)u\,dW_t + \text{source terms}
$$

(with $\mathcal{L}$ a differential operator for diffusion / drift of volume, multiplicative noise for high-frequency cancellations). Finite-dimensional Markov realizations exist under suitable assumptions.

## 6. Deterministic Pursuit Curves (Explicitly Contrasted)
Classical geometric pursuit (e.g., tractrix / radiodrome) assumes fixed coupling / leash length:

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{y}{\sqrt{a^2 - y^2}}
$$

(or the equivalent ODE for constant-speed pursuit of a moving target).  

Economic market systems are non-holonomic and adaptive: coupling (liquidity, leverage, policy) varies continuously, destroying clean analytic pursuit trajectories and producing stochastic or chaotic feedback instead. Bubble morphology is only a phenomenological resemblance arising from one class of stochastic feedback regimes; historical crashes (1987, 2008, 2015, 2020, …) exhibit distinct geometries generated by different regimes.
